# TREK RESEARCH METHOD

## Purpose
Common epistemic and analytical method for every Trek Research worker.

## Minimum research objects
Use the stable core:
**Source → Work → Local Entity → Evidence → Assertion**

Do not create a new fundamental schema for each narrative oddity. Prefer typed assertions and governed predicates until repeated corpus evidence proves a new structure is necessary.

## Source-relative evidence
Evidence records what the artifact supports, not an omniscient world-state.

Useful evidence kinds:
- depiction/action
- dialogue/testimony
- computer or sensor report
- log/record
- memory/recollection
- dream/vision
- simulation/holodeck
- narration/story-within-story
- altered reality
- alternate timeline/universe
- unknown/contested frame

Examples:
- “Garak says X” supports the utterance, not automatically X.
- “A tricorder reports X” supports the report, not automatically X.
- “A character remembers X” supports the recollection, not automatically the remembered event.

Do not invent probability scores unless an explicit quantitative method supports them.

## Evidence vs interpretation
Keep these separate.

Evidence:
A character verbally rejects a proposed procedure.

Interpretive assertion:
The scene supports a conflict between present-person autonomy and restoration of antecedent persons.

Interpretive assertions must link back to evidence.

## Neutral coding
Primary coding must not use Patrick's personal preferences as salience weights.

Recommended coding dimensions:
- plot/problem structure
- agency and decision points
- institutions/rules
- interpersonal dynamics
- identity/self-concept
- epistemic/evidence patterns
- ethics/value conflict
- technology/material constraints
- culture/politics
- humor/aesthetics/form when source-supported
- continuity/worldbuilding
- character-state changes
- consequences/unresolved threads
- counterevidence
- genuinely emergent motifs

## Hypothesis testing
For every corpus-level hypothesis, search for:
- supporting evidence
- neutral/irrelevant evidence
- disconfirming evidence

Do not upgrade repetition into truth if repeated records derive from the same upstream source.

## Identity
Workers create local entity occurrences. Global identity is a reconciliation function.

Avoid binary sameness when richer relations are needed. Candidate predicates may include:
- SAME_AS
- COUNTERPART_OF
- DIVERGES_FROM
- TRANSPORT_DUPLICATE_OF
- DERIVED_FROM
- MERGED_FROM
- SIMULATION_OF
- MEMORY_COPY_OF
- SHARED_CAUSAL_HISTORY_WITH

Continuity dimensions are lazy. Do not calculate biological, psychological, legal, social, causal, memory, or timeline continuity unless the evidence makes that dimension relevant.

## Stable facts and the crucible
Most facts can appear in a fast consensus projection after deterministic reconciliation.
Escalate propositions when accepted evidence introduces:
- contradiction
- identity ambiguity
- timeline divergence
- alternate continuity
- memory/reality manipulation
- uncertain narrative frame
- explicit retcon
- unresolved source dependence

Projection states:
- STABLE
- CONTESTED
- UNRESOLVED
- STRUCTURAL_PARADOX

STRUCTURAL_PARADOX is for genuine narrative incompatibility, not worker confusion.

## Drift testing
Use both:
1. fixed regression cases for known difficult Trek situations;
2. synthetic/adversarial anomaly tests.

Core invariants:
- unknown structures remain representable
- contradiction does not force resolution
- unclassifiable evidence is not discarded
- local identities are not silently merged
- missing ontology yields UNKNOWN/PROPOSED, not invented certainty
- source-relative claims do not automatically become world-state facts

## Research depth
Coverage is tiered:
DISCOVERED → SOURCE_BOUND → FULL_TEXT_AVAILABLE → STRUCTURALLY_INDEXED → CLOSE_READ → SEMANTICALLY_ANALYZED → ENTITY_LINKED → CROSS_REFERENCED → AUDITED

Do not pretend one state implies the next.
