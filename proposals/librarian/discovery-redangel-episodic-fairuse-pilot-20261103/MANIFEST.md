## Four factors — *only* for that exact planned context

[17 U.S.C. §107](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t17/s107/) and [US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/) require a factual, aggregate balance. The original Ninth Circuit [*Kelly v. Arriba Soft* thumbnail-index opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2003/07/07/0055521oop.pdf) is relevant to genuine low-resolution navigational indexing, not a blanket still-image exception. [Supreme Court *Warhol v. Goldsmith*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-869_87ad.pdf) cautions that the particular challenged purpose may overlap a photographer's normal licensing market despite newly added meaning or a different caption.

1. **Purpose/character — mixed, significant objection.** Noncommercial source-custody research, tiny subordinate visual, critical prose and immediate link-through support an information-location purpose. But the original studio article **also publishes this exact photo to advertise and identify the same Discovery episode**. Using it as an attractive episode-index tile could repeat precisely that promotional purpose. The indexing rationale depends on a real critical interface; a large Trek gallery or background decorative reuse defeats it.
2. **Nature — against.** Michael Gibson's credited, staged photograph of actors in costume is creative visual expression. It was previously published; that does not erase its copyright or contribution.
3. **Amount/substantiality — mixed.** The photo's entire composition remains represented, albeit resampled without crop to **160×107**, **2,017 bytes** from 1000×668, with no high-resolution download. It cannot plausibly substitute for a print/publicity quality photo; nevertheless copying the whole photo may capture its expressive “heart.” Percent original pixel count is *not* a fair measure of how much protected expression is used.
4. **Market — unresolved and possibly adverse.** A tiny research tile is unlikely to replace the actual episode or full photographer image and routes traffic to the publisher. Nevertheless the credit and studio-controlled publicity setting suggest a plausible normal licensing market for editorials, thumbnails, publicity photo packages, and even small image indices. No rate card or rightsholder-specific market evidence was independently gathered; at library scale repeated use could affect that market. The burden of further adversarial research remains **open**, so the fourth factor cannot be assumed favorable.

**Interim judgment:** a source-linked tiny-use argument deserves independent case-specific review; **public use remains HELD**. Fair use may in law operate without permission where factors warrant, but a publicly accessible photograph and attribution are not a license. Any eventual display needs an exact-image, exact surrounding-text, use/purpose and market falsifier review. No rights, coverage, merge or site effect is requested by this packet.
