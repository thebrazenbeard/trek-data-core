## Four §107 factors — honest objection included

Authorities: [17 U.S.C. §107](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t17/s107/) and [Copyright Office guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/); compare the factual thumbnail search context in [*Kelly v. Arriba Soft*, 336 F.3d 811 (9th Cir. 2003)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2003/07/07/0055521oop.pdf) and purpose-specific inquiry in [*Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith*, 598 U.S. 508 (2023)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-869_87ad.pdf). *Kelly* supports thumbnail source-indexing on its specific circumstances, not automatically every thumbnail; *Warhol* means attaching commentary cannot by itself eliminate a matching publicity/identification purpose.

1. **Purpose/character — genuinely mixed.** Noncommercial, source-custody research navigation, size limits, no image-only gallery, source click-through and actual analysis all favor a new informational indexing function. However the publisher's own film page uses **this exact image to identify/promote the film**, and our thumbnail would also identify the film: that similarity is a serious counterargument. It may be more analogous to replacing a licensed promotional thumbnail than *Kelly*'s independent internet search engine, especially if users browse the still for entertainment. Conditional research use only; actual rendered UI must be examined.
2. **Nature — against.** A dramatic close-up from a creative fictional motion picture is core visual expression. Published status helps but is not dispositive.
3. **Amount/substantiality — mixed.** The source is one entire production photograph/frame-like composition. It is reproduced *in full* at 160×90, only 1,106 bytes; the miniature has little standalone entertainment/print value. Nonetheless copying the whole image can capture its qualitative heart; it is misleading to call it “only 1% of the copyrighted still” merely because pixels were thrown away. No film runtime evidence supports a claim this is one negligible frame of the movie in relation to all separate rights.
4. **Market — material unresolved question.** It cannot substitute for the movie or 1430×805 source quality and directs viewers back to the official page. But there may be a licensing market for small official publicity/editorial stills, catalog thumbnails, or authorized promotional images — possibly directly overlapping **this same** use. No primary market inventory, rate card, studio authorization, or market impact study for this still was obtained. Hundreds of replicated stills would increase potential aggregate substitution. The reviewer should actively seek opposing publicity-license evidence before calling the factor favorable.

**No categorical claim:** A factual, narrowly tailored §107 argument *may* independently justify a no-license thumbnail. A publisher grant is **not categorically required**, but neither a publisher URL nor free access creates a general display license. This specific pilot remains **HOLD pending independent factual/legal-purpose, actual pixel, and final UI/context audit**.
