## Four-factor §107 assessment — specifically this proposed use

Primary authorities: [17 U.S.C. §107](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t17/s107/) and [US Copyright Office fair-use guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/). The [Ninth Circuit's *Kelly v. Arriba Soft*, 336 F.3d 811 (2003), opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2003/07/07/0055521oop.pdf) found a bona fide thumbnail image-search index fair use under its facts, emphasizing indexing function and inability of low-res images to substitute for originals. It cautions that low resolution alone is not dispositive. [*Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith*, 598 U.S. 508 (2023)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/21-869_87ad.pdf) requires the *specific challenged use* and its purpose to be compared against the rightsholder's use; simply labelling an image “educational” or adding prose does not create an automatic new purpose.

1. **Purpose and character — limited favor, conditional.** The intended use identifies a primary-source *episode-index article* for navigation and original, source-critical research, not decoration or commercial resale. Patrick's project is noncommercial; that is relevant but not conclusive. The image would be minute, subordinate to original analysis and link-through, akin to *Kelly* search thumbnails, **if and only if** actual UI behaves this way. Risk: a visually attractive Trek episode gallery could overlap normal publisher promotional uses; the same pixels shown decoratively would materially weaken this rationale. Mere `alt`, title, or attribution is not transformative by itself.
2. **Nature — weighs against.** This is highly creative fictional animated character/scene artwork, not an uncopyrightable factual table or ordinary utilitarian data. It appears publicly published by the studio, which helps somewhat relative to unpublished work, but creative expression remains protected.
3. **Amount and substantiality — mixed, not “1% copied.”** The complete image composition is downsampled from 1434×1080 to 159×120 without crop; 2,616 bytes and 19,080 output pixels limit aesthetic/reproduction function. Nevertheless the *entire scene* is recognizable, so the qualitative heart of the image is copied; “low resolution” cannot erase that. A single still from a longer episode is not automatically a negligible portion of each separate image copyright. This is the smallest readable navigation target tested; further reduction may defeat source identification.
4. **Market — unresolved material risk.** 159×120 cannot plausibly replace quality viewing of a 1434×1080 still or the episode, and the card sends users to the publisher's own page. However publisher/studio licensing of small promotional/editorial images, authorized thumbnail packages, and catalog merchandising markets might exist. No independent market inventory, price terms or rightsholder licensing practice has been verified for this exact image. Repetitive thousands-image gallery scale could change aggregate substitution risk. Do not assert there is *no* adverse market. Independent reviewer should test plausible legitimate licensing harm and the site's actual eventual presentation.

**Interim conclusion:** a defensible *argument for a tiny source-bound contextual research-index fair use* exists, but factor 2 is against and factor 4 remains materially uncertain. **GO for review of an unreleased pilot only; NO-GO for public JPEG deployment pending factual independent four-factor and final UI use-specific audit.** An affirmative publisher license is an alternative, **not a prerequisite if fair use applies**. This proposal is deliberately different from the separate publisher-source inventory [PR #445](https://github.com/thebrazenbeard/trek-data-core/pull/445).
