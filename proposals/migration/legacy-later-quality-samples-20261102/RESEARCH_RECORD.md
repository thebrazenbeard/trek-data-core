## Method and limitations

- The repo's accepted `reports/branch-census-2026-11-02.json` supplies historical PR-head candidates. Exact historical ref names were resolved against independently fetched Git objects at `refs/trek-audit/pr-heads/<number>`; exact blob IDs were obtained from those trees. Content of the sampled blobs was read through the authenticated GitHub blob reader, not by executing any historical code.
- A PR head is a historical proposal snapshot, **not** necessarily its later branch head, a merged change, or current accepted `main`. A staging note saying a source was read is **evidence of the worker's recorded claim**, not an independent fresh re-read of that source. This audit verifies historical research *artifacts* and their contents, not the underlying Trek events.
- The samples distinguish richly structured source-relative research, transcript-derived notes without reproducible byte custody, mere status/sync indexes, and external metadata discovery. Counts from reports are not accepted denominators, and the same upstream transcript family or duplicate branch may not corroborate itself.
- Full copyrighted transcripts, source media, and other protected text were not copied into this repository. Source sites and page locations are identifiers only; source content itself was not independently authenticated in this sampling tranche.

## Additional ref-scope finding

In the sampled later PR range, PRs #128–#150 except #138 correspond to **22 separate TNG staging proposal heads**, most with episode-specific markdown records and local validation JSON, covering portions of Seasons 4–7. The table above samples five TNG per-episode files and does **not** claim the other 17 PR contents were independently checked, or that 22 batches represent disjoint episodes without a full registry crosswalk.

These later PRs are strong falsifiers of an interpretation that the entire repository was always only an empty catalog: substantial original analysis exists in recoverable historical Git blobs. Conversely, #99 is a falsifier of the stronger claim that each historical show necessarily had an audited source read, and #169 is a falsifier of treating episode QID discovery as semantic analysis.

## Migration frontier

1. **Inventory/dedup original blobs**, not just branch names or summary counters. Track source site, episode, transcript edition/line numbering and duplicate branch lineage. Historic PR head vs current branch head must be distinguished.
2. **Librarian source custody:** verify exact full-source editions where lawfully available and record reproducible digest / site/edition locator. Transcript-derived research may be preserved provisionally without promoting AV-specific claims or accepting the prior worker's own source-read claim as independent proof.
3. **Schema migration:** for each bounded batch, map staged worker-local entities, evidence frames and assertions to current schema with evidence locators, explicit source-family independence and immutable supersession. Keep summary-only artifact quality separate from line-ranged per-work evidence.
4. **Independent adversarial re-read:** sample source against the staged claims; particularly altered/perceptual frames, apparent corroboration, chronology and counterevidence. Reject contaminated or missing source tails instead of transferring historic counters.
5. **Authority gate:** only separately accepted Source↔Work binding, batch manifest, tests, and reviewed coverage transition can advance accepted `main`. This report itself changes none.

## Hostile challenges

> These are model-authored historical notes, not primary source bytes. A detailed paragraph can be original and still wrong, incomplete, or derived from a recap. The documented URL plus line range improves testability but does not by itself authenticate the past retrieval or prove full reading.

> Episode and season ordinals can differ across publisher representations; the migration must key work identity to reviewed work/source records, not a historical filename, an external dataset's QID, or a worker's old batch count.
