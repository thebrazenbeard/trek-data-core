## Neutral close-read findings

### S01E01 — The Vulcan Hello

- Burnham initially resists speculative threat claims about an unknown object, then progressively adopts a Klingon-threat model as new evidence appears. Her judgments remain character reasoning rather than omniscient fact.
- Georgiou and Burnham diverge over whether first contact should prioritize Starfleet diplomatic norms or a preemptive tactic Burnham attributes to Vulcan experience with Klingons.
- Burnham's broader Klingon-threat framing is explicitly challenged by Starfleet leadership that distinguishes species from political/cultural behavior; the episode therefore contains counterevidence against species-essentialist coding.
- T'Kuvma's unification project is presented through his own speeches and followers' responses. His claims about Federation intent remain political/religious testimony until separately supported.

### S01E02 — Battle at the Binary Stars

- Burnham commits mutiny and later accepts responsibility for her acts. That does not by itself establish the broader causal claim that she caused the war.
- T'Kuvma's death, Georgiou's death, Burnham's actions, and Klingon/Federation escalation are directly depicted; later institutional or personal claims about who caused the war should remain attributed judgments.
- Starfleet attempts communication while Klingon actors pursue their own strategic/religious aims. The episode supports conflict between incompatible interpretations and goals rather than one universally shared account.
- Burnham survives and is imprisoned; legal culpability, causal responsibility for the wider war, and moral evaluation remain distinct dimensions.

### S01E03 — Context Is for Kings

- Burnham initially suspects Discovery's classified work may involve biological weapons. Lorca later identifies the project as experimental propulsion research. The source therefore directly disconfirms Burnham's initial hypothesis.
- Stamets describes the mycelial network and the Glenn/Discovery research program; his scientific explanation is testimony that gains support from later demonstrations but should not be promoted beyond what is depicted.
- Lorca frames wartime necessity as justification for extraordinary research and personnel use. Stamets resists militarization of his work. This is an explicit institutional/scientific conflict.
- Burnham's stigmatized status among prisoners and Starfleet personnel is depicted separately from her actual technical competence and later assignment.

### S01E04 — The Butcher's Knife Cares Not for the Lamb's Cry

- Ripper is initially interpreted as a violent monster/weapon from appearance and lethal behavior. Burnham develops a self-defense hypothesis; later evidence from Saru's threat response and Ripper's interaction with spores supports a navigation/symbiosis model.
- Landry maintains a weapon-oriented interpretation and dies while attempting to exploit the creature. Her interpretation remains part of the evidence record rather than being erased after later model revision.
- Discovery's use of Ripper as a spore-drive navigator succeeds operationally but visibly harms the creature, creating an exploitation/necessity conflict.
- Klingon political developments are source-relative to the characters and factions shown; claims about house loyalty or succession are not automatically species-wide facts.

### S01E05 — Choose Your Pain

- Burnham's concern that Ripper is being harmed begins as a hypothesis. Culber's later scans report cumulative neurological injury, materially strengthening the claim with medical evidence.
- Tests raise the possibility of sentience rather than establishing it immediately. Saru explicitly weighs unresolved person-status concerns against the lives of 134 crew members.
- Stamets substitutes himself as the spore-drive interface, changing the immediate ethical configuration without resolving the technology's long-term safety.
- Saru ultimately orders Ripper released because Starfleet has no legitimate claim over it. The decision is an institutional/command action, not retroactive proof that every earlier character judgment was correct.
- Lorca's captivity and Mudd's conduct provide testimony about Klingon treatment and Mudd's motives; neither should be generalized beyond the depicted context without additional evidence.

## Cross-episode hypotheses to test later, not conclusions

2. Wartime necessity creates recurring conflict with ordinary scientific, medical, legal, and ethical constraints.
3. Person-status questions around nonhuman/synthetic/altered beings emerge through evidence and institutional treatment rather than by name or appearance alone.
