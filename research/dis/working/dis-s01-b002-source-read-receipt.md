## Neutral close-read findings

### S01E06 — Lethe

- Sarek's apparent recollection of Burnham's rejection initially supports Burnham's interpretation that she failed him; the later mind-meld reveals a materially different account in which Sarek was forced to choose between Burnham and Spock and concealed that choice from Burnham. The episode therefore contains both a false/incomplete remembered explanation and later corrective testimony within the same mind-linked frame.
- The katra link is established through Burnham's experience, prior history, Vulcan confirmation of Sarek's sabotage, and successful use of the link to recover his transponder. Metaphysical extrapolations beyond those depicted functions remain interpretation.
- Cornwell challenges Lorca's post-Buran fitness and alleges that he deceived prior psychological evaluations. Lorca admits lying and needing help, but subsequently arranges circumstances in which Cornwell leaves Discovery for the peace mission. The episode supports institutional concern and Lorca's admission; motive beyond what is depicted remains open.
- Tyler's service record and personal-history claims are treated as verified by Lorca's background check inside the episode, but later-series identity complications mean this worker should preserve those claims as source-relative records rather than use them as global identity proof.
- Burnham shifts from a single-path command model for Tilly to acknowledging multiple routes to command, a concrete change in mentoring stance rather than a general thesis about leadership.

### S01E07 — Magic to Make the Sanest Man Go Mad

- Mudd creates repeated approximately thirty-minute loops. Most crew members reset without memory; Stamets retains cross-loop memory, attributed by Stamets/Burnham to his tardigrade-DNA/mycelial condition. The episode depicts the retention and the correlation but does not independently prove the characters' causal explanation.
- Repeated deaths inside reset loops are experienced and remembered by Stamets; only the final non-reset state persists into ordinary continuity. This distinction must remain explicit in any later evidence model.
- Burnham supplies Mudd with her identity and apparent value as a Klingon target as part of a deliberate deception designed to induce one more reset. Her self-description is strategically true in key particulars while embedded in a con.
- Mudd's stated revenge narrative concerning Stella is directly undermined by records showing he fled Stella and her father after taking her dowry. His testimony therefore has explicit disconfirming evidence in the same episode.
- Burnham/Tyler relationship development includes events remembered by Stamets but erased for Burnham and Tyler by the loop reset. Later relational assertions should distinguish experienced-but-erased events from retained autobiographical memory.

### S01E08 — Si Vis Pacem, Para Bellum

- Starfleet's first-contact procedure requires Pahvan understanding and consent before altering their transmitter. Burnham and Tyler later proceed after concluding Saru is compromised, while explicitly recognizing the procedural conflict. The episode therefore supports an ethics/institutional conflict rather than uncomplicated authorization.
- Saru reports lifelong Kelpien fear and experiences temporary freedom from it on Pahvo. His subsequent lying, coercion, communicator destruction, and attack on Burnham occur while he describes himself as finally in harmony. He later insists that the actions were still his, resisting a simple external-control account.
- The Pahvans' actual intervention differs from Starfleet's plan: rather than merely enabling cloak detection, they signal both Federation and Klingon forces in an attempt to bring the parties together. Burnham's initial modification and the Pahvans' later action must not be flattened into one intent.
- L'Rell offers defection to Cornwell and attacks her as part of an escape plan, then later presents a different account to Kol. Her statements in captivity are operationally motivated and require source-relative treatment.
- Stamets describes episodes of altered knowledge/identity continuity after genetic manipulation and withholds them to protect Culber from professional conflict. The symptoms are self-report at this stage, not yet a settled mechanism.

### S01E09 — Into the Forest I Go

- Lorca deliberately delays compliance with Starfleet's retreat order and creates a plan to return to Pahvo if a cloak-breaking solution can be found. This is depicted command disobedience with an explicit protective/strategic rationale.
- Culber's scans report structural changes in Stamets' medial temporal white matter before Lorca asks for 133 micro-jumps. Medical objection is explicit; Lorca orders the mission to proceed and later uses Stamets' exploratory interest as part of his persuasion.
- Stamets completes the jump sequence under escalating physiological stress. Culber orders abort on medical grounds; Lorca orders continued support because of the stakes. This provides direct evidence of a command/medical ethics conflict.
- Tyler's severe reaction aboard the Klingon ship is initially framed by Cornwell as trauma/PTSD. Later L'Rell-linked flashes and statements introduce evidence that this explanation is incomplete, but S01E09 alone does not yet establish the transformation mechanism.
- Burnham openly identifies herself to Kol as T'Kuvma's killer to draw him into combat and buy time. Kol states that Burnham's earlier act facilitated his own rise. His causal claim is testimony from a politically interested participant and should remain so.
- The final jump fails to reach Starbase 46. Stamets shows acute neurological symptoms and describes perceiving many/infinite permutations; the ship's location becomes unknown. The episode establishes the anomalous state, not yet its full interpretation.

### S01E10 — Despite Yourself

- Sensor and recovered-data evidence establishes that Discovery is in a parallel universe with different quantum signatures. The crew infers that their Discovery may have exchanged places with this universe's Discovery; that exchange remains an inference at this episode's point of evidence.
- Recovered records describe the Terran Empire as a human supremacist regime and show familiar persons/ships occupying different political and biographical roles. Similar names/appearances are therefore insufficient for global SAME_AS reconciliation.
- Records concerning Burnham, Lorca, Tilly, Connor, and others are alternate-universe records and must be scoped accordingly. The episode itself repeatedly demonstrates counterpart divergence.
- Defiant records introduce a cross-universe and temporal anomaly: data suggests a ship from the crew's universe will enter this universe's past. The episode presents this as recovered intelligence plus crew interpretation, not as a fully explained causal mechanism.
- Tyler reports memory flashes and explicitly questions whether he is himself. L'Rell says he has another name; Culber's deeper scans find extensive bodily alteration and lead him to theorize that a new personality may have been overlaid on an original one. Culber explicitly marks this as theory and says the endpoint is still unknown.
- Earlier tests had reportedly found no hidden personality engrams. The new overlay theory is therefore not simple confirmation of the earlier model but a revised hypothesis prompted by new anatomical evidence.
- Culber's statement that, for clinical purposes, Tyler is 'not you' is a medical judgment in context and must not be promoted by the worker into a global identity verdict.
- Discovery adopts Terran identities and behavior as a survival cover. Actions and statements made under that cover need explicit frame tagging so performed-Terran conduct is not automatically treated as stable Prime-character values.

## Cross-episode hypotheses to test later, not conclusions

1. Wartime necessity repeatedly conflicts with Starfleet medical, legal, and first-contact constraints. Supporting evidence exists in E06-E09, but later episodes must be searched for neutral and disconfirming cases before corpus-level promotion.

3. Lorca repeatedly privileges strategic outcomes over institutional constraint. The pattern is supported here, but motive and identity explanations must remain unresolved pending later primary-source evidence.
