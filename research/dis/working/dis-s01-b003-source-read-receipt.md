## Neutral close-read findings

### S01E11 — The Wolf Inside

- Burnham is ordered to destroy the Harlak resistance but seeks another course because the coalition contains multiple species cooperating under a Klingon leader. Her attempt to learn how that coalition functions is both mission-relevant and peace-seeking; the episode does not reduce it to simple insubordination or simple altruism.
- Mirror Sarek performs a mind-meld and concludes that Burnham intends no harm. This is evidence of Sarek's assessment within the mind-meld frame, not automatic proof of every motive Burnham may have.
- Burnham protects the resistance by arranging evacuation before a staged attack and accepts temporary intelligence that will become useless after evacuation. Her apparent compliance with Terran orders is therefore deliberately deceptive and must be frame-tagged.
- Tyler's recovered memories culminate in self-identification as Voq and an admission that he killed Culber. He then attempts to kill Burnham. These are strong identity/self-report and action records, but Discovery still treats him under Federation custody rather than resolving global personhood by self-identification alone.
- Saru refuses Terran-style execution and remands Tyler to the brig for tribunal under Federation law. Legal treatment therefore diverges from Tyler's own identity claim and from Terran expectations.
- Tilly develops a theory that Stamets' abnormal neural activity functions as a link into the mycelial network and uses spores therapeutically. The episode depicts physiological improvement and revival, supporting the intervention while leaving some causal explanation interpretive.

### S01E12 — Vaulting Ambition

- Stamets experiences an apparent encounter with his Terran counterpart inside the mycelial network. The counterpart explains prior visions and claims both consciousnesses are present in the network while their bodies remain elsewhere. These claims occur within an altered/mycelial frame and should not be treated as ordinary physical co-presence.
- Culber appears to Stamets within that network and identifies himself as dead while warning that damage to the network is spreading. The later return of Stamets to consciousness supports that the encounter had operational consequences, but the precise ontological status of the Culber appearance remains source-frame dependent.
- L'Rell gives the clearest transformation account so far: Tyler was captured, his DNA and consciousness were harvested/reconstructed, Voq was physically altered to appear human, and Voq's psyche was grafted into Tyler's. She explicitly says she does not know where Voq ends and Tyler begins. The source therefore directly supports an unresolved composite/continuity state rather than binary replacement.
- Medical data continues to show Tyler's genome matching Starfleet's Ash Tyler record while his brain activity and behavior diverge. Biological, psychological, memory, and legal identity dimensions are therefore not aligned.
- Georgiou's account of the Defiant says interphasic transit produced temporal displacement and severe cognitive effects. This is testimony based on Imperial records, not direct observation by the Discovery crew.
- Burnham and Georgiou infer that Lorca is the Terran counterpart of Discovery's apparent captain based on his history, light sensitivity, access, and the pattern of the 133 jumps. The episode ends with the conclusion stated, while E13 supplies direct Lorca confirmation.

### S01E13 — What's Past Is Prologue

- Lorca confirms that he originated in the Terran universe and describes an ion-storm/transporter accident that carried him to the Prime universe after his failed coup. This converts the prior episode's inference into direct testimony from Lorca, though details of the transit mechanism remain his account.
- Lorca repeatedly interprets his survival and return as destiny. Burnham rejects his claim that he can determine her future and explicitly frames her path as her own responsibility. The episode therefore contains direct counterevidence to treating Lorca's destiny language as narrative fact.
- Discovery's crew identifies the Charon's mycelial reactor as draining and poisoning the network. Stamets/Tilly predict broader collapse; after the reactor is destroyed, the network visibly regenerates. The sequence supports the local causal relationship while the full multiversal extinction prediction remains a scientific forecast rather than directly observed outcome.
- Saru explicitly reframes Discovery as belonging to its crew rather than Lorca and rejects a no-win conclusion. His command decisions are contrasted with Lorca's instrumental use of Starfleet personnel without requiring a general theory of command style.
- Burnham pretends to accept Lorca's offer in order to lower the containment field. Her apparent allegiance is operational deception and must not be coded as a stable value change.
- Georgiou kills Lorca after Burnham refuses to do so. Burnham then transports Georgiou away from the doomed Charon despite Georgiou's stated intention to remain and die. The result is an involuntary cross-universe displacement with later political consequences.
- Discovery returns to the Prime universe but overshoots by nine months. This establishes a concrete temporal displacement caused by the return sequence; the crew's later understanding of the war is therefore nine months out of step with their departure point.

### S01E14 — The War Without, The War Within

- L'Rell's procedure leaves Tyler in a state that medical staff explicitly characterize as neither simply human nor Klingon, possibly both, and not yet knowable without understanding the reassignment science. This is direct evidence for preserving identity uncertainty.
- Tyler says he can still access Voq's memories but experiences them as someone else's life. Memory continuity therefore persists while subjective ownership is altered.
- Saru assigns responsibility for prior crimes to Voq and declines to imprison Tyler, while restricting his privileges. That is an institutional/legal judgment, not a global ontology decision.
- Burnham cannot simply resume her relationship with Tyler despite intellectually separating him from Voq's acts. Her embodied memory of Tyler/Voq attempting to kill her conflicts with conceptual exculpation. The episode therefore preserves distinct legal, psychological, relational, and memory-continuity dimensions.
- Cornwell and Sarek initially verify Discovery's crew because the ship was believed destroyed. Their later conclusion is that the wreckage belonged to the Terran Discovery, implying the two Discoverys exchanged universes. This is a reconstruction from wreckage plus crew evidence, not something directly witnessed by Starfleet Command.
- The Klingon war has fragmented among the Great Houses rather than becoming a unified command structure. Cornwell reports severe Federation losses; L'Rell describes the conflict as one that will not end without conquest. Her claim is politically interested testimony and not automatically a universal Klingon position.
- Burnham asks Georgiou how the Terran Empire defeated the Klingons. Georgiou advocates destruction at the Klingon center of power and later bargains for freedom in exchange for helping Starfleet. This begins an explicit institutional compromise with Terran methods rather than merely a private Burnham choice.
- Georgiou is treated simultaneously as a dangerous alternate-universe ruler, a possible asylum case, a restricted guest/prisoner, and a strategic asset. Those statuses should remain separately coded.

### S01E15 — Will You Take My Hand?

- Starfleet secretly authorizes Georgiou to execute a plan that would make Qo'noS uninhabitable by detonating a hydro-bomb in the volcanic system. Tilly discovers that the supposed mapping drone is actually the weapon, and Burnham concludes Georgiou is following Starfleet orders. The episode therefore depicts institutional authorization of an exterminatory strategy under existential pressure.
- Burnham challenges Cornwell by explicitly comparing the plan to her own earlier belief that survival outranked principle. Cornwell then accepts a change of plan. This is important counterevidence both to a claim that Starfleet always follows its ideals and to a claim that it inevitably abandons them under threat.
- The replacement strategy gives L'Rell control of the detonator so she can threaten Qo'noS and compel the Klingon houses to stop the war and accept her authority. The war ends through coercive leverage, not through an ordinary negotiated peace process.
- Georgiou and Burnham explicitly reject counterpart equivalence: Georgiou states she is not Burnham's Georgiou, and Burnham recognizes that fact. Emotional resemblance and shared appearance therefore do not establish SAME_AS.
- Tyler uses autobiographical markers from his human life while retaining Voq's memories and says he may be useful to both sides. His subsequent choice to accompany L'Rell is an agency decision made from a mixed-continuity state, not evidence that one antecedent identity has simply erased the other.
- Burnham later tells Tyler that she sees Ash in him. This is relational recognition and character testimony, not a global identity resolution.
- Federation institutions formally pardon Burnham, expunge her record, restore/advance her commission to commander, admit Tilly to command training, and honor Saru. These are explicit institutional status changes and should be recorded separately from moral evaluation.
- Starfleet chooses not to continue using a human as the spore-drive interface while seeking a non-human alternative. This is a concrete institutional response to the prior biological/medical costs of the technology.
- The season ends when Discovery receives a priority-one distress call identified as coming from Captain Pike aboard USS Enterprise. This establishes the next source frontier but does not itself resolve any cross-series identity link.

## Cross-episode hypotheses to test later, not conclusions

1. Discovery repeatedly separates biological, psychological, memory, legal, relational, and self-identified continuity rather than treating personhood as one binary variable. S01E11-E15 strongly support this, especially Tyler/Voq, but later evidence must still be allowed to revise or complicate the pattern.
2. Wartime institutions can authorize conduct inconsistent with their stated norms and can also reverse course through internal dissent. The Qo'noS plan supplies both supporting and disconfirming evidence against simplistic institutional models.
3. Mirror-universe resemblance produces emotionally powerful counterpart relationships without erasing factual divergence. Burnham/Georgiou and Lorca/Lorca are strong cases; global cross-universe identity remains reconciliation work.
4. Claims framed as destiny are repeatedly contested by choices made under pressure. Lorca treats events as fate; Burnham explicitly rejects that framing and acts against it.
5. Scientific explanations around the mycelial network often begin as testimony/hypothesis and gain partial support through successful intervention, but the narrative still leaves ontological questions about consciousness, death, and network-presence unresolved.
