## Neutral close-read findings

### S02E01 — Brother

- Pike assumes temporary command of Discovery under an emergency command-transfer regulation, with Saru requiring DNA-authenticated transfer. Pike explicitly distinguishes his command style from Lorca's and invites open disagreement; this is a depicted command transition, not proof of a general Starfleet command norm.
- The seven red bursts are treated operationally as signals because of their synchronized anomalous appearance. Their cause, sender, and intent remain unknown at this point. Pike connects the mission's urgency to Starfleet's recent experience with an anomaly preceding the Klingon war, but that concern does not establish a causal relationship.
- Burnham initially warns against Connolly's preferred navigation approach around the asteroid field. Connolly rejects her advice and is killed. Later, Pike allows Burnham to depart from his earlier mission restriction to rescue him after the crew argues against abandoning him. The episode supports command flexibility under changed circumstances rather than a simple obedience/disobedience binary.
- The Hiawatha survivors have remained alive for roughly ten months through Reno's improvised engineering and medical interventions. Her explanations of those techniques are direct testimony supported by the surviving patients, but broader judgments about Starfleet medicine or engineering should not be inferred from one emergency setting.
- A dark-matter asteroid sample is retained for study. Possible interactions with the spore system are hypotheses at this stage, not established mechanisms.
- Stamets plans to transfer away from Discovery because the ship and spore system evoke grief associated with Culber. The transfer is postponed by Pike's mission. Grief, professional intent, and institutional assignment are distinct states.
- Pike states that Enterprise was intentionally withheld from the Klingon war so it could serve as a last-resort Federation asset if the war was lost. This is Pike's account of Starfleet strategy, not independently reconstructed institutional history within the episode.
- Spock's personal log records recurring visions/nightmares connected to the red signals. Pike says he trusted Spock enough to grant leave. Burnham admits responsibility for the breakdown of her relationship with Spock but does not yet give the full causal account.

### S02E02 — New Eden

- The spore drive has been decommissioned because using a genetically modified human navigator conflicts with Starfleet's ban on genetic manipulation; its wartime use had been tolerated as an emergency exception. Pike seeks authorization to reactivate it for the signals mission. This is explicit institutional rule/exception evidence.
- Stamets says he encountered the deceased Culber in the mycelial network. He had initially interpreted the experience as memory generated by his own mind and now expresses uncertainty. The source therefore preserves multiple explanations rather than establishing the encounter's ontology.
- The Terralysium settlement descends from Earth humans displaced from the period of World War III despite lacking the technology needed for interstellar travel. Their syncretic religion interprets the displacement as divine rescue. The historical displacement is supported by records and material evidence; the religious explanation is community belief.
- Pike entertains the possibility that the red burst deliberately brought Discovery to Terralysium. Burnham warns against assigning intent to an unexplained phenomenon. Both interpretations remain in the evidentiary record.
- General Order 1 governs the initial decision not to reveal advanced technology to the pre-warp settlement. Pike and Burnham disagree over whether the unusual human origin changes the ethical problem. Pike ultimately reveals the truth only to Jacob, who had independently accumulated evidence of an offworld origin, and exchanges a durable power source for historical camera evidence. This is a bounded departure from strict concealment, not wholesale abandonment of noninterference.
- Pike initially withholds his sighting of the winged/angelic figure because he thinks it may have been a hallucination. Burnham declines to interpret the figure as divine. The sighting is therefore a visual experience with explicitly unresolved explanation.
- Discovery prevents radioactive debris from devastating Terralysium. Residents interpret the event through their religious framework, while the crew's intervention has a technological explanation within the episode. These two frames should not be collapsed.
- Tilly acts against medical advice while attempting to develop a nonhuman spore-interface solution. Saru warns her about recklessness and self-care; her continued work nevertheless contributes to solving the immediate crisis. The source supplies both harmful-risk and productive-consequence evidence.
- The apparent childhood acquaintance May is identified by Federation records as someone who died years earlier. That record contradicts a normal physical-presence interpretation and motivates investigation of the apparition.

### S02E03 — Point of Light

- L'Rell's authority over the Klingon houses is contested. Tyler describes himself as Klingon whose body was changed to appear human and asks L'Rell to treat him as Voq, while L'Rell says intimacy with him in Tyler's body feels like a violation. Self-identification, bodily history, and relational recognition therefore diverge.
- Tyler learns that he and L'Rell have a child. He retains Voq's memories and says the child made his divided experience feel whole, yet also says he cannot simply be Voq. This is additional evidence for mixed continuity rather than a binary restoration of one antecedent identity.
- The child becomes a political vulnerability. Georgiou and Section 31 help stage Tyler's and the child's deaths; the child is placed at Boreth and Tyler is recruited into Section 31. L'Rell's later public account is deliberate political deception and must be coded as such.
- Georgiou operates under a false Starfleet-facing presentation as the retired Prime-universe captain/security consultant. Later evidence in the tranche shows that Section 31 leadership knows her actual Terran origin, while Pike does not.
- Pike initially refuses to decrypt Spock's protected medical file because of regulations. After learning Spock is accused of multiple murders, Pike orders Burnham to break the encryption so Spock can receive a fair investigation. The episode again depicts rule adherence being overridden by a competing institutional value.
- Spock's medical file describes intact reasoning alongside severe emotional/empathetic disturbance. Characters consider several explanations for the alleged killings, including innocence, compromised self-defense, and severe pathology. None is established merely by being discussed.
- Amanda reveals that Spock reported the Red Angel as a child and gave information that helped locate young Burnham when she ran away. Family members had previously rationalized the event differently. This supports a long-duration history of Spock's claimed visions without proving their cause.
- Burnham tells Amanda that she intentionally wounded Spock emotionally as a child because she believed distancing him would protect him from Logic Extremists targeting her. This is Burnham's causal/motive account and Amanda's response, not omniscient proof of every motive involved.
- The entity using May's appearance displays knowledge and reactions inconsistent with an ordinary hallucination generated solely from Tilly's memories. Stamets then detects a physical multidimensional fungal organism. The May appearance is therefore an adopted relational form rather than the deceased May simply returning as an ordinary human.

### S02E04 — An Obol for Charon

- Number One reports that the Spock case is highly classified and says elements of the official account do not add up. Her investigation is described as outside or at the edge of ordinary authorization; its findings should remain attributed to her rather than treated as settled institutional truth.
- Discovery encounters an ancient sphere that initially immobilizes and damages the ship. Burnham explicitly warns against assigning anthropomorphic intent too early. Pike initially treats the encounter as threatening; Saru later hypothesizes that the sphere is dying and trying to communicate. Its subsequent transmission of accumulated data and release of Discovery support the latter interpretation while documenting how the crew's model changed with evidence.
- What initially presents as a destructive computer/language infection becomes understood as an information transfer from the sphere. Discovery receives an enormous archive representing the sphere's accumulated observations. The transfer itself is depicted; the truth of every contained observation is not independently audited in the episode.
- Saru experiences Vaharai and believes death is biologically inevitable based on Kelpien knowledge and culture. When his threat ganglia separate naturally and his condition stabilizes, the expected death does not occur and his fear response changes. This directly falsifies the prior inevitability model available to Saru.
- Saru concludes that the Kelpien 'Great Balance' account is false and begins questioning the noninterference promise that prevents his return to Kaminar. His conclusion is grounded in new personal biological evidence but broader Kaminar social history remains to be tested in later sources.
- The JahSepp entity admits entering Tilly through the memory-form of May because Tilly would trust that appearance. It also says Discovery's spore passages are damaging the JahSepp ecosystem and asks Tilly for help. These ecological claims are alien testimony at E04 and receive additional perspective in E05.
- Tilly's emotional resistance is affected by chemicals secreted by the organism. Characters offer competing interpretations of whether the effect is calming or suppressing resistance; the source does not require one motive label.
- Sensor data recovered from the sphere provides a trail connected to Spock's shuttle, advancing the search without explaining Spock's actions.

### S02E05 — Saints of Imperfection

- Scans of the cocoon left by the JahSepp find no human remains, leading Stamets to hypothesize that Tilly was transported into the mycelial network rather than destroyed. The later rescue supports the transport hypothesis, but his initial reasoning should remain distinct from later confirmation.
- The JahSepp says Tilly was broken down, moved across a dimensional boundary, and reconstituted in its environment. The episode subsequently depicts Tilly alive there and successfully returned, supporting the claimed transport function without requiring broader conclusions about all forms of continuity through such transport.
- Georgiou is found aboard Spock's shuttle rather than Spock. Pike distrusts the murder allegations against Spock and Section 31's handling of the search. Leland is identified as Section 31's leader and knows Georgiou's Terran identity; Pike does not. Burnham temporarily withholds that truth from Pike while asking for time to explain.
- Tyler describes Section 31 as a place where people who do not fit elsewhere can still serve. Burnham explicitly challenges that positive framing because Georgiou is among its agents. The source therefore supplies both internal justification and skeptical counterevidence concerning the institution.
- Stamets proposes a partial spore jump that leaves Discovery partly within the mycelial network long enough to recover Tilly. Pike authorizes the extreme risk and frames Starfleet as a mutual promise not to leave crew behind. This is Pike's command/value statement in the immediate rescue context, not automatically a universal Starfleet rule.
- May identifies a destructive 'monster' pursuing the JahSepp. When the crew encounters the being, it is identified as Culber. Evidence that he uses toxic mycelial material defensively causes the crew to reinterpret apparent predation as self-protection. Tilly explicitly reframes the conflict reciprocally: from Culber's perspective, the JahSepp are the threat. This is strong counterevidence against one-sided threat coding.
- Stamets uses highly specific shared autobiographical memories to persuade Culber that he recognizes him as Hugh. Culber had believed he might be losing his mind. Relational recognition and memory continuity are strong identity evidence but do not by themselves settle all biological or causal continuity dimensions.
- Stamets theorizes that Culber's energy entered the network through him at death and later took form there. May explains that Culber's body was built from JahSepp/network matter, which is why he cannot simply cross into normal space. These are scientific/alien explanations linked to observed constraints, not a reason for the worker to declare an unqualified metaphysical 'resurrection' mechanism.
- Culber chooses to let the JahSepp reclaim his network-constructed matter rather than continue harming their ecosystem. Stamets resists losing him again. Tilly and May instead devise an uncertain reconstruction attempt using Culber's DNA plus the cocoon made from normal-space matter. The attempt returns Culber to normal space. Biological, psychological, memory, causal, and legal continuity should therefore remain separately representable rather than collapsing the event into SAME_AS by fiat.
- Section 31 assists Discovery during the rescue, while command authority between Pike and Leland is openly contested. Admiral Cornwell later rebukes Leland for concealing his vessel from Pike, while also defending Section 31 as a necessary Starfleet intelligence division. This produces institutional evidence both for oversight failure and for formal Starfleet acceptance of the organization.
- Residual tachyon radiation associated with a red signal is said to be consistent with time travel but also with cloaking or transporter effects. Cornwell explicitly preserves multiple possibilities. The signal's mechanism remains unresolved.
- The recovered subtitle source has apparent extra preview contamination after the episode's closing narration. Those lines were excluded from E05 coding; their presence is a source-quality defect, not episode evidence.

## Cross-episode hypotheses to test later, not conclusions

1. Discovery repeatedly depicts Starfleet rules as genuine constraints that can be overridden through explicit emergency exceptions, competing duties, or command judgment. E01-E05 supports this through command transfer, genetic-manipulation restrictions, General Order 1, medical-record access, and the Tilly rescue, but later episodes must supply neutral and disconfirming cases before corpus-level promotion.
2. The red signals/Red Angel accumulate observations without yielding a stable causal explanation. Characters repeatedly generate competing models and sometimes explicitly resist assigning intent. This is an epistemic pattern, not evidence that every signal-related claim is equally plausible.
3. Identity continuity remains multidimensional across Tyler/Voq, May/JahSepp, and Culber. Biological substrate, memory, self-identification, relational recognition, causal history, and institutional treatment do not always align.
4. Threat interpretation is repeatedly revised when additional evidence becomes available: the sphere, the JahSepp/Culber conflict, and several signal-related events begin under one frame and change as observations accumulate. This supports tracking model revision rather than coding first impressions as objective world-state.
5. Section 31 is shown neither as simply external to Starfleet nor as ethically equivalent to ordinary Starfleet command. It is institutionally embedded, defended by senior leadership, and simultaneously criticized for secrecy and methods. Later evidence should test both integration and conflict.
