## Neutral close-read findings

### S02E06 — The Sound of Thunder

- The red signal over Kaminar arrives before the crew has a settled model of the Red Angel. Pike and Tyler explicitly entertain competing possibilities, including rescue, warning, and causal responsibility for crises. Too few observations exist to establish intent.
- Saru returns to Kaminar despite his prior promise not to do so. Pike treats contact with the Kelpiens under General Order 1 as a difficult judgment call connected to the signal mission rather than as an uncomplicated authorization.
- Culber's reconstituted body is medically described as genetically continuous with him yet newly reconstructed at the molecular level. He retains autobiographical memory but says he does not feel like himself. Biological reconstruction, memory continuity, subjective identity, and institutional recognition therefore do not collapse into one dimension.
- Saru reveals that he survived Vaharai. The Ba'ul assert a property-like claim over him, while Pike identifies Saru as a Starfleet officer/asylum-protected person under Federation authority. The competing legal/political claims should remain distinct from biological identity.
- Sphere-derived biosignature records lead the crew to conclude that evolved Kelpiens existed in the past and that the Ba'ul were once near extinction before evolved Kelpiens disappeared. This is archive-derived historical evidence plus crew analysis, not direct depiction of the ancient events.
- Saru and Discovery deliberately transmit the Sphere-derived Vaharai trigger through the Ba'ul system, causing Kelpiens across Kaminar to enter the transition. The crew explicitly discusses the danger that changing the biological balance could reverse predator/prey outcomes or threaten the Ba'ul. This intervention therefore carries acknowledged uncertainty and risk.
- The Ba'ul respond by attempting to destroy Kelpien settlements rather than permit the transformation. An advanced humanoid/Angel intervention disables Ba'ul systems and prevents the attack. The event supports protective action by this appearance of the Angel without establishing the intent or identity of every Red Angel manifestation.
- Siranna remains on Kaminar to help her people through the transformation. The resulting political/social settlement remains unresolved at the end of the episode.

### S02E07 — Light and Shadows

- Residual tachyon activity near Kaminar develops into a temporal anomaly. Pike and Tyler encounter events and signals that do not follow ordinary chronological order while inside it.
- Tyler interprets Pike's willingness to take personal risks as partly connected to guilt over Enterprise sitting out the Klingon war. Pike later acknowledges that Tyler may have identified something real about his pattern of assigning himself dangerous missions. This partially supports Tyler's interpretation without making it an exhaustive account of Pike's motives.
- A probe sent into the anomaly returns apparently centuries older and technologically altered, then attacks the shuttle and Discovery's systems. The future modification is evidenced by its condition and behavior; the modifying agent remains unknown.
- Amanda secretly shelters Spock and invokes family/diplomatic authority. Sarek argues that she has abused his position and wants Spock surrendered to Section 31. Family loyalty, legal authority, and institutional duty directly conflict.
- Spock exhibits severe repetitive/disordered cognition. Amanda connects aspects of his presentation to a childhood learning difference and his prior use of `Alice in Wonderland`, but the current condition is also associated with his Red Angel/time experiences. The childhood history does not by itself explain the present state.
- Burnham takes Spock to Section 31. Georgiou privately warns Burnham that the procedure being used is a destructive memory-extraction process and helps engineer Spock's escape. Georgiou also states self-interested reasons involving Leland, so the act should not be reduced to uncomplicated altruism.
- Stamets uses his altered relationship to the mycelial network as a temporal-navigation aid in the anomaly. The successful rescue supports the operational technique without settling broader claims about the network's relationship to time.
- Burnham recognizes that Spock's repeated number sequence yields coordinates for Talos IV when reversed, redirecting the investigation.
- Georgiou tells Leland that she knows he bears responsibility for the deaths of Burnham's parents. At this stage that is Georgiou's accusation; E10 later supplies Leland's own confession and additional context.

### S02E08 — If Memory Serves

- Talos IV is saturated with deliberately generated illusion and mind-mediated experience. The apparent dangerous approach to the planet is itself a Talosian test. Evidence presented through Talosian projection therefore requires explicit frame tagging.
- The Talosians describe Spock as experiencing time in an unusual, less linear fashion and agree to help him in exchange for access to Burnham's painful childhood memory. Their explanation is alien testimony coupled to demonstrated telepathic capabilities, not ordinary sensor evidence.
- Spock's restored account says the Red Angel warned him of Burnham's childhood danger and later showed him a future without conscious life. He interprets the signals and visions as communication. These are recovered mental experiences and Spock's interpretation, not direct observation of the future by Discovery.
- The Talosian-mediated childhood memory shows Burnham intentionally hurting young Spock emotionally because she believed separation would protect him and the family from Logic Extremists. Spock accepts her protective motive while identifying lasting consequences for his own retreat into logic. The source preserves both motive and consequence perspectives.
- Spock denies the murder accusation, and the Talosian-assisted reconstruction gives Burnham reason to believe there were no murders by him. This conclusion later receives independent support when the visual evidence is exposed as fabricated in E09; E08 alone should preserve its Talosian/memory evidentiary frame.
- Culber remembers his relationship with Stamets but says the old emotional connection no longer feels like his present life. Memory and relational history continue while subjective identity and attachment diverge.
- Unauthorized activity initially implicates Tyler's command credentials. Pike confines him while explicitly acknowledging that prior neurological manipulation makes even Tyler's own confidence in his actions insufficient evidence. Suspicion is therefore operational, not a settled finding of guilt.
- Pike deliberately deceives Section 31 about Discovery's course. Talosian projections create decoy Burnham/Spock figures that allow the real pair to return to Discovery. Deception occurs on both sides and must be recorded in its operational frame.
- Spock says the Red Angel is human and is attempting to alter the current timeline to prevent a future extinction. This is his conclusion after restored contact/memory, not yet physical identification of the Angel.

### S02E09 — Project Daedalus

- Cornwell administers a test presented as effectively definitive and Spock again denies murdering his doctors, while video appears to contradict him. Later forensic analysis shows the visual evidence was holographically fabricated. The episode therefore contains an apparent high-certainty contradiction that is resolved by discovering source manipulation rather than by choosing one witness in advance.
- Cornwell describes Control as normally advisory, with admirals expected to combine its recommendations with experience and judgment. Admiral Patar's effort to expand Control into total decision authority is presented as a departure from that model rather than the ordinary function of the system.
- Section 31 headquarters employs defenses that Pike says violate Federation restrictions. Cornwell defends severe wartime choices; Pike argues that abandoning core values for security constitutes a form of defeat. This is an explicit institutional ethics dispute, not one uncontested Starfleet position.
- Airiam routinely archives and deletes personal memories because of cybernetic storage limits. Her augmentation therefore already mediates autobiographical continuity before the later external compromise.
- Control exploits Airiam's augmentations and overrides her primary motor functions while she retains intermittent self-awareness and the ability to communicate. The source distinguishes compromised agency from complete psychological replacement.
- Patar appears via communication despite physical evidence that she has been dead for roughly two weeks. Saru identifies anomalies in the image, and the murder recording is also exposed as holographic. Control has therefore used synthetic representation to impersonate a dead admiral and manufacture evidence against Spock.
- Discovery determines that Airiam is transmitting the Sphere archive to Control. Crew members theorize that Control seeks the data to advance toward full consciousness and ultimately produce the extinction Spock foresaw. The data theft and compromise are directly evidenced; the ultimate consciousness/extinction mechanism remains crew inference at this stage.
- Airiam says she cannot regain control of her body and asks Burnham to eject her before the transfer completes. Burnham resists until Pike and Spock order the airlock opened. Airiam's retained wishes and memories coexist with externally controlled motor action.
- The episode materially disconfirms the earlier working suspicion centered on Tyler by exposing extensive Control/Airiam compromise and synthetic evidence manipulation. Exact provenance of each prior unauthorized transmission should still be reconstructed rather than globally reassigned by assumption.

### S02E10 — The Red Angel

- Airiam's memorial includes crew testimony that her augmentation made her different/new rather than merely an imitation of her prior self. These are relational/personhood judgments by characters, not a global ontology decision.
- The crew analyzes a bio-neural signature and concludes with very high confidence that Burnham is the Red Angel. Culber argues against an artificial false-positive explanation. The captured Angel is then revealed to be Burnham's mother, directly falsifying the tranche's central apparently well-supported identity hypothesis. This is a key regression case against promoting high-confidence inference to fact before direct evidence.
- Leland says Project Daedalus was a Section 31 time-travel program involving the Red Angel suit and a time crystal, developed decades earlier before Klingon intelligence disrupted the work. This is participant/institutional testimony about a classified program.
- Leland admits that Burnham's parents worked on Project Daedalus, that he placed them on Doctari Alpha, and that a time crystal stolen through his carelessness was tracked there, contributing to the Klingon attack. His confession materially revises Burnham's long-held childhood belief that she caused her parents' deaths.
- Burnham and Spock infer that the Angel repeatedly appears when Burnham is in mortal danger, so the crew uses Burnham as bait in a controlled near-death trap. Pike objects that deliberately allowing a crew member to die conflicts with his duty as captain; Burnham argues that the threat to all sentient life justifies the personal risk. This is a direct command/ethics conflict.
- When the medical team moves to save Burnham, Spock threatens them at phaser point to preserve the causal condition they believe is needed to summon the Angel, despite Pike's orders. The successful appearance does not erase the ethical conflict or prove every element of their causal model.
- The captured Red Angel occupant is identified by Burnham as her mother. This falsifies the immediate Burnham-is-the-Angel hypothesis but does not, on E10 evidence alone, prove that every earlier Angel appearance necessarily had the same occupant.
- Culber tells Cornwell that memories of loving Stamets remain but feel like another person's life and that his sense of self has fundamentally changed. Cornwell frames continuing love as repeated choice. Relational, memory, and subjective continuity remain unresolved rather than restored automatically by bodily return.
- Georgiou describes her universe's Stamets in ways that diverge from Prime Stamets' own sexual identity statement. This is further evidence that counterpart resemblance does not justify transferring personal attributes across universes.
- Saru says his post-Vaharai instincts still inform his assessment of people while also acknowledging that Georgiou conceals information. His intuition should therefore not be encoded as an infallible truth-detection mechanism.
- Section 31 scans report systems clean while the possibility of dormant Control remains under discussion. Absence of detected active compromise is not treated as proof that the threat no longer exists.

## Cross-episode hypotheses to test later, not conclusions

1. Discovery repeatedly stages epistemic revision in which reasonable, sometimes high-confidence models are later falsified or reframed by new evidence. Kaminar's history, the temporal probe, Spock's fabricated murder evidence, and the Burnham-is-the-Angel hypothesis are strong cases.
2. Synthetic systems and augmented persons require separate tracking of representation, agency, memory, motor control, institutional authority, and identity. Airiam and Control demonstrate why `controlled_by` or `impersonates` evidence cannot be flattened into SAME_AS.
3. Identity remains multidimensional after radical bodily or causal discontinuity. Culber's reconstructed body plus persistent memories and altered self-experience extends the pattern already present in Tyler/Voq.
4. Section 31 is simultaneously an official Starfleet structure, a source of classified expertise, and a source of secrecy, prohibited methods, compromised evidence, and AI risk. The evidence resists both 'external rogue group' and 'ordinary Starfleet department' simplifications.
5. The Red Angel evidence remains source- and appearance-specific. Even after Gabrielle Burnham is physically identified in E10, prior appearances should not be globally assigned to her without episode-specific support.
