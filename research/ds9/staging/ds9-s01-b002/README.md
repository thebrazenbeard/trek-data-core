## Source-relative evidence notes

`ql-e01` — A Federation runabout returns from the Gamma Quadrant carrying Vash, who says she has spent more than two years there and did not use the newly known wormhole to arrive there originally.

`ql-e02` — Vash repeatedly declines to explain how she originally reached the Gamma Quadrant, describing it as a personal matter.

`ql-e03` — Q appears on the station and claims an existing relationship/partnership with Vash; Vash rejects continuing that relationship and repeatedly asks him to leave her life.

`ql-e04` — Station systems experience broad power drains associated with increasing graviton activity. Characters initially suspect Q because of his known capabilities and presence.

`ql-e05` — Q denies responsibility for the station's worsening graviton/power problem. At that point the denial is testimony only and does not independently establish innocence.

`ql-e06` — Diagnostic work links the power drain to the Promenade and then to one object among Vash's auctioned Gamma Quadrant artifacts.

`ql-e07` — The object exhibits a massive graviton buildup and is treated as an embryonic life-form. After it is transported away from the station, graviton levels return to normal and the station can be restored to position.

`ql-e08` — Quark and Vash negotiate profit from Gamma Quadrant artifacts; Vash openly states she chooses profit over scientific access when forced to choose.

`ql-e09` — Vash initially says she intends to return to Earth and ordinary archaeological life, but by the end she asks Quark for the quickest route to another archaeological site after Q prompts her with the prospect of further exploration.

## Candidate interpretive assertions

`ql-a01` — **PROVISIONAL / supported by ql-e04, ql-e06, ql-e07:** The immediate station-threatening graviton event is supported as originating from the transported Gamma Quadrant organism/artifact rather than from Q's direct action.

Counterevidence/limit: Q's earlier denial is not itself reliable proof; the stronger support is the observed localization and resolution after removal of the object.

`ql-a02` — **PROVISIONAL / supported by ql-e03:** Q and Vash have sharply conflicting agency claims about their relationship. Q frames continued association as his prerogative; Vash frames separation as her decision.

`ql-a03` — **PROVISIONAL / supported by ql-e08, ql-e09:** Vash is depicted as balancing scholarly identity, commercial acquisition, and appetite for exploration rather than fitting a simple science-versus-profit binary.

`ql-a04` — **PROVISIONAL / epistemic:** The episode explicitly demonstrates why a plausible suspect with extraordinary capabilities should not be treated as the cause of an anomaly solely because the suspect is present and capable.

No global decision is made here about whether `Jadzia Dax`, `Curzon Dax`, and the `Dax symbiont` are one entity, multiple entities, or entities related along several continuity dimensions.

## Source-relative evidence notes

`dax-e01` — Klaestron officials seek extradition of Jadzia Dax for treason and murder allegedly committed by Curzon Dax about thirty years earlier.

`dax-e02` — Sisko explicitly argues that Curzon Dax and Jadzia Dax are different persons because the host is different; the extradition side argues continuity through the surviving symbiont.

`dax-e03` — A Trill expert testifies that a symbiont carries memories from previous hosts and that a later host can remember acts committed in an earlier joining.

`dax-e04` — The same expert describes joining as a total sharing/blending in which neither host nor symbiont is suppressed, and agrees that each new host produces a new personality/new person from that standpoint.

`dax-e05` — Bashir testifies that Curzon and Jadzia differ biologically and neurologically in several host-level respects, while also acknowledging no demonstrated change in the symbiont itself from the available evidence.

`dax-e06` — Host and symbiont are described as biologically interdependent after joining; separating them is not presented as a simple way to assign punishment to only one component.

`dax-e07` — Jadzia states that she retains Curzon's shame over his affair with Enina and treats Curzon's promise of silence as binding enough that she is willing to risk extradition and death rather than reveal the affair.

`dax-e08` — Jadzia also distinguishes herself from Curzon in conversation, including telling Sisko that some obligations and behavior belonged to Curzon.

`dax-e09` — The Arbiter explicitly notes difficulty because Jadzia appears, in relevant senses, to be both much older than the host body and approximately the age of the present host.

`dax-e10` — Enina supplies an alibi showing Curzon could not have transmitted the information used to betray General Tandro; she further states that the General himself attempted betrayal and was killed by the rebels.

`dax-e11` — The extradition hearing ends because the factual accusation against Curzon is undermined, not because the hearing produces a final legal ruling on Trill cross-host criminal responsibility.

## Candidate interpretive assertions

`dax-a01` — **PROVISIONAL / supported by dax-e02 through dax-e09:** The source resists a binary same-person/different-person model. Biological, psychological, memory, social, moral, and legal continuity point in partially different directions.

`dax-a02` — **PROVISIONAL / UNRESOLVED:** The episode does not establish a governing legal answer to whether a later joined Trill is criminally liable for an antecedent host's act.

Critical counterevidence: the hearing is terminated by new evidence about the alleged crime before the identity/liability question is adjudicated.

`dax-a03` — **PROVISIONAL:** Jadzia's conduct demonstrates inherited memory and felt obligation without proving that every antecedent obligation is legally or morally binding on the present joined person.

`dax-a04` — **PROVISIONAL / identity calibration:** Any later reconciliation should preserve at least separate host occurrence, symbiont continuity, joined-person occurrence, memory continuity, and legal-responsibility dimensions rather than collapsing all into one `SAME_AS` relation.

The `Vantika-pattern occupying Bashir` candidate remains local to this work and should not be silently merged with either Vantika's corpse or Bashir.

## Source-relative evidence notes

`pass-e01` — Kajada repeatedly asserts that Vantika has survived apparent death before and requests unusually extensive confirmation that his body is truly dead.

`pass-e02` — Autopsy, fingerprints, retinal evidence, cerebral scans, and later DNA comparison identify the corpse as Rao Vantika and find no clone substitution.

`pass-e03` — Kajada's survival theory is initially treated by others as fixation or implausible suspicion, but subsequent computer sabotage resembles methods she attributes to Vantika.

`pass-e04` — Records among Vantika's belongings include simulations concerning storage of neural energy patterns in different brain regions.

`pass-e05` — Dax and Bashir discuss the possibility that a second neural pattern could occupy unused neural capacity without the host's awareness; this begins as hypothesis, not established fact.

`pass-e06` — Dax later identifies a plausible transfer mechanism involving a small device associated with Vantika's fingernail and bio-coded electrical transfer through glial cells.

`pass-e07` — Bashir's body subsequently leads the attempted deuridium theft, self-identifies as Rao Vantika, speaks of Bashir's body as something he is using, and demonstrates knowledge/intent consistent with the theft plan.

`pass-e08` — Dax designs an electromagnetic disruption using Vantika's modeled neural pattern; after the pulse, Bashir re-emerges and lowers the shields.

`pass-e09` — A transporter procedure isolates cells/patterns matching Vantika's neural signature from Bashir into a containment field. Subsequent scan reads only human neural patterns in Bashir.

`pass-e10` — Bashir reports little or no memory of the period of control and experiences humiliation despite other characters explicitly distinguishing his agency from Vantika's.

## Candidate interpretive assertions

`pass-a01` — **PROVISIONAL / supported by pass-e04 through pass-e09:** The episode depicts transferable neural-pattern information capable of producing Vantika-consistent agency through Bashir's body.

`pass-a02` — **PROVISIONAL / terminology limit:** Characters call the transferred pattern Vantika's consciousness, but the source support is strongest for transfer of a neural pattern plus Vantika-consistent memory/agency. A metaphysical claim about consciousness should not be silently upgraded beyond that evidence.

`pass-a03` — **PROVISIONAL / identity:** Bashir's body during the occupancy should not be modeled as ordinary Bashir behavior, nor should the occupying pattern automatically be equated with Vantika's dead biological body.

`pass-a04` — **PROVISIONAL / agency:** Bashir's lack of remembered participation and the demonstrated foreign-pattern mechanism are counterevidence to assigning him ordinary personal responsibility for the hijacking acts performed through his body.

## Source-relative evidence notes

`mah-e01` — The Wadi arrive as an early Gamma Quadrant first-contact delegation and immediately prioritize games over Starfleet reception protocol.

`mah-e02` — Quark cheats the Wadi at dabo. The Wadi then introduce Chula and require Quark to play.

`mah-e03` — Sisko, Kira, Dax, and Bashir disappear from the station without normal departure records while Quark's Chula board simultaneously contains four player pieces.

`mah-e04` — The four officers experience a shared maze-like environment with physical-seeming obstacles, injury, toxic-seeming exposure, spatial separation, and interactive Wadi figures.

`mah-e05` — The officers themselves debate possible frames: dream, behavioral test, maze, and game. Their uncertainty is explicit.

`mah-e06` — Odo detects an unusual persistent bipolar energy flux on the Wadi ship; a transporter-like mechanism is suggested but not established.

`mah-e07` — Quark's choices in the board game correspond to changes in the officers' experienced environment, including path selection, increased peril, and apparent loss/sacrifice of players.

`mah-e08` — Quark is eventually forced to confront a choice framed as sacrificing one player so two may live. He breaks down and refuses to make the choice; the Wadi instead randomize the selection.

`mah-e09` — Sisko refuses to abandon injured Dax; Kira also refuses a direct order to leave, while noting that she is not subject to Starfleet court-martial authority.

`mah-e10` — After apparent fatal/loss conditions in the game, the officers return. Falow states that they were never in real danger because it was only a game.

`mah-e11` — The source does not provide a definitive technical explanation for where the officers physically were, how the game representations were linked to them, or whether the experienced injuries/perils were simulation, transport, matter conversion, remote projection, or another mechanism.

## Candidate interpretive assertions

`mah-a01` — **PROVISIONAL / UNRESOLVED FRAME:** The Chula sequence must not be flattened into objective ordinary-space events, but the text also does not justify reducing it to a conventional holodeck simulation.

`mah-a02` — **PROVISIONAL / supported by mah-e03, mah-e06, mah-e07, mah-e10:** There is a causal or representational link between Quark's board-game state and the officers' experienced environment, but the mechanism remains unresolved.

`mah-a03` — **PROVISIONAL / counterevidence:** Falow's claim that no one was in real danger is testimony after the fact. It is evidence about Wadi framing, not a complete technical account of the episode's reality layer.

`mah-a04` — **PROVISIONAL / ethics:** Quark's emotional collapse under the forced-sacrifice decision is evidence of a limit on his willingness to convert other persons' apparent lives into gambling stakes, despite his earlier cheating and profit-seeking behavior.

## Source-relative evidence notes

`nagus-e01` — Zek arrives, convenes Ferengi business leaders, and presents Gamma Quadrant expansion as a major commercial opportunity.

`nagus-e02` — Zek unexpectedly names Quark as his successor, and Quark begins exercising the office of Grand Nagus.

`nagus-e03` — Zek apparently dies. Ferengi participants and Quark treat the death as real; later events show this interpretation was mistaken.

`nagus-e04` — Quark becomes the target of attempted murder and experiences rapidly changing alliances from Ferengi who seek commercial advantage under the new office-holder.

`nagus-e05` — Quark distributes opportunities and begins adapting to the power structure rather than merely trying to escape it.

`nagus-e06` — Rom and Krax later cooperate in an attempt to kill Quark. Their motives and succession interests are depicted through their actions and dialogue.

`nagus-e07` — Zek reappears alive and explains that he entered a Dolbargy sleeping trance. He frames the apparent death and succession crisis as a test of Krax's readiness.

`nagus-e08` — Zek tells Krax that effective power should be accumulated indirectly and that Quark's bar could have been used as an information-gathering position while another person visibly held the sceptre.

`nagus-e09` — Nog is removed from the Federation school after Zek condemns his attendance; Jake and Nog continue their friendship despite both fathers' concerns.

`nagus-e10` — Jake secretly teaches Nog to read. Sisko discovers them and, rather than forcibly separating the boys, affirms Jake and allows the friendship to continue.

`nagus-e11` — Several Rules of Acquisition are spoken as Ferengi cultural/business maxims. These utterances establish character/cultural invocation of the rules in this source; they do not by themselves constitute an exhaustive external Ferengi legal code.

## Candidate interpretive assertions

`nagus-a01` — **PROVISIONAL / supported by nagus-e03, nagus-e07:** Zek's apparent death is a mistaken in-story interpretation deliberately engineered by Zek, not an unresolved resurrection event.

`nagus-a02` — **PROVISIONAL / institutional:** The Grand Nagus role is depicted as a political-economic office whose authority depends not only on formal title but on networks of loyalty, leverage, information, and commercial allocation.

`nagus-a03` — **PROVISIONAL / family-culture:** Nog's schooling conflict depicts real tension among Ferengi cultural expectations, parental authority, literacy/education, and cross-cultural friendship without establishing that one side's stated values exhaust Ferengi culture as a whole.

`nagus-a04` — **PROVISIONAL / character agency:** Sisko's handling of Jake and Nog shifts from concern about Nog as a negative influence toward acceptance after he directly observes Jake teaching Nog to read.

# Cross-work neutral hypotheses from batch 002

These are research hypotheses only. They are not consensus facts and require support, neutral cases, and disconfirming evidence across later DS9 works.

## H1 — DS9 repeatedly makes epistemic procedure part of the plot

Support in this batch:

- Q-Less: suspicion of Q is displaced by diagnostic localization of the actual graviton source.
- Dax: testimony, medical evidence, legal definitions, memory continuity, and new alibi evidence pull in different directions.
- The Passenger: an initially implausible survival claim gains support through converging technical evidence.
- Move Along Home: participants explicitly debate the frame of their experience and never receive a complete mechanism.
- The Nagus: apparent death is later revealed as staged trance.

Counterexample requirement: later episodes in which apparently strange evidence is straightforwardly reliable should remain ordinary cases rather than being forced into this motif.

## H2 — Identity questions in DS9 often require more than biological continuity

Support:

- Dax: present host, antecedent host, symbiont, memories, promises, criminal liability, and friendship do not align into one binary identity answer.
- The Passenger: biological Bashir persists while foreign neural patterns produce Vantika-consistent agency.

Neutral cases:

- Q-Less and The Nagus do not require new identity ontology merely because Q appears or Zek appears dead.

This hypothesis should remain lazy and activate continuity dimensions only when source evidence requires them.

## H3 — Institutional rules and personal agency are repeatedly negotiated rather than mechanically applied

Support:

- Dax: extradition rules collide with Bajoran jurisdiction and unresolved Trill personhood.
- The Passenger: Odo/Primmin jurisdiction is negotiated rather than assumed.
- Move Along Home: Starfleet first-contact expectations, Kira's non-Starfleet status, and Quark's commercial choices produce overlapping authority problems.
- The Nagus: formal title and practical power diverge.

Counterevidence requirement: later straightforward chains of command must be coded as such and not reinterpreted as hidden conflict.
