## The Forsaken

Local entities: Sisko, Bashir, Odo, Lwaxana Troi, Dax, O'Brien, ambassador delegation, station computer, Gamma-origin computer-associated entity.

Evidence:
F01 Federation ambassadors arrive for a wormhole fact-finding visit; Bashir is assigned liaison duty.
F02 Lwaxana reports theft of her latinum hair brooch; Odo investigates.
F03 Odo and Lwaxana become trapped in a turbolift during cascading station failures.
F04 Odo's inability to regenerate becomes dangerous while trapped; Lwaxana provides a container and privacy.
F05 Station computer failures spread in a pattern ordinary diagnostics do not initially explain.
F06 Dax/O'Brien identify an anomalous informational/energy presence associated with the computer after a Gamma Quadrant return.
F07 Dax treats the anomaly as potentially life-like/nonbiological but explicitly notes evidence of sentience is lacking.
F08 Attempts to isolate/remove the anomaly trigger defensive or disruptive station responses, supporting agency-like behavior without proving reflective sentience.
F09 The anomaly is ultimately transferred into a contained computer environment rather than destroyed.
F10 Counterevidence: apparent preference, attachment, or self-preserving reactions fit several mechanisms; no language, self-report, or independent reflective-consciousness test is established.

Assertions:
FA01 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The Gamma-origin computer-associated entity is a local nonbiological-life candidate; sentience remains UNRESOLVED. Evidence F05-F10.
FA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Odo's regeneration requirement creates a material dependency capable of placing him in acute danger. Evidence F03-F04.
FA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Lwaxana supplies direct evidence of empathy/privacy-respect toward Odo, complicating a purely intrusive characterization. Evidence F03-F04.

## Dramatis Personae

Local entities: Sisko, Kira, Dax, O'Brien, Bashir, Odo, Hon'Tihl, Toh'Kaht, Saltah'na telepathic matrix, Valerian ship.

Evidence:
D01 A Klingon survivor beams aboard from the exploding Toh'Kaht, says “victory,” and dies; cause is initially unknown.
D02 Recovered Klingon mission records describe energy spheres containing a telepathic archive of an ancient Saltah'na power struggle.
D03 Sisko, Kira, Dax, O'Brien, and Bashir develop marked behavioral/loyalty changes after exposure; Odo notices parallels with the Klingon mutiny.
D04 The affected crew divide into competing factions and reinterpret ordinary station disputes as existential power struggles.
D05 Bashir identifies altered membrane permeability in Klingon brain cells and treats a telepathic-field explanation as physiologically plausible.
D06 Odo hypothesizes that his non-humanoid neurophysiology prevented the matrix from affecting him.
D07 Counterevidence: Bashir initially believes himself unaffected, but his opportunistic behavior makes self-assessment under influence unreliable.
D08 Odo/Bashir construct resonance interference calibrated to the telepathic field.
D09 Activating the interference visibly expels the field from affected crew; behavior returns to baseline.
D10 With no host/container, the matrix disperses into space; crew report return to normal and retain little explanatory access to affected actions.

Assertions:
DA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Convergent evidence supports the telepathic matrix as cause of altered behavior. D02-D10.
DA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Behavioral responsibility is explicitly mitigated because affected persons act under externally imposed influence. D03-D10.
DA03 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Odo's immunity is associated with non-humanoid neurophysiology but not isolated as the only possible causal factor. D06-D09.

## Duet

Local entities: Kira, Sisko, Odo, Bashir, Dax, Aamin Marritza, recorded Gul Darhe'el, Kainon, Gallitep survivors.

Evidence:
U01 A Cardassian passenger presents with Kalla-Nohra, which Kira associates with survivors of Gallitep.
U02 The detainee identifies himself as Aamin Marritza and claims to have been a filing clerk at Gallitep.
U03 He alternately minimizes/denies atrocities, then offers increasingly provocative statements apparently designed to produce hatred and punishment.
U04 Archival imagery initially appears to identify the detainee as Gul Darhe'el while separately showing Marritza, creating an identity conflict.
U05 Cardassian records report Darhe'el died six years earlier; Kira initially treats that record as potentially forged rather than dispositive.
U06 Bashir discovers long-term dermal regenerative treatment associated with cosmetic alteration.
U07 Under questioning, the Darhe'el persona collapses; the detainee acknowledges being Marritza and describes hearing prisoners' screams while unable to intervene.
U08 Marritza states he altered his appearance and sought capture so a public trial would force Cardassia to acknowledge collective guilt.
U09 Kira refuses to sustain the false identity or facilitate Marritza's desired execution, distinguishing remorse from Darhe'el's crimes.
U10 Kainon kills Marritza solely because he is Cardassian; Kira explicitly rejects species identity as sufficient reason.

Assertions:
UA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The detainee is source-supported as Marritza impersonating Darhe'el for a self-sacrificial political purpose. U02-U08.
UA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — The episode separates witnessed/collective guilt, personal moral responsibility, and legal responsibility for specific atrocities. U07-U10.
UA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Kira's identity conclusion changes because medical, archival, and behavioral evidence disconfirm her initial position. U01-U09.
UA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — The final murder is direct counterevidence to indiscriminate species-based moral attribution. U10.

## In the Hands of the Prophets

Local entities: Sisko, Kira, Keiko, O'Brien, Vedek Winn, Vedek Bareil, Neela, Aquino, Prophets-as-religious-referent, wormhole-entities-as-scientific-referent.

Evidence:
P01 Keiko teaches the wormhole as a stable/artificial phenomenon and distinguishes scientific description from Bajoran spiritual belief.
P02 Winn identifies the wormhole as the Celestial Temple and its inhabitants as Prophets; this scene establishes religious interpretation, not objective metaphysical truth by itself.
P03 Winn removes/supports removal of Bajoran children and demands curriculum alteration; Keiko refuses to omit scientific discussion.
P04 Kira initially understands Winn as a true believer and experiences the dispute partly through her own religious commitments.
P05 School conflict escalates into broader political mobilization and bombing while Sisko argues for coexistence of philosophies rather than suppression of religion.
P06 O'Brien traces missing tools, Aquino's death, security bypasses, and hidden computer modifications toward Neela.
P07 Neela meets Winn privately; when Neela fears capture/execution, Winn frames sacrifice as demanded by the Prophets and promises eternal reward.
P08 Neela disables/evades weapon-detection systems and attempts to assassinate Bareil.
P09 After capture, Neela insists the Prophets spoke and that she answered their call; this establishes professed motive, not that the Prophets issued the command.
P10 Kira accuses Winn of orchestrating the school crisis, protests, bombing, and assassination setup, but Neela refuses to implicate Winn; Sisko says they cannot prove Winn's involvement.

Assertions:
PA01 PROPOSED/CONTESTED — The school dispute is simultaneously scientific/educational, religious, and political. P01-P05.
PA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Neela attempts to assassinate Bareil and acts from professed religious conviction. P06-P09.
PA03 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Winn's orchestration is strongly implied but unproven within the episode. P07-P10.
PA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Neela's prophetic-instruction claim remains testimony and is not sufficient evidence that the Prophets objectively commanded violence. P09.

## The Homecoming

Local entities: Sisko, Kira, O'Brien, Li Nalas, Jaro, Quark, Odo, Cardassian camp authority, the Circle, Bajoran prisoners.

Evidence:
H01 A Bajoran earring smuggled from Cardassia IV carries residue matched to Li Nalas's records, supporting Kira's conclusion he may still be alive.
H02 Bajoran ministers decline intervention; Sisko authorizes Kira's runabout mission and assigns O'Brien, linking rescue to political stability and prisoner treatment.
H03 Kira/O'Brien infiltrate Hutet labour camp and identify multiple Bajoran prisoners including Li.
H04 Several prisoners elect to remain temporarily to cover escape; Kira initially waits but leaves when Cardassian warships make continued extraction untenable.
H05 Dukat later claims the camp violated Cardassian directives and announces release of remaining prisoners; motive for accommodation remains unresolved.
H06 Li tells Sisko his celebrated killing of Gul Zarale was a chaotic encounter with a nearly unarmed Cardassian, later transformed by resistance mythology.
H07 Li says later resistance victories were attributed to him regardless of actual role and that his reputation sustained prisoners.
H08 Sisko argues Bajor needs Li as a symbol even if the popular legend is not biographically accurate.
H09 The Circle brands Quark and anti-non-Bajoran hostility affects Jake's social life, showing exclusionary politics producing coercion/discrimination on DS9.
H10 Jaro's government creates the title Navarch, installs Li as liaison to DS9, and recalls Kira without Sisko's consultation.

Assertions:
HA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Li's public heroic identity is explicitly distinguished from his autobiographical account; the legend has social causal power without being literal biography. H06-H08.
HA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Sisko knowingly supports political use of Li's symbolic identity after learning the legend is embellished. H06-H08.
HA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — The Circle's exclusionary politics have moved from rhetoric into intimidation/discriminatory effects. H09.
HA04 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Cardassia's explanation for the surviving labour camp and sudden cooperation is testimony whose motive remains unresolved. H03-H05.
