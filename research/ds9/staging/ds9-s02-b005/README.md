## The Circle

Local entities: Sisko, Kira, Li Nalas, Odo, Quark, Bareil, Winn, Jaro, General Krim, Circle organization, Kressari intermediaries, Cardassian suppliers.

Evidence:
C01 Kira is removed from the DS9 liaison post after returning Li Nalas; Jaro describes the reassignment as a promotion while Sisko treats the lack of consultation and rationale as suspicious.
C02 Odo tells Kira that the Circle has enough weapons and supplies to support an army and identifies Kressari delivery activity as an investigative lead.
C03 Kira visits Bareil's monastery and undergoes an Orb experience; Bareil says Kira appeared in an earlier vision of his, while Kira says he did not appear in hers.
C04 Counterframe: Orb experiences are recorded as vision/experience testimony; they are not converted here into objective predictions or causal commands.
C05 Kira learns that Jaro is the Circle's political center and that he deliberately moved Li away from Bajor's capital because Li's symbolic popularity threatened the coup.
C06 Jaro states his goal is a stronger, independent Bajor and treats Federation presence as another form of subordination; Kira rejects his coup method despite her own past criticism of the provisional government.
C07 Kira is abducted and interrogated by Circle forces; Sisko's team rescues her.
C08 A Cardassian-origin weapon with a Gul thumb scan supplies physical/documentary evidence that Cardassians are secretly supplying the Circle through Kressari intermediaries; the Circle itself is portrayed as unaware of the ultimate supplier.
C09 Jaro seeks Winn's public religious support; Winn treats support as politically negotiable in relation to the forthcoming Kai selection.
C10 Starfleet orders evacuation because the conflict is classified as internal to Bajor despite Cardassian support; Sisko concludes some personnel will remain behind long enough to expose that support.

Assertions:
CA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jaro/Circle political goals are not reducible to Cardassian puppetry; the source supports a genuine Bajoran nationalist coup that is covertly exploited by Cardassia. C05-C10.
CA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Winn's religious office and Kai ambitions are directly entangled with political bargaining in this work. C09.
CA03 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Orb experiences inform Kira/Bareil decisions but their metaphysical status and predictive force remain source-relative and unresolved. C03-C04.
CA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Kira distinguishes criticism of existing government from support for violent seizure of power. C05-C07.

## The Siege

Local entities: Sisko, Kira, Dax, O'Brien, Bashir, Odo, Li Nalas, Jaro, General Krim, Colonel Day, Bajoran assault force, Cardassian/Kressari supply evidence.

Evidence:
S01 Starfleet evacuation is ordered, but Sisko and volunteers remain under the practical cover of an incomplete evacuation/inventory to delay the Circle and expose Cardassian involvement.
S02 Bajoran and non-Bajoran civilians are separated by evacuation risk; Li publicly urges Bajorans not to abandon their own world.
S03 Kira and Dax use an old Bajoran resistance raider to carry the Cardassian-supply evidence toward the Chamber of Ministers.
S04 The station defenders deliberately sabotage systems, hide, capture patrols, and use nonlethal/deadly force constraints while presenting the occupation force with evidence of Cardassian involvement.
S05 General Krim repeatedly treats Sisko's conduct as militarily rational and remains more cautious than Colonel Day's ideological certainty.
S06 Kira and Dax are attacked en route; Kira is injured, reaches Bareil's monastery, and then enters the Chamber disguised with Dax/Bareil support.
S07 Kira presents the Kressari manifest/thumb-scan evidence before the Chamber; Jaro calls the accusations wild but agrees to inspection when challenged.
S08 Krim concludes the Circle is broken when the Chamber begins examining the Cardassian evidence and returns control of DS9 to Starfleet.
S09 Li Nalas is killed during the station conflict while protecting/participating with Sisko's group.
S10 After Li's death, Sisko deliberately preserves the heroic public account rather than correcting it with Li's private autobiographical confession from `The Homecoming`.

Assertions:
SA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The coup collapses because material provenance evidence changes the political/military coalition, not because Jaro voluntarily abandons his ideology. S03-S08.
SA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Sisko knowingly participates in maintaining Li Nalas's public legend after Li's death. S09-S10.
SA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — The station defense is constrained warfare/political signaling rather than an attempt to annihilate Bajoran forces. S01-S05.
SA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — General Krim's conduct supplies counterevidence to treating all Circle-aligned military personnel as ideologically identical. S05-S08.

## Invasive Procedures

Local entities: Jadzia Dax, Dax symbiont, Verad, Verad Dax, Mareel, T'Kar, Yeto, Sisko, Bashir, Kira, Odo, Quark.

Evidence:
I01 During a station evacuation for a plasma storm, Verad's group enters under a false rescue pretext, neutralizes security, captures the skeleton crew, and places Odo in stasis.
I02 Verad says he was rejected by the symbiosis board, regards an unjoined life as condemned to mediocrity, and has selected Dax because he believes it is his best match.
I03 Jadzia states removal of the symbiont will kill her within hours; Bashir also warns an improper joining can harm both host and symbiont.
I04 Jadzia agrees to surgery under coercive threat to others, not free consent.
I05 After joining, Verad identifies himself as Verad Dax, recalls previous Dax-host memories, recognizes Sisko through Dax's history, and exhibits changed posture, confidence, priorities, and relationship behavior.
I06 Sisko explicitly distinguishes Jadzia's memories surviving within Verad Dax from Jadzia herself remaining alive; Verad Dax acknowledges that Jadzia will die if the symbiont is not returned.
I07 Mareel initially insists Verad remains essentially the same, then observes behavioral/relational changes and ultimately helps the station crew stop him.
I08 Verad Dax refuses voluntary return of the symbiont, prioritizing joined identity/knowledge over Jadzia's separate survival.
I09 After Dax is removed, Verad reports emptiness and loss of the acquired knowledge/confidence/memories; the source depicts a marked reversion toward his prior self-presentation.
I10 After rejoining Jadzia, Jadzia remembers what Verad Dax thought/did and says Verad will remain with her as memory/experience.

Assertions:
IA01 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — `Verad`, `Verad Dax`, `Jadzia Dax`, and `Dax symbiont` must remain distinct local entities/identity states; the work supplies asymmetric memory, behavioral, causal, and bodily continuity rather than a binary sameness answer. I02-I10.
IA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jadzia's apparent agreement to surgery is coerced and does not constitute free consent to symbiont removal. I03-I04.
IA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Joining materially changes Verad's memory access, behavior, social relationships, and self-identification. I05-I09.
IA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jadzia after restoration has memory continuity with the Verad Dax interval even though her body was not the active host during that interval. I10.
IA05 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The source uses language of rebirth/different person and blending, but does not itself provide a single governed rule for legal/metaphysical person identity across joining. I05-I10.

## Cardassians

Local entities: Rugal, Proka Migdal, Rugal's Bajoran foster mother/family, Kotran Pa'Dar, Garak, Bashir, Sisko, Dukat, Jomat Luson, Cardassian war orphans.

Evidence:
K01 Rugal, a Cardassian child raised by Bajorans, attacks Garak; Dukat immediately frames the incident as proof that Bajorans raise Cardassian orphans to hate their own people.
K02 Rugal's foster father says the family told Rugal the truth about the occupation, denies abuse, says they love him as their own child, and identifies him socially as Bajoran.
K03 Rugal says he wants to return home to Bajor, hates being Cardassian, and describes his Bajoran parents as loving and non-abusive.
K04 O'Brien directly challenges Rugal's totalizing hatred of Cardassians and distinguishes individuals from racial categories.
K05 Pa'Dar identifies Rugal as his lost biological son, describes Cardassian family obligations, and expresses grief/shame over losing him; Rugal rejects Pa'Dar as father and refuses Cardassian identity/homecoming.
K06 Sisko accepts an arbitrator role because the custody dispute crosses Bajoran/Cardassian law and both parties accept a non-Bajoran/non-Cardassian decision-maker.
K07 Garak/Bashir investigate missing adoption records rather than treating Dukat's public orphan concern as sufficient explanation.
K08 Jomat Luson recalls Rugal was delivered to the resettlement center by a Cardassian officer attached to Terok Nor who knew the child's identity.
K09 Evidence ties the contemporary exposure to Dukat's command of Terok Nor and Pa'Dar's political vulnerability; Garak infers a deliberate old conspiracy designed to humiliate Pa'Dar, a political enemy involved in ending the occupation.
K10 Sisko awards custody to Pa'Dar despite Rugal's expressed wish to remain on Bajor, reasoning that Rugal has been made a victim of the conspiracy and needs healing; Pa'Dar later says the exposure gives him leverage to neutralize Dukat politically.

Assertions:
KA01 PROPOSED/CONTESTED — Rugal's identity has conflicting biological, cultural, familial, legal, and self-identification dimensions; the episode does not make them coextensive. K01-K10.
KA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — The foster parents' alleged abuse is not corroborated by Rugal; direct source testimony instead supports love/non-abuse while also supporting intense anti-Cardassian socialization. K02-K04.
KA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Sisko's custody ruling overrides Rugal's stated preference and privileges biological/political repair considerations; this is an institutional decision, not proof of Rugal's “true” identity. K03-K10.
KA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Dukat's public humanitarian framing is undermined by provenance evidence tying Rugal's placement to a political conspiracy under his command. K07-K10.

## Melora

Local entities: Melora Pazlar, Bashir, Dax, Sisko, O'Brien, Quark, Fallit Kot, Ashrock, Elaysian low-gravity physiology.

Evidence:
M01 Melora is the first Elaysian Starfleet officer presented in the source and requires ramps/anti-grav/low-gravity accommodation because DS9's Cardassian construction and standard gravity are inaccessible to her.
M02 Melora repeatedly rejects unnecessary assistance and objects when others treat accommodation as evidence of illness or incapacity; she explicitly distinguishes a “Melora problem” from problems created by the environment/other people.
M03 Sisko denies her request to take a runabout alone on ordinary command-risk grounds rather than accepting a medicalized assumption that she is incapable.
M04 Melora experiences a fall caused partly by station architecture and later acknowledges that total independence is neither possible nor required; Bashir argues station life is interdependent for everyone.
M05 Bashir develops a neurochemical treatment that could adapt Melora permanently to standard gravity and allow unsupported walking.
M06 The treatment would make low-gravity environments increasingly incompatible and could prevent Melora from meaningfully returning to her home-world environment.
M07 Melora initially pursues the treatment, experiences walking in standard gravity, then recognizes that the change would cost part of her Elaysian embodied identity and reverses the treatment before it becomes permanent.
M08 During Fallit Kot's hostage escape, Melora uses the low-gravity state strategically after gravity generators are disabled, contributing directly to stopping the hijacking.
M09 Counterevidence: the episode does not romanticize all dependence; Melora values mobility/autonomy and accepts accommodations when they preserve rather than erase her chosen way of living.
M10 Bashir accepts Melora's decision to stop the treatment rather than treating medical normalization as the superior outcome.

Assertions:
MA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The episode distinguishes impairment created by environment from illness intrinsic to Melora; accessibility barriers are material features of DS9's design. M01-M04.
MA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Medical ability to normalize Melora to standard gravity is not treated as an obligation; her refusal is an autonomy/identity decision. M05-M10.
MA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Melora's low-gravity embodiment is not merely a deficit; in the hostage sequence it becomes a situational capability. M08.
MA04 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The work raises continuity/self-concept consequences of permanent physiological adaptation without defining a universal threshold at which bodily change becomes identity change. M05-M10.

## Cross-work neutral coding / hypothesis tests

Hypothesis A — DS9 repeatedly treats institutional labels as imperfect proxies for lived identity.
Support: Rugal's biological/legal/cultural conflict; Verad versus Verad Dax; Kira's removal/reassignment despite functional role; Melora's disability classification versus self-concept.
Disconfirmation/limits: some institutional determinations are operationally necessary and source-supported; the pattern does not mean institutions are always wrong.
Status: PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis B — bodily transformation or occupancy changes agency relationships without automatically settling person identity.
Support: Verad Dax; Melora's proposed adaptation; Dax restoration.
Counterevidence: mechanisms and stakes differ radically, and no global predicate should collapse joining into medical adaptation.
Status: PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis C — provenance investigation repeatedly defeats politically useful surface narratives.
Support: Cardassian weapons behind the Circle; Rugal placement conspiracy; Quark's role enabling station takeover in `Invasive Procedures` is exposed by event sequence rather than his self-presentation.
Counterevidence: `The Siege` also relies on symbolic mythmaking around Li even after provenance is known, showing truth discovery does not automatically determine public narrative.
Status: PROVISIONAL.
