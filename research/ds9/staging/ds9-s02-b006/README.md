## Rules of Acquisition

Local entities: Quark, Pel, Pel's male-coded business presentation, Dax, Rom, Zek, Dosi negotiators, Karemma referent, Dominion referent.

Evidence:
R01 Pel demonstrates detailed knowledge of Ferengi commercial rules and repeatedly improves Quark's business strategy.
R02 Pel privately tells Dax that Ferengi women cannot leave home, wear clothes, or earn profit under the governing Ferengi social/legal order described in the work.
R03 Pel has adopted male-coded presentation, artificial lobes, and public status to participate in commerce; Dax preserves the secret.
R04 Pel states that commercial ambition and love for Quark both matter to her; neither motive exhausts the other.
R05 Pel's negotiating contribution helps Quark trace the Dosi through the Karemma toward the previously vague Dominion referent.
R06 Quark learns Pel's sex/presentation secret and first tries to force her departure, explicitly citing Ferengi law and his own reputational risk.
R07 Pel publicly reveals the secret to Zek rather than allowing permanent concealment to remain entirely on Quark's terms.
R08 Zek threatens imprisonment because Ferengi law forbids women participating in business and also threatens Quark for accepting female business advice.
R09 Quark prevents punishment by threatening Zek's reputation: Pel successfully represented Zek in business and exposure would show that success came through a woman.
R10 Pel leaves rather than accept the conventional Ferengi-wife role; Quark refuses her invitation to leave Ferengi society with her.

Assertions:
RA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Pel's commercial exclusion is institutionally imposed rather than evidence of incapacity; her performance directly disconfirms the Ferengi stereotype used to justify exclusion. R01-R09.
RA02 PROPOSED/CONTESTED — Pel's presentation should be represented as a work-local concealment/social-role strategy under coercive law; the episode's sex/gender language is preserved rather than retrofitted into a global identity category not established by the source. R02-R10.
RA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Quark simultaneously benefits from Pel's capability, protects her from punishment, and refuses to reject the gender order that excludes her. R05-R10.
RA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — The Dosi negotiation establishes the Karemma/Dominion as an emerging commercial/political discovery, not yet a fully known world-state structure to the characters. R05.

## Necessary Evil

Local entities: present Odo, occupation-era Odo, present Kira, occupation-era Kira, Quark, Rom, Vaatrik, Pallra Vaatrik, Ches'sarro, Dukat, Bajoran collaborators list.

Evidence:
N01 Present-day attempted murder of Quark and theft/search for an occupation-era list reopen Odo's first murder investigation.
N02 Odo's log/recollection frames the flashback as his remembered investigation under Cardassian occupation; reconstructed scenes remain evidence within that recollective/log frame.
N03 Dukat appoints Odo investigator partly because Bajorans regard him as less partisan than Cardassian justice mechanisms.
N04 Occupation-era Kira supplies a paid false alibi through Quark and initially denies involvement in Vaatrik's killing.
N05 Odo uncovers the false alibi but accepts Kira's alternative explanation that she was conducting underground sabotage at the time.
N06 Present investigation reveals Vaatrik belonged to a network of Bajoran collaborators connected to Dukat and explains the hidden list's political value.
N07 Kira finally tells Odo she was searching Vaatrik's quarters for the collaborators list when he interrupted her and that she killed him in the confrontation.
N08 Odo realizes his original conclusion cleared the actual killer; he also recognizes that Kira withheld the truth throughout their subsequent friendship.
N09 Kira asks whether Odo will ever trust her the same way; the source leaves the interpersonal consequence open rather than restoring trust by declaration.
N10 Counterevidence: Kira's Resistance affiliation and sabotage alibi were true even though they functioned to conceal the murder, illustrating that truthful subsidiary claims can support a false overall conclusion.

Assertions:
NA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Kira killed Vaatrik during an occupation-era underground operation and successfully concealed that fact from Odo until the present investigation. N04-N08.
NA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Odo's self-concept as an impartial investigator is challenged by discovering that his original evidence evaluation produced a false exoneration. N03-N09.
NA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — The episode demonstrates an epistemic distinction between true supporting details and a deceptive overall narrative. N04-N10.
NA04 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The future trust consequence between Odo and Kira remains unresolved at episode end. N08-N09.

## Second Sight

Local entities: Sisko, Fenna, Nidell, Seyetik, Dax, Odo, Halanan psychoprojective ability, Epsilon 119.

Evidence:
E01 Sisko repeatedly encounters Fenna, who appears/disappears without docking/transporter provenance and provides almost no autobiographical history.
E02 Sisko later meets Nidell, Seyetik's wife, who is physically identical to Fenna but denies having met Sisko.
E03 Odo's transport/docking investigation provides counterevidence to Fenna being an ordinary independently arrived traveler.
E04 Dax scans Fenna and finds no cellular structure or DNA, only an energetic form.
E05 Seyetik identifies Nidell as a Halanan psychoprojective telepath and Fenna as an unconscious projection produced during severe emotional distress; Dax's energy readings are consistent with that explanation.
E06 Fenna initially asserts independent reality and denies Seyetik's account, but cannot supply memories predating her meetings with Sisko.
E07 Fenna chooses to reintegrate/return to Nidell when continued projection threatens Nidell's life.
E08 After reintegration Nidell recovers physically but has no memory of Fenna's experiences; Sisko retains those memories.
E09 Seyetik deliberately pilots the protomatter delivery shuttle into the dead star, disables rescue, frames the act as setting Nidell free, and dies while the star is successfully reignited.
E10 Counterevidence: Seyetik is both an informed spouse and emotionally implicated party; his explanation gains support from Dax's observations and outcome, not from his testimony alone.

Assertions:
EA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Fenna is strongly source-supported as an energetic psychoprojective manifestation causally dependent on Nidell. E01-E08.
EA02 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Fenna's first-person selfhood should not be erased merely because she is dependent/projection-derived; the source depicts autonomous-seeming preference, fear, love, and choice while also establishing dependence. E04-E08.
EA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Reintegration is memory-asymmetric: Nidell survives without Fenna's episodic memories while Sisko remembers Fenna. E07-E08.
EA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Seyetik's death is deliberate self-sacrifice/suicide within the depicted sequence, not an accidental protomatter-delivery failure. E09.

## Sanctuary

Local entities: Kira, Sisko, Haneek, Tumak, Skrreean refugees, Bajoran provisional government, Bajoran interceptors, Draylon II, Kentanna religious/cultural referent, Dominion referent.

Evidence:
S01 Initial Skrreean arrivals cannot communicate until translation adapts; early impressions occur before mutual language is available.
S02 Haneek reports roughly three million Skrreeans escaped eight centuries of T-Rogoran forced labor after the T-Rogorans were conquered by an entity/group called the Dominion.
S03 Skrreean sacred texts describe a home called Kentanna beyond the Eye of the Universe; Haneek eventually identifies Bajor as Kentanna.
S04 Haneek becomes an emergent leader despite describing herself as an ordinary farmer; symbolic first passage through the wormhole contributes to expectations placed on her.
S05 Station interactions include mockery, stereotyping, conflict, and Quark's open resentment alongside Kira/Varani acts of welcome and cultural empathy.
S06 Sisko locates Draylon II as a materially viable resettlement candidate.
S07 Bajoran ministers and Vedek representatives reject Skrreean settlement on Bajor after projections predict unacceptable resource/famine risk; Haneek argues Skrreean farmers could instead improve Bajor's devastated agricultural land.
S08 Kira personally accepts the government's refusal despite friendship with Haneek, distinguishing personal sympathy from institutional/resource judgment.
S09 Haneek experiences Kira's decision as betrayal; the episode does not reconcile their competing risk assessments.
S10 Tumak attempts to reach Bajor despite exclusion orders; his damaged ship exchanges fire with Bajoran interceptors and explodes after a phaser impact ignites its radiation leak.
S11 Haneek's final judgment is that Bajor may have rejected people who could have helped its famine; Kira can only answer that the counterfactual will never be known.

Assertions:
SA01 PROPOSED/CONTESTED — Bajor's refusal is source-supported as a resource-risk decision under post-occupation scarcity and as a decision plausibly carrying xenophobic/fearful social context; the episode does not prove either prudence or moral failure as objective fact. S05-S11.
SA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Skrreean identification of Bajor as Kentanna is a religious/cultural interpretation and claim, not independently proven prophecy. S03-S07.
SA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Tumak's death results from combined unauthorized approach, ship damage/radiation leak, armed interception, and exchanged fire; attributing it solely to one side would flatten the depicted causal chain. S10.
SA04 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Whether Skrreean settlement would have alleviated or worsened Bajor's food crisis remains explicitly counterfactual/unresolved. S07-S11.

## Rivals

Local entities: Odo, Martus Mazur, Quark, Alsia, O'Brien, Bashir, gambling device original, replicated probability devices, station neutrino/probability field.

Evidence:
V01 Odo arrests Martus after victims accuse him of exploiting El-Aurian listening/confidence to obtain financial access and make self-serving investments.
V02 Martus acquires an alien gambling device from a dying prisoner whose history is described in terms of extraordinary misfortune.
V03 Martus replicates enlarged versions of the device and opens a competing gambling club; he initially experiences unusually favorable outcomes.
V04 Station-wide clusters of improbable accidents and system failures increase while Martus's devices operate.
V05 Dax/technical investigation finds anomalous solar-neutrino spin inside the station while neutrinos outside remain normal.
V06 During a racquetball rematch O'Brien experiences improbably perfect outcomes while Bashir experiences improbable failures; both recognize the distribution cannot plausibly be ordinary performance variance.
V07 Investigation links the probability distortion to Martus's replicated gambling devices; the devices affect nonplayers as well as gamblers.
V08 Destroying/neutralizing the devices ends the anomalous condition in the episode's causal sequence.
V09 Martus is separately re-arrested when earlier victims renew charges; his probability-device culpability is not used as a substitute for proving the financial complaints.
V10 Alsia later scams Martus with the same sort of confidence/investment manipulation he used on others, a narrative reversal rather than independent evidence about the alien technology.

Assertions:
VA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The replicated gambling devices are strongly causally supported as producing a station-local probability distortion. V02-V08.
VA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — The work distinguishes extremely improbable outcomes from logically impossible outcomes and uses correlated physical measurements to move from coincidence toward causal attribution. V04-V08.
VA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Martus's financial fraud allegations and the probability anomaly are separate evidentiary matters even though both involve him. V01,V09.
VA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Alsia's reversal of Martus's scam pattern is narratively symmetrical but should not be mistaken for supernatural karmic evidence. V10.

## Cross-work hypothesis tests

Hypothesis A — DS9 repeatedly creates person-like states whose memory and embodiment do not align neatly.
Support: Fenna/Nidell, earlier Verad Dax/Jadzia Dax.
Counterevidence: Pel's concealment is social/legal presentation without an anomalous embodiment mechanism; these must not be collapsed.
Status: PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis B — characters improve causal judgments by adding independent material measurements to testimony.
Support: probability devices plus neutrino anomaly; Fenna energy scan plus Seyetik explanation; Necessary Evil financial/communication records plus recollection.
Counterevidence: Sanctuary's decisive policy question remains counterfactual despite extensive projections and testimony.
Status: PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis C — institutional exclusion can generate concealment, migration, or resistance without proving all resulting choices morally equivalent.
Support: Pel under Ferengi law; Skrreean immigration refusal; Kira occupation underground activity.
Counterevidence: legal/social contexts, stakes, and individual agency differ substantially.
Status: PROVISIONAL.
