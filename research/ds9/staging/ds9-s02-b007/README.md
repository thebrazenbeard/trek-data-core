## The Alternate

Local entities: Odo, gas-altered Odo creature-state, Mora Pol, Dax, Bashir, O'Brien, LS Six lifeform specimen, Gamma Quadrant pillar, Dr Weld.

Evidence:
A01 Mora returns and frames the Gamma Quadrant expedition as a possible route to Odo's origins after a Bajoran probe detects DNA patterns resembling Odo's.
A02 Mora recounts that he initially did not recognize Odo as sentient; Odo says he had to teach Mora that fact by finding a form of communication.
A03 The expedition retrieves a metamorphic silicate lifeform and an inscribed pillar; a volcanic gas incapacitates the humanoids but not Odo because he lacks ordinary respiration.
A04 A lifeform later escapes/dies in DS9's environment while destructive incidents continue, weakening the initial theory that the imported lifeform caused all damage.
A05 Organic residue from a later attack is eventually identified as originating from Odo rather than the imported specimen.
A06 The destructive episodes occur at intervals corresponding approximately to Odo's regeneration cycle; Mora infers Odo transforms during periods Odo remembers as being in his pail.
A07 Mora pressures Odo by predicting the crew will imprison/quarantine/exhibit him, while Odo explicitly rejects Mora's claim to know how his present community will respond.
A08 During confrontation Odo visibly undergoes the uncontrolled transformation and attacks/pursues Mora; station personnel contain him and he returns to ordinary Odo form.
A09 Bashir removes remaining planetary gas from Odo's cellular structure but cannot fully explain the mechanism linking exposure to transformation.
A10 Odo and Mora reconcile partially after Mora acknowledges the coercive/prisoner dynamic of their earlier laboratory relationship.

Assertions:
AA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The station creature is strongly source-supported as an altered/uncontrolled state of Odo rather than a separate imported organism. A04-A09.
AA02 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Planetary gas is associated with the transformation and removal ends the immediate condition, but the source does not fully specify mechanism or whether all behavioral content originated solely from the gas. A03-A09.
AA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Mora's early scientific relationship with Odo includes a documented epistemic failure to recognize sentience and a coercive developmental history that remains emotionally active for Odo. A02,A07,A10.
AA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Odo's transformation does not erase the need to distinguish involuntary altered-state behavior from ordinary intentional conduct. A06-A09.

## Armageddon Game

Local entities: Bashir, O'Brien, Sisko, Dax, Keiko, T'Lani representatives, Kellerun representatives, harvester weapon knowledge, falsified death recording.

Evidence:
G01 T'Lani and Kellerun scientists with Bashir/O'Brien successfully destroy harvester weapons and purge technical files after centuries of war.
G02 Armed forces then attack the joint research site and kill participating scientists; Bashir/O'Brien escape but initially infer one side has betrayed the other.
G03 Both governments jointly tell Sisko that Bashir/O'Brien died in an accidental security-device discharge and provide sensor imagery as proof.
G04 Keiko identifies a behavioral detail in the recording inconsistent with Miles's routine: apparent afternoon coffee consumption; spectral data confirms it is coffee.
G05 Investigation of the Ganges reveals erased computer-log time and remote transporter activity after the alleged fatal event, demonstrating the death recording was altered.
G06 Bashir discovers O'Brien was infected by harvester material during the attack and progressively loses motor/sensory function.
G07 T'Lani/Kellerun representatives jointly admit they killed their own scientists and intend to kill Bashir/O'Brien because any surviving knowledge could recreate the weapons.
G08 Sisko/Dax rescue the officers and use deception/remote navigation to make the pursuing warship believe a runabout carrying them was destroyed.
G09 The governments' professed peace is therefore accompanied by coordinated extrajudicial killing designed to erase dangerous knowledge.
G10 Counterevidence: the stated motive of preventing future weapons is not evidence that murder was the only available method, nor does the source establish whether all technical knowledge was actually erased globally.

Assertions:
GA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The alleged accidental deaths are a coordinated falsification by both former belligerents. G03-G08.
GA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Keiko's ordinary personal knowledge of O'Brien becomes high-value forensic counterevidence when combined with spectral/log data. G04-G05.
GA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Peace institutions in this work commit lethal secrecy enforcement despite publicly renouncing the weapon class, separating disarmament goals from lawful/ethical means. G01,G07-G10.

## Whispers

Local entities: O'Brien-replicant viewpoint, biological Miles O'Brien, Keiko, Molly, Sisko, Bashir, Odo, Paradan government scientists, Paradan rebels, programmed assassination function.

Evidence:
W01 The episode is largely framed through a first-person personal log by a figure who sincerely identifies as Miles O'Brien and remembers ordinary family, work, and mission history.
W02 After returning from Parada training, he interprets changed behavior from Keiko, command staff, Bashir, and security restrictions as evidence of conspiracy or replacement.
W03 His medical exam reports normal health and he retains O'Brien's engineering expertise, habits, intimate memories, and ability to navigate DS9 systems.
W04 Protected station logs show others are analyzing his Paradan report and excluding him from sensitive security arrangements, reinforcing his suspicion from his own perspective.
W05 Odo initially appears to become an ally but then participates in efforts to detain him; the viewpoint figure flees toward Parada to warn participants in the peace talks.
W06 On Parada he encounters the biological Miles O'Brien, whom rebels say they rescued after learning he had been replaced.
W07 Paradan rebels explain government scientists created the duplicate/replicant with O'Brien's memories and a hidden program expected to activate during the talks, possibly for assassination.
W08 The duplicate passed a physical and behaved convincingly enough that DS9 personnel could not initially prove he was not O'Brien; their precautions were containment while the biological officer was sought.
W09 After learning the truth and being mortally wounded, the duplicate's final concern is Keiko and his love for her, preserving the sincerity of attachments generated/copied into him.
W10 Counterevidence: sincere self-identification and memory continuity do not establish biological identity with Miles; simultaneous existence of biological O'Brien supplies decisive body/causal divergence evidence.

Assertions:
WA01 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The O'Brien replicant is a distinct local person-like entity with copied O'Brien memories, skills, attachments, and self-model; global personhood/legal identity must not be silently resolved. W01-W10.
WA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — The biological O'Brien and the replicant coexist and therefore cannot be represented as one uninterrupted biological individual. W06-W10.
WA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — The replicant's conspiracy inference is locally rational given his evidence but false about DS9 personnel's identity/status; epistemic reasonableness and truth diverge. W02-W08.
WA04 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The hidden assassination programming is reported by rebels and explains government motive, but the program never activates in the observed sequence, so exact intended target/action remains uncertain. W07.

## Paradise

Local entities: Sisko, O'Brien, Alixus, Vinod, Joseph, Cassandra, Meg, Santa Maria colony, artificial duonetic-field generator.

Evidence:
P01 Sisko/O'Brien find a decade-old human colony where electromagnetic technology fails under a duonetic field and residents say their original ship became stranded accidentally.
P02 Alixus promotes a philosophy that technological civilization has alienated humans from a more authentic core identity; colonists describe genuine community accomplishments and attachment to their life there.
P03 Medical technology that could save the ill Meg is refused while available natural treatments fail; Meg dies.
P04 The colony uses a punitive confinement box under severe heat; Sisko explicitly distinguishes discipline from torture.
P05 Alixus uses sleep deprivation, forced labor, confinement, water deprivation, ideological pressure, and attempted social/sexual integration to push Sisko/O'Brien toward compliance.
P06 O'Brien discovers the duonetic field is artificial, shuts it down, and restores technological operation.
P07 Alixus admits she helped invent the field, deliberately selected the isolated planet, engineered the Santa Maria's failure, destroyed/removed technology, and deceived the colonists for ten years as an intentional social experiment.
P08 Alixus accepts that deaths, including deaths from treatable illness, were foreseeable/acceptable casualties for the community project and says she would accept the same for her son.
P09 Sisko arrests/removes Alixus and Vinod, but the remaining colonists choose to stay and preserve much of the community they built.
P10 Counterevidence: continued colonist attachment to the community demonstrates that outcomes they value can arise from an origin involving deception/coercion; those later values do not retroactively constitute informed consent to the original experiment.

Assertions:
PA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The colony's isolation was deliberately engineered by Alixus, not an accidental crash caused by natural astatine deposits. P01,P06-P08.
PA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Alixus's social project relied on sustained deception and coercion, including acceptance of preventable deaths. P03-P08.
PA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Colonists' later choice to remain is distinct evidence of present attachment and cannot be used to infer original consent. P07-P10.
PA04 PROPOSED/CONTESTED — The source depicts real community benefits alongside serious coercive harms; neither cancels the other. P02-P10.

## Shadowplay

Local entities: Odo, Dax, Taya, Rurigan, Colyus, Anetra, holographic villagers, holographic village/environment, hologenerator, physical Rurigan, Dominion referent.

Evidence:
H01 Odo/Dax investigate disappearances in a valley whose particle field initially obscures ordinary sensor readings.
H02 The village protector reports 22 residents have vanished, while inhabitants show a striking lack of travel outside the valley.
H03 Taya physically interacts, remembers family history, expresses fear/hope, and forms a relationship with Odo before either knows her substrate.
H04 Dax discovers the field is a holographic/omicron-particle system forming physically interactive objects and inhabitants; crossing the field boundary causes projected body parts to disappear/rematerialize.
H05 System failure causes individual villagers to vanish and threatens the whole village.
H06 The actual physical Rurigan reveals he created the village after fleeing Dominion takeover of Yadera Prime, using a hologenerator to recreate what he lost.
H07 Rurigan says he programmed villagers to reproduce/produce children; Taya was not directly authored as an individual but emerged from programmed parent personalities.
H08 Rurigan initially treats villagers as unreal simulations despite living with them for thirty years and loving Taya.
H09 Odo challenges substrate-only reality criteria by pointing to Taya's thoughts, feelings, emergent ancestry, reciprocal love, and Rurigan's own behavior toward her.
H10 Rurigan chooses to remain; Dax restores/upgrades the generator and missing villagers rematerialize. Rurigan asks that his distinct physical status not be disclosed to the villagers so he will not be treated differently.

Assertions:
HA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The villagers are projection-derived/omicron-based entities physically dependent on the hologenerator. H04-H10.
HA02 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The source deliberately contests whether substrate dependence makes the villagers unreal; Odo's argument and Rurigan's eventual conduct support person-like social reality without supplying a universal ontology rule. H03,H07-H10.
HA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Taya is not merely a direct copy of a specific lost individual; she is an emergent descendant generated through parent-personality rules. H07.
HA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Physical Rurigan and holographic villagers require distinct local identity handling even though he socially belongs to the community. H06-H10.

## Cross-work hypothesis tests

Hypothesis A — copied/constructed minds can possess sincere first-person identity that diverges from causal/biological origin.
Support: O'Brien replicant; Taya/holographic villagers.
Counterevidence/limits: Odo's altered creature-state is transformation of an existing individual, not a constructed duplicate. These mechanisms must remain separate.
Status: PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis B — later endorsement cannot automatically repair nonconsensual origins.
Support: Paradise colonists remain despite Alixus's engineered deception; Rurigan's villagers value their lives despite constructed origins.
Counterevidence: Shadowplay creation is not depicted as an intentional coercive experiment on already-existing subjects in the same manner as Paradise.
Status: PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis C — small anomaly observations can falsify highly authoritative narratives.
Support: Keiko's coffee observation exposes the Armageddon recording; alternate-lifeform theory fails against organic-residue evidence; Whispers reverses the viewpoint narrative when simultaneous biological O'Brien is found.
Counterevidence: anomaly detection can also support false theories, as the O'Brien replicant's reasonable conspiracy inference demonstrates.
Status: PROVISIONAL.
