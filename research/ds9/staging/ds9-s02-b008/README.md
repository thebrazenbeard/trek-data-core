## Playing God

Local entities: Jadzia Dax, Dax symbiont, Curzon Dax referent, Arjin, Sisko, O'Brien, proto-universe, possible proto-universe inhabitants/life signs.

Evidence:
PG01 Arjin arrives as a Trill host candidate assigned to Jadzia for evaluation; Jadzia is initially anxious about reproducing Curzon's harsh assessment style.
PG02 Jadzia recounts that Curzon terminated her from the host program; she reapplied after finding her own voice and was later selected for joining.
PG03 Jadzia explicitly distinguishes herself from prior hosts: she is not Curzon or Lela, while also saying she is Dax and is still determining what that means.
PG04 She tells Arjin that joining can involve prior-host desires/dreams and warns that a host must not enter joining merely to meet others' expectations.
PG05 A proto-universe becomes attached to a runabout/station and expands in a way that threatens DS9 and potentially the surrounding region.
PG06 Initial survival planning includes destroying the proto-universe, but Dax finds nonrandom thermodynamic/power-use patterns interpreted as life signs and potentially intelligent civilization developing at a different temporal rate.
PG07 Sisko rejects destruction once the life evidence becomes material, reasoning that knowingly destroying an inhabited universe could be mass murder; the crew instead attempts risky return through the wormhole.
PG08 Arjin pilots a precision passage through the wormhole after containment collapses; the proto-universe is returned to the Gamma Quadrant side rather than destroyed.
PG09 Arjin says Jadzia is unlike his idealized expectation of joined Trill; Jadzia answers that she is also unlike what she expected and recommends joining only when he is ready.
PG10 Counterevidence: the life-sign inference is based on measured nonrandom energy behavior, not direct communication with inhabitants; exact civilization/personhood status remains unresolved.

Assertions:
PGA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jadzia explicitly differentiates current-host identity from Curzon while preserving Dax continuity. PG02-PG04,PG09.
PGA02 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The proto-universe is source-supported as potentially inhabited/complexly living, but exact inhabitants and intelligence remain inferred rather than directly observed. PG05-PG10.
PGA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Sisko's decision changes when evidence changes the anticipated moral object from dangerous phenomenon to potentially inhabited universe. PG05-PG08.
PGA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Arjin's host-candidacy resolution centers on autonomous readiness rather than satisfying family/evaluator expectations. PG01-PG04,PG09.

## Profit and Loss

Local entities: Quark, Natima Lang, Rekelen, Hogue, Garak, Odo, Sisko, Gul Toran, Cardassian Central Command, Cardassian dissident movement.

Evidence:
PL01 Natima arrives with students Rekelen/Hogue after an attack and is identified as Quark's former lover and former Cardassian Information Service correspondent on occupied Terok Nor.
PL02 Natima says Quark once used access codes she trusted him with to steal while also recalling that he sold food to Bajorans and did not report her dissident activity.
PL03 Natima identifies herself and the students with an underground political movement seeking change in Cardassia.
PL04 Central Command labels the students terrorists and demands surrender; Sisko initially refuses coercive seizure.
PL05 Bajor later accepts a prisoner exchange, and Sisko states he must abide by Bajoran station authority despite opposing the decision.
PL06 Toran privately orders Garak to ensure Natima/Rekelen/Hogue do not leave alive, offering restoration of Garak's standing; this contradicts the publicly accepted exchange.
PL07 Quark begs Odo to release them; Odo ultimately agrees on justice grounds because their acts do not warrant death.
PL08 Garak appears to enforce Toran's order but instead kills Toran and allows the dissidents to depart.
PL09 Garak says love of Cardassia motivated his choice, separating loyalty to society/home from obedience to Central Command.
PL10 Natima refuses to stay with Quark because her political work continues; both profess love while accepting separation.

Assertions:
PLA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Cardassian Central Command covertly repudiates the prisoner exchange by ordering the dissidents killed. PL04-PL08.
PLA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Garak's action distinguishes national/home attachment from institutional obedience. PL06-PL09.
PLA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Odo frees the dissidents on an articulated justice rationale, not merely as a personal favor to Quark. PL07.
PLA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Natima's relationship decision preserves political agency rather than treating romantic reunion as overriding her dissident commitments. PL03,PL10.

## Blood Oath

Local entities: Jadzia Dax, Curzon Dax referent, Kang, Kor, Koloth, Albino, murdered Klingon sons/godson, Sisko, Kira.

Evidence:
BO01 Kor/Koloth/Kang recognize Dax through Curzon-linked memories; Jadzia identifies herself as Jadzia while also asserting Dax continuity and recalling intimate Klingon history.
BO02 The Albino previously retaliated against three Klingon captains by infecting their firstborn children with a fatal genetic virus; Curzon's godson was among the dead.
BO03 Curzon joined the Klingons' blood oath of vengeance; Jadzia remembers the funeral and says she feels an obligation to Curzon's commitment.
BO04 Kang explicitly releases Jadzia from Curzon's oath and states new Trill hosts are not obligated to past commitments.
BO05 Jadzia nevertheless voluntarily claims the obligation as her own and persuades the Klingons to include her.
BO06 Kira tells Jadzia that killing takes part of the killer as well; Sisko refuses leave and emphasizes Jadzia's separate Starfleet oath and legal/moral responsibilities.
BO07 Jadzia disobeys/works around the Starfleet command boundary to join the mission, accepting possible professional consequences.
BO08 Kang's proposed frontal assault is revealed as a planned honorable suicide after he learns the Albino expects them; Jadzia rejects death-seeking fatalism and develops a more survivable strategy.
BO09 The Albino has prepared a mine/ambush rather than the promised honorable battle; the group disables directed-energy weapons and attacks by another route.
BO10 Koloth and Kang die during the assault. Jadzia confronts the Albino; the transcript establishes completion of the revenge confrontation while not cleanly specifying every physical detail of the final fatal act, so no finer-grained killer attribution is asserted here.

Assertions:
BOA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jadzia's participation is voluntarily assumed rather than imposed by Trill continuity or Klingon demand. BO03-BO07.
BOA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — The episode explicitly presents competing continuity norms: Jadzia feels Curzon's obligation while Kang says a new host has no obligation to prior commitments. BO03-BO05.
BOA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jadzia's current-host scientific/tactical capabilities materially alter the plan beyond what Curzon could have provided. BO08-BO09.
BOA04 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Memory/continuity supplies motivational force but does not by itself settle legal or metaphysical identity between Curzon and Jadzia. BO01-BO07.

## The Maquis, Part I

Local entities: Sisko, Kira, Dax, O'Brien, Odo, Calvin Hudson, Gul Dukat, Sakonna, Quark, William Samuels, Bok'Nor crew, Federation colonists, Cardassian colonists, covert Maquis cell, Cardassian local attack ships.

Evidence:
M101 Bok'Nor is destroyed after departing DS9; technical investigation finds no ordinary engine fault and later identifies a Federation-origin implosive device via mercassium casing evidence.
M102 Federation and Bajoran authorities initially lack a clear attribution; Cardassian retaliation is feared but not assumed as fact.
M103 Hudson describes Federation colonists who refused relocation under the new treaty and alleges escalating violence plus Cardassian authority complicity/arms support.
M104 Dukat secretly approaches Sisko, says Central Command does not know he is there, and claims Federation colonists are responsible for Bok'Nor.
M105 During Sisko/Dukat travel, armed Cardassian colonial craft attack a Federation merchant vessel while a modified Federation civilian vessel intervenes; the incident shows organized violence by colonists on both sides without proving state command of either force.
M106 Cardassians present William Samuels's confession to bombing Bok'Nor; Hudson says the confession may have been coerced, and Samuels is then reported dead in custody under a disputed suicide account.
M107 O'Brien's debris analysis independently establishes the Bok'Nor device was Federation-built, materially supporting Federation-colonist involvement without independently validating the circumstances of Samuels's confession.
M108 Sakonna secretly buys extensive weapons from Quark.
M109 Kira argues that Cardassians, not Federation colonists, are the enemy and sympathizes with settler self-defense; Sisko rejects abandoning treaty obligations or arming the colonists.
M110 A group kidnaps Dukat, uses forged transport provenance, and publicly identifies itself as the Maquis; the trail leads toward the Badlands and Hudson.

Assertions:
M1A01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Bok'Nor destruction is materially linked to a Federation-origin implosive device; Samuels's exact culpability remains partly dependent on a confession obtained under contested conditions. M101,M106-M107.
M1A02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Organized armed violence exists among both Cardassian and Federation colonists before the Maquis political label fully crystallizes on screen. M103,M105.
M1A03 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — Central Command's direct role in supplying Cardassian colonists remains an allegation at Part I's end, despite circumstantial patterns and Hudson's reports. M103-M105.
M1A04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Sisko and Kira have a substantive policy/moral disagreement over settler self-defense versus Federation treaty obligations. M109.

## The Maquis, Part II

Local entities: Sisko, Hudson, Dukat, Sakonna, Maquis members, Quark, Odo, Kira, Admiral Nechayev, Legate Parn, Cardassian Central Command, Xepolite intermediary, Cardassian weapons depot/colonists.

Evidence:
M201 Hudson openly identifies with the Maquis, argues Federation withdrawal left settlers to protect themselves, and asks Sisko for station support; Sisko refuses and frames continued attacks as escalating toward war.
M202 Starfleet leadership initially characterizes Maquis as irresponsible citizens who should be reachable through dialogue; Sisko argues Earth-based assumptions underestimate frontier fear and anger.
M203 Quark's dealings establish Sakonna was procuring shields, photon torpedoes, cannons, and other weapons for the Maquis.
M204 Maquis captors attempt to interrogate Dukat; Sakonna's mind-meld fails because he shields his thoughts. Sisko's team rescues him without adopting Cardassian torture methods.
M205 Legate Parn tells Sisko that Dukat himself was responsible for arms smuggling and that Central Command would prefer him dead. Dukat recognizes this as political abandonment rather than confirmation of his culpability.
M206 Dukat then admits he had been misinformed and concludes Central Command is in fact secretly supplying Cardassian colonists, while saying he personally had not been informed.
M207 Dukat identifies Xepolite/free-trader intermediary routes. Sisko/Dukat intercept an arms shipment, providing material confirmation of Central Command weapons smuggling.
M208 Quark argues to Sakonna that once covert Cardassian supply is stopped, the relative price/risk of peace is lower than continued escalation; Sakonna supplies timing of a planned Maquis depot attack but not location.
M209 Sisko warns colonists publicly and tries to dissuade Hudson, but Hudson states the conflict has become a war and attacks the Cardassian depot anyway.
M210 Sisko's force disables Maquis ships rather than seeking lethal destruction; Sisko declines an opportunity to kill Hudson after stopping the immediate attack. Hudson escapes and declares himself an outlaw beginning a new life.
M211 Starfleet later congratulates Sisko for preserving peace; Sisko explicitly questions whether he prevented war or merely delayed it.

Assertions:
M2A01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Central Command covertly supplies Cardassian colonists with weapons through intermediaries, and this is materially corroborated rather than remaining only Maquis testimony. M205-M207.
M2A02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Dukat is politically expendable to Central Command and is falsely/strategically blamed by Parn for the smuggling operation. M205-M207.
M2A03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Maquis violence is not reducible to fabricated grievance: real covert Cardassian treaty violations coexist with Maquis bombings, kidnapping, arms purchases, and offensive plans. M201-M210.
M2A04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Sisko rejects both Cardassian covert arming and Maquis insurgent escalation while using force to enforce the treaty; the episode does not make neutrality equivalent to absence of coercion. M207-M211.
M2A05 PROPOSED/UNRESOLVED — The final peace status is explicitly provisional; Sisko himself questions whether conflict was solved or delayed. M211.

## Cross-work hypothesis tests

Hypothesis A — inherited/remembered obligation can motivate action without establishing mandatory identity continuity.
Support: Jadzia's voluntary Blood Oath assumption; Playing God distinction between Jadzia and Curzon.
Counterevidence: Kang explicitly says new hosts have no obligation to past commitments. Status PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis B — political attribution requires separating grievance, actor, intermediary, and state sponsor.
Support: Maquis bombing versus Federation origin; Cardassian colonial violence versus Central Command covert supply; Xepolite intermediaries; Cardassian dissidents versus Central Command in Profit and Loss.
Counterevidence: some actions are directly attributable to named individuals/organizations, so provenance discipline must not become generalized agnosticism. Status PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis C — loyalty to a polity/community can diverge from obedience to its government.
Support: Garak and Cardassia; Natima and Cardassia; Hudson and Federation settlers/Federation; Kira and Bajor in prior batches.
Counterevidence: motivations differ substantially and should not be collapsed into one moral category. Status PROVISIONAL.
