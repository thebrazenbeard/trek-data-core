## Rejoined
RJ01 Jadzia Dax meets Lenara Kahn, whose symbiont's prior host Nilani was married to Torias Dax.
RJ02 Trill reassociation law forbids joined Trill from resuming past-host relationships under penalty of exile and future symbiont death when current hosts die.
RJ03 Jadzia and Lenara both retain relational memories and develop/acknowledge present attraction; neither is depicted as literally being Torias or Nilani.
RJ04 Jadzia is willing to accept exile; Lenara ultimately leaves despite reciprocal feeling because she cannot accept the social/biological consequence.
Assertions: current-host identities remain distinct from prior hosts; inherited memory can ground genuine present feeling without making prior marriage automatically current; the taboo is an institutional Trill rule rather than a demonstrated metaphysical law.

## Starship Down
SD01 Defiant meets Karemma inside a gas giant because Karemma fear direct Dominion-observed Federation trade; Quark's hidden tariffs are exposed during negotiation.
SD02 Jem'Hadar attack the Karemma/Defiant, damaging Defiant and forcing survival under extreme atmospheric pressure and sensor limitations.
SD03 Sisko is seriously injured; Kira keeps him conscious through conversation despite her discomfort relating to the Emissary as an ordinary injured person.
SD04 Worf's initial command style treats engineering crew as interchangeable subordinates; under pressure he adapts toward O'Brien's recommendation to use individual skills/trust.
SD05 Quark and Hanok survive an unexploded torpedo crisis and revise mutual stereotypes while negotiating honestly after Quark's earlier commercial manipulation is exposed.
Assertions: altered command/interpersonal judgments arise from observed crisis behavior, not species stereotypes; Kira's religious relationship to Sisko coexists with ordinary command/friendship; Quark's original hidden charges remain deceptive even though later cooperation is genuine.

## Little Green Men
LG01 Quark, Rom and Nog encounter a shuttle sabotage/kemocite interaction that sends them to Earth in 1947 rather than simply crashing in their own time.
LG02 Human military personnel interpret the Ferengi as extraterrestrial visitors; initial inability to communicate is caused by translator malfunction rather than lack of language/intelligence.
LG03 After repairing translation, Quark attempts to exploit future knowledge commercially while Rom/Nog increasingly resist contaminating history.
LG04 The Ferengi are the source of the Roswell 'little green men' incident within this temporal frame, but they return to their own century and the historical public record remains distorted/incomplete.
LG05 Nog openly identifies Starfleet/Federation values as preferable to Quark's plan and prioritizes reaching the Academy.
Assertions: this is temporal displacement of the same local Ferengi individuals, not ancestor/counterpart replacement; 1947 human interpretations are evidence of witness belief under limited information; the episode creates a closed historical participation loop without establishing that every later Roswell legend is accurate.

## The Sword of Kahless
SK01 Kor, Worf and Dax recover an ancient bat'leth strongly identified as the Sword of Kahless from a Hur'q site.
SK02 Possession rapidly amplifies/organizes Kor's and Worf's ambition, distrust and competing claims to political destiny, while Dax repeatedly challenges both.
SK03 Toral also seeks the Sword for political legitimacy; physical possession becomes a symbolic claim to rule rather than mere archaeology.
SK04 Worf and Kor ultimately conclude the Sword's political power would divide the Empire and beam it into space rather than give it to the Emperor or keep it.
SK05 Claims that the Sword itself speaks/influences or that Kahless literally guides Worf remain character interpretations; the source also supports ordinary ambition, rivalry and symbolic intoxication.
Assertions: authenticity of the recovered artifact is strongly treated as established locally; causal supernatural influence remains UNRESOLVED; symbolic objects can exert political/psychological effects without needing literal agency.

## Our Man Bashir
OMB01 A sabotaged runabout explodes during emergency transport of Sisko, Kira, Dax, Worf and O'Brien.
OMB02 Station systems preserve their transporter data by distributing neural patterns across station memory while physical patterns are stored/processed in Bashir's active holosuite as character bodies.
OMB03 The resulting holo characters have the physical appearances/patterns of the officers but act according to Bashir's spy-program roles rather than the officers' ordinary memories/personality.
OMB04 Bashir must keep the program running because ending it would destroy the stored physical patterns; simulated lethal events can therefore threaten the officers' recoverability despite the character minds being program-derived.
OMB05 Rom/Eddington reintegrate neural and physical patterns via Defiant transport systems, restoring the officers.
OMB06 Bashir deliberately lets the villain's world-destruction plan run inside the simulation to stall for time, demonstrating that simulated world destruction is not external Earth destruction.
Assertions: physical-pattern continuity and neural-pattern continuity are temporarily separable in this work's transporter mechanics; holo-role Sisko/Kira/Dax/Worf/O'Brien occurrences are simulations using stored physical patterns, not ordinary conscious occurrences of those people; successful reintegration restores them but the episode does not supply a universal metaphysical theory of transporter identity.

## Cross-work notes
- Reassociation, temporal displacement, symbolic influence and transporter/holo pattern separation are different identity mechanisms and must remain separately typed.
- `Our Man Bashir` is a regression case for the invariant that a body/appearance in a simulation does not automatically establish ordinary-person agency.
- `Little Green Men` is a regression case for historical witness records that are sincere but materially incomplete.
