## Bar Association

Evidence:
BA01 Rom nearly dies after continuing to work through a serious ear infection because his Ferengi labor contract forbids leaving during business hours and provides no sick leave, vacations or paid overtime.
BA02 Quark cuts employee wages by one-third during a temporary business downturn while retaining unilateral management power.
BA03 Rom organizes Ferengi and non-Ferengi employees into the Guild of Restaurant and Casino Employees, demanding higher pay, shorter hours, paid sick leave and collective bargaining.
BA04 Quark initially refuses negotiations and the employees strike; Federation/DS9 personnel support the picket and Sisko uses the Federation's ownership/lease leverage to pressure Quark to negotiate.
BA05 FCA Liquidator Brunt arrives because Ferengi law/culture prohibits unions and collective labor action; FCA enforcement includes coercive violence intended to break the strike.
BA06 Quark is badly beaten by Brunt's Nausicaans, demonstrating that the anti-union institution threatens owners as well as workers when they fail to enforce Ferengi orthodoxy.
BA07 Quark ultimately reaches an agreement with Rom that meets the workers' material demands while publicly dissolving the union, allowing both labor gains and Ferengi face-saving.
BA08 Rom then quits Quark's employment voluntarily and takes a junior diagnostic/repair technician job on DS9, explicitly saying he needs a life of his own rather than waiting to inherit the bar.
BA09 Quark and Rom preserve their brother relationship despite ending employer/employee dependence.

Assertions:
BAA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Quark's workers are materially exploited under the described Ferengi labor regime; the union emerges from concrete health/pay/work-condition evidence rather than abstract ideology alone. BA01-BA04.
BAA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — FCA anti-union enforcement is coercive and violent, not merely a cultural preference. BA05-BA06.
BAA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — The strike materially improves conditions even though the union is formally dissolved as part of the settlement. BA07.
BAA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Rom's occupational shift is an autonomous identity/career decision separating family relationship from economic subordination. BA08-BA09.

## Accession

Evidence:
AC01 Akorem Laan emerges from the wormhole in an ancient Bajoran lightship after having disappeared roughly two centuries earlier from linear Bajoran history.
AC02 Akorem identifies himself as the first Bajoran to find the Celestial Temple and argues that the Prophets returned him to claim the Emissary role.
AC03 Sisko initially yields the Emissary title because ancient texts appear to fit Akorem more literally and because Sisko has remained ambivalent about religious office.
AC04 Akorem calls for restoration of Bajor's hereditary D'jarra caste system, including legal sanctions against people who refuse assigned occupations; Kai Winn supports the program even though it threatens Federation admission.
AC05 Kira begins complying with her family's artist caste despite abandoning work for which she is trained, demonstrating the social force of Emissary authority on sincere believers.
AC06 A Vedek kills another monk for refusing to leave the order despite an “unclean” burial caste, providing direct evidence that caste restoration is producing lethal coercion rather than merely voluntary heritage revival.
AC07 Sisko reclaims the Emissary role and enters the wormhole with Akorem to ask the Prophets directly.
AC08 The wormhole entities say Akorem was preserved because he was injured and that the D'jarras belong to the past; they identify Sisko as their Emissary and say Akorem's return occurred for Sisko.
AC09 At Sisko's request Akorem is returned to his own era alive and without memory of the future interval rather than being allowed to die.
AC10 In the restored present Bajoran history incorporates Akorem's completed literary work, showing that the returned Akorem lived onward in his own time while the caste-restoration episode no longer persists as current policy.

Assertions:
ACA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Within direct wormhole-entity dialogue, Sisko rather than Akorem is identified as the intended Emissary. AC07-AC09.
ACA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Akorem's claim of prophetic authorization for D'jarra restoration is directly contradicted by the wormhole entities. AC04,AC08.
ACA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Akorem is temporally displaced and later returned to his own era; he is not a duplicate/counterpart of another person. AC01,AC09-AC10.
ACA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Religious office has measurable political/social effects on Bajor, including coerced caste compliance and violence, independently of metaphysical questions about the Prophets. AC04-AC06.

## Rules of Engagement

Evidence:
RE01 Worf commands Defiant on a humanitarian convoy escort through an area of active Klingon attacks.
RE02 During battle a cloaked vessel suddenly decloaks in front of Defiant; Worf orders fire before positively identifying it and the vessel is reported as a civilian transport carrying 441 Klingons.
RE03 The Klingon Empire seeks extradition for murder/gross negligence; Ch'Pok uses Worf's Klingon identity, combat preferences, holosuite behavior and resentment over House Mogh to argue that bloodlust overrode Starfleet judgment.
RE04 Worf testifies that he would make the same immediate fire decision again to protect ship/crew/convoy, while also grieving the reported civilians.
RE05 Sisko later discovers the names of the 441 “dead” are identical to passengers reported killed in a transport crash three months earlier, making the casualty manifest impossible as presented.
RE06 Ch'Pok concedes it is possible no civilians were aboard and that the vessel generated false sensor images as part of a staged operation intended to discredit Worf/Federation convoy protection.
RE07 The extradition/massacre case therefore collapses because the alleged civilian deaths were manufactured.
RE08 Afterward Worf admits he accepted the command partly from a desire to confront Klingons and recognizes that he should not have taken the mission in that state.
RE09 Sisko separately tells Worf that firing at an unidentified decloaking vessel in a civilian shipping area was a genuine Starfleet command error even though no civilians were actually killed.

Assertions:
REA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The Klingon civilian-massacre evidence is staged/fabricated; the 441 alleged victims were not established as aboard the vessel Worf destroyed. RE05-RE07.
REA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Worf is exonerated of the alleged massacre without being exonerated of all command error. RE02,RE08-RE09.
REA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Ch'Pok's identity-based argument selectively uses real facts about Worf's anger/combat desire but those facts do not prove the fabricated civilian deaths. RE03,RE05-RE09.
REA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Worf's own retrospective admission provides stronger evidence of impaired mission-selection judgment than the prosecution's essentialist claim that Klingon identity makes him inherently unable to follow Starfleet ethics. RE03,RE08-RE09.

## Hard Time

Evidence:
HT01 Argrathi authorities punish O'Brien for an espionage conviction by implanting an interactive memory of approximately twenty years' imprisonment into his mind over only a few external hours.
HT02 Kira and Bashir explain that O'Brien was never physically imprisoned for those decades, while O'Brien insists the memories are experientially real to him.
HT03 In implanted prison memory O'Brien has a long-term cellmate named Ee'char despite initially telling Bashir he was alone.
HT04 The memory sequence includes deprivation, conflict, attachment and O'Brien eventually killing Ee'char in a rage over food he wrongly believes Ee'char hoarded.
HT05 In external reality Ee'char appears to O'Brien as a recurring memory/hallucinatory representation; no independent external Ee'char is established by the source.
HT06 Bashir says removing the implanted prison memories would require wiping O'Brien's broader memory, so treatment focuses on medication, counseling and reintegration rather than simple deletion.
HT07 O'Brien becomes irritable/violent, threatens Quark, nearly strikes Molly and experiences severe guilt/self-alienation.
HT08 Sisko places O'Brien on medical leave after he resists treatment and his impairment affects duty.
HT09 O'Brien prepares to kill himself with a phaser because he believes he has become dangerous to his family/community; Bashir intervenes and elicits the concealed Ee'char memory/guilt.
HT10 O'Brien distinguishes the remembered murder's factual unreality from its moral/psychological reality to him; Bashir argues his remorse itself is counterevidence to the belief that the prison experience defines him as irredeemably violent.
HT11 O'Brien puts down the weapon, accepts treatment/support and returns to his family; trauma is not depicted as instantly erased.

Assertions:
HTA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — The twenty-year incarceration is an implanted interactive memory, not twenty externally elapsed years of O'Brien's bodily history. HT01-HT02.
HTA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Ee'char is a memory/simulation figure within O'Brien's implanted experience and later internal representation; no external-world Ee'char occurrence is independently established. HT03-HT05.
HTA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Artificial memories produce persistent real psychological/behavioral consequences even when their depicted events did not externally occur. HT02-HT11.
HTA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — O'Brien's belief that the prison-defined self has replaced his prior self is his trauma-driven self-assessment, not an objective identity replacement established by the source. HT07-HT11.

## Shattered Mirror

Evidence:
SM01 Mirror Jennifer visits prime DS9 and openly tells Jake she is not his mother despite identical appearance/voice and her own former marriage to mirror Sisko.
SM02 Jake forms a strong emotional bond with her because of resemblance to his deceased mother.
SM03 Jennifer transports Jake to the mirror universe without Sisko's consent as part of a rebel plan to lure prime Sisko into helping repair the mirror Defiant.
SM04 Mirror O'Brien explicitly admits the rebels used Jake because Sisko would otherwise refuse to intervene in their war.
SM05 Prime Sisko helps make the mirror Defiant operational under coercive leverage, then voluntarily remains to help fight once the Alliance attack begins.
SM06 Jennifer later tells Jake the truth about using him and decides to return him home after recognizing the depth of his attachment.
SM07 Mirror Jennifer and prime Jake remain distinct family-related counterparts/relations: she is not his biological/history mother, while his feelings toward her are nevertheless genuine.
SM08 During escape mirror Intendant Kira shoots Jennifer when Jennifer protects Jake; Jennifer dies after Sisko returns from battle.
SM09 The mirror Defiant defeats Alliance forces using prime-universe design knowledge, but remains a separately built ship in the mirror continuity.
SM10 Prime Sisko/Jake return home after grieving mirror Jennifer; her death does not rewrite the prior death/history of prime Jennifer.

Assertions:
SMA01 PROPOSED/STABLE — Mirror Jennifer and prime Jennifer are distinct local entities; resemblance and cross-universe marital counterpart relations do not establish sameness. SM01-SM10.
SMA02 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jake's attachment to mirror Jennifer is authentic emotional evidence even though it is partly grounded in resemblance to his mother and begins under deception. SM01-SM07.
SMA03 PROPOSED/STABLE — Jennifer/rebels intentionally use Jake as leverage to secure Sisko's assistance; her later remorse/protective action does not make the original coercion nonexistent. SM03-SM08.
SMA04 PROPOSED/STABLE — Mirror Defiant is derived from copied prime-universe design files but is a separate constructed vessel. SM04-SM09.

## Cross-work hypothesis tests

Hypothesis A — subjective or institutional reality can remain consequential after its factual basis is disproved.
Support: O'Brien's implanted prison trauma; Worf's guilt over fabricated civilian casualties; Akorem's Emissary policy effects before Prophet contradiction.
Counterevidence: consequences differ across memory, legal accusation and religious authority and require separate frame typing. Status PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis B — identity-linked arguments can contain real biographical evidence while still producing false essentialist conclusions.
Support: Ch'Pok's use of Worf's Klingon anger; Jake's response to mirror Jennifer; D'jarra assignment.
Counterevidence: source sometimes depicts identity traditions as voluntarily meaningful, so rejecting essentialism does not erase chosen affiliation. Status PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis C — coercion and later autonomous action can coexist in one relationship/event chain.
Support: mirror rebels coercing Sisko then Sisko choosing to remain in battle; Rom's strike under FCA pressure; O'Brien's imposed memories followed by treatment choices.
Counterevidence: later choice does not retroactively authorize original coercion. Status PROVISIONAL.
