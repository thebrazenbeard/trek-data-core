## The Muse
MU01 Lwaxana arrives pregnant and says Tavnian husband Jeyal intends to take their male child under a sex-segregated legal custom; she says he had previously promised their marriage would be different and later treated her as property.
MU02 Odo researches Tavnian law and identifies a legal route: the male child belongs to the mother's husband, so a valid marriage between Odo and Lwaxana would terminate Jeyal's claim.
MU03 The Tavnian ceremony requires Odo to make a sincere public declaration convincing enough to withstand challenge. Odo's final declaration draws on genuine personal history with Lwaxana, including her acceptance of his shapeshifting difference.
MU04 Jeyal does not challenge the completed marriage and leaves, relinquishing the immediate claim. Lwaxana later says she remains romantically in love with Odo but recognizes he does not reciprocate in the same way and leaves for Betazed to preserve the friendship.
MU05 Onaya approaches Jake as a patron/muse figure, gives him a famous writer's pen and uses psionic/bioelectric stimulation to accelerate creative output.
MU06 Jake's neural activity, neurotransmitter production and cerebral capillary dilation rise to dangerous levels, nearly causing synaptic collapse.
MU07 Onaya abducts Jake from medical care and continues extracting energy while encouraging him to finish his novel.
MU08 Confronted by Sisko, Onaya says she has similarly fostered other artists and obtains what she needs to survive from them. She escapes as psionic energy despite Sisko's phaser.
MU09 Jake doubts authorship afterward because Onaya helped release the work; Sisko distinguishes facilitation/extraction from authorship and Jake ultimately signs the manuscript `Anselm` as his own.
Assertions: Odo/Lwaxana's marriage is a legal protective arrangement supported by genuine emotional testimony but not evidence of reciprocal romantic love; Onaya materially enhances and exploits Jake's creativity but does not supply the novel's semantic content as her own authorship; Jake's work remains his source-supported creation despite external stimulation.

## For the Cause
FC01 Odo and Eddington present circumstantial evidence that Kasidy Yates may be supplying the Maquis; Sisko initially resists belief but agrees to investigate.
FC02 Surveillance confirms the Xhosa transfers mostly organic cargo, likely food/medical supplies, to a Maquis vessel. Kasidy's Maquis support is therefore materially corroborated rather than inferred solely from association.
FC03 Sisko allows Kasidy another run while attempting to identify the recipient network, creating direct conflict between his personal relationship and command duty.
FC04 Eddington repeatedly presents himself as a neutral Starfleet officer whose personal opinion of the Maquis is irrelevant, while covertly arranging to remain at DS9 during the replicator operation.
FC05 Eddington gives false Starfleet orders, imposes communications blackout, shoots Kira, steals industrial replicators intended for Cardassia, and departs with them.
FC06 Eddington then openly identifies with the Maquis and says his prior Starfleet security position was used to mislead Starfleet and support Maquis operations.
FC07 Kasidy has an opportunity to remain with the Maquis after Sisko's investigation but later returns to DS9 and accepts arrest rather than permanently fleeing the legal consequences.
FC08 Sisko distinguishes Kasidy's conduct from Eddington's betrayal while still treating both as offenses rather than excusing Kasidy because of their relationship.
Assertions: Eddington's Starfleet role and Maquis allegiance overlap during a covert period; his self-presentation of neutrality is deceptive testimony. Kasidy knowingly supplies the Maquis but later voluntarily returns into Federation custody. Eddington's betrayal does not make every Maquis grievance false, and Kasidy's relationship with Sisko does not negate her agency or culpability.

## To the Death
TD01 Renegade Jem'Hadar attack DS9 and steal technical equipment while a Dominion group led by Weyoun/Omet'iklan is also attacked by the same renegades.
TD02 Weyoun seeks Federation help destroying an Iconian Gateway on Vandros Four because renegade Jem'Hadar could use instantaneous transport to destabilize both Dominion and Alpha Quadrant security.
TD03 Weyoun states Founder control over Jem'Hadar has been overstated and white addiction is an additional control mechanism; this is informed Vorta testimony, not a complete quantitative account of all Jem'Hadar loyalty.
TD04 Omet'iklan's loyal Jem'Hadar know about the Gateway despite Weyoun's attempt to withhold it and say loyalty to the Founders exceeds loyalty to Vorta.
TD05 Starfleet and loyal Jem'Hadar differ sharply over discipline, survival and sacrifice. Omet'iklan kills his own Second Toman'torax for disobeying orders, while Sisko disciplines Worf nonlethally for the same brawl.
TD06 The allied force fights the renegades and destroys the Gateway. Sisko protects Omet'iklan during battle despite Omet'iklan's earlier threat to kill him.
TD07 After victory Omet'iklan vaporizes Weyoun specifically for questioning Jem'Hadar loyalty, then says enough killing has occurred.
TD08 Omet'iklan's troops remain to eliminate surviving renegades and tell Sisko the next meeting may be as enemies.
Assertions: loyal and renegade Jem'Hadar are distinct political/military groups in this work; Jem'Hadar loyalty is not reducible to white addiction because loyal soldiers explicitly prioritize Founder allegiance even while resenting Vorta control. Weyoun's death is inflicted by loyal Omet'iklan, not by the renegades. Temporary Federation-Dominion cooperation is instrumental and does not establish political alliance beyond the shared Gateway threat.

## The Quickening
QU01 Teplans say the Dominion/Jem'Hadar deliberately inflicted the hereditary Blight after their world resisted Dominion control, leaving subsequent generations born infected.
QU02 Trevean provides requested euthanasia to patients after quickening; Bashir initially interprets this as surrender/killing while patients describe it as relief from otherwise unavoidable severe death.
QU03 Bashir develops an antigen from viral analysis and recruits quickened volunteers, while acknowledging no guaranteed cure.
QU04 Federation medical instruments' electromagnetic fields unexpectedly accelerate viral mutation. The experimental patients quicken catastrophically and die; Bashir recognizes his treatment environment caused the disaster.
QU05 Bashir explicitly acknowledges arrogance in believing he could solve the Blight quickly and remains on the planet after Dax/Kira leave.
QU06 Pregnant Ekoria rejects Trevean's offer of assisted death because she wants her child to have a chance to live.
QU07 Ekoria's immune system rejects the antigen from her own bloodstream, but the antigen accumulates through the placenta. Her child is born without Blight while Ekoria dies after delivery.
QU08 Bashir determines the result is a vaccine rather than a cure: inoculating pregnant women can protect unborn children but does not cure currently infected people.
QU09 Trevean accepts the task of producing/distributing the vaccine; Bashir returns to DS9 and continues research for a cure because existing patients remain infected.
Assertions: the Blight is source-supported as intentional Dominion punishment through victim/history testimony, while exact engineering details remain unobserved. Bashir's initial intervention causes lethal iatrogenic worsening through equipment interaction and is explicitly acknowledged as such. The final result prevents transmission/manifestation in future children but is not a cure for existing patients. Trevean's euthanasia occurs with depicted patient request and should not be collapsed into involuntary killing.

## Body Parts
BP01 Quark receives a Ferengi medical diagnosis of incurable Dorek syndrome and is told he has roughly six or seven days to live.
BP02 To settle debts and die profitably, Quark auctions fifty-two disks of his future vacuum-desiccated remains. An anonymous buyer offers five hundred bars of latinum and Quark accepts the contract.
BP03 Dr Orpax later reports the Dorek diagnosis was erroneous. Quark is healthy, but the anonymous buyer is Brunt, who already knew the correction and insists on literal delivery of the contracted remains.
BP04 Brunt says his motive is personal/institutional hostility to Quark's insufficiently ruthless Ferengi conduct, citing Quark's aid to Ishka, settlement with striking workers and comparatively generous business practices.
BP05 Quark initially treats suicide/death as preferable to breaking a Ferengi contract and asks Garak to kill him. A dream/vision involving Gint externalizes Quark's own conflict over the Rules; Quark later recognizes it as a dream and chooses to break the contract.
BP06 Quark refunds Brunt and accepts FCA penalties: confiscation of assets, loss of business standing and social/economic exclusion from Ferengi commerce.
BP07 DS9 friends/associates bring goods, furniture and supplies that allow Quark to restart, leading him to recognize social relationships as assets even though the FCA has stripped conventional property.
BP08 In the parallel medical emergency Keiko is severely injured during a runabout accident while pregnant. Bashir stabilizes her but the fetus's autonomic functions fail.
BP09 Bashir transfers the fetus to Kira because another viable womb is urgently required; later dialogue in subsequent work explicitly characterizes Kira as having volunteered after Bashir proposed the transfer.
BP10 Because Bajoran pregnancy rapidly creates a dense vascular connection, transferring the fetus back to recovered Keiko would likely cause massive hemorrhage to Kira and respiratory trauma to the child, so Kira must carry the pregnancy to term.
BP11 Kira explicitly tells Keiko the child remains Keiko's baby; the O'Briens and Kira reorganize living arrangements so the parents can remain close to the pregnancy.
Assertions: Quark's terminal status is a false medical diagnosis, but his body-parts contract and subsequent FCA punishment are real institutional consequences. Quark's Gint encounter is a dream/vision representation of his internal rule conflict, not evidence that historical Gint literally appeared. Kira becomes gestational carrier after emergency fetal transfer; this does not transfer biological/social parentage from Keiko/Miles to Kira. The transfer is a life-saving intervention with later source evidence of Kira's volunteering, while the exact immediacy of consent at the emergency moment should not be overstated beyond depicted testimony.

## Cross-work hypothesis tests

Hypothesis A — institutional role and actual allegiance can diverge without erasing legal responsibility.
Support: Eddington as Starfleet security chief/Maquis operative; Quark as Ferengi businessman who repeatedly violates Ferengi orthodoxy.
Counterevidence: role divergence differs from false identity and does not imply the role itself was wholly fictitious. Status PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis B — life-saving or creativity-enhancing interventions can simultaneously confer benefit and impose serious autonomy/health costs.
Support: Onaya/Jake; Bashir's failed Blight treatment; emergency fetal transfer.
Counterevidence: Onaya is exploitative and nonconsensual at the dangerous stage, Bashir's intervention is therapeutic but iatrogenic, and Kira's gestational transfer is emergency medical care. The mechanisms are not morally interchangeable. Status PROVISIONAL.

Hypothesis C — stated institutional rules can be selectively weaponized for personal goals.
Support: Brunt's body-parts enforcement; Tavnian child-custody law; Vorta use of white/control claims.
Counterevidence: some rules also structure genuinely chosen commitments, so rule existence alone does not determine coercion. Status PROVISIONAL.
