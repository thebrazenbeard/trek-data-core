## Broken Link
BL01 Odo develops molecular instability that Bashir cannot treat; Founder intervention restores temporary stability and reveals the condition was deliberately induced to force Odo home for judgment.
BL02 The Founders judge Odo for killing another changeling in `The Adversary`, explicitly invoking their rule that no changeling harms another.
BL03 Odo voluntarily submits to judgment despite Sisko's concern because he regards accepting justice as consistent with his own professional/moral commitments.
BL04 Garak attempts to seize Defiant weapons and destroy the Founder planet/Great Link, explicitly accepting Odo/Sisko/Bashir and himself as collateral; Worf stops him and rejects genocide as murder rather than honorable warfare.
BL05 The Founders transform Odo into a physiologically human solid with organs/blood while intentionally retaining his familiar facial appearance as a reminder of what he lost.
BL06 Odo loses shapeshifting/Great Link access but retains autobiographical memory, relationships and security role. He describes solid embodiment as punishment/loss, not fulfillment of an earlier preference.
BL07 After residual Link memories surface, Odo believes he has identified Gowron as a Founder. This is Odo's sincere post-Link conclusion at the episode end, not yet corroborated identity fact.
Assertions: Odo-before/solid-Odo are one causally continuous local person with radically changed biology; imposed solidification is punishment, not ordinary voluntary medical transition. Garak's genocide proposal is deliberate and independently stopped. Odo's Gowron conclusion remains provisional testimony/inference pending later evidence.

## Apocalypse Rising
AR01 Starfleet acts on Odo's apparent Link memory that Gowron is a changeling and sends Sisko, O'Brien, Odo and Worf covertly into Klingon military headquarters using surgical Klingon disguises.
AR02 General Martok independently says Gowron's wartime behavior changed and supports killing him as the only sure way to reveal changeling nature after death.
AR03 The team reaches Gowron, but blood/behavioral evidence does not resolve him as a Founder before combat.
AR04 Odo recognizes Martok, not Gowron, as the changeling. The Martok impersonator is killed and reverts, directly falsifying the original Gowron attribution.
AR05 Gowron concludes the Dominion manipulated Odo's memory/impression to induce Federation assassination of Gowron, allowing the Martok infiltrator to retain influence and prolong Federation-Klingon war.
AR06 Gowron acknowledges the Martok changeling pushed conflict but refuses simply to reverse all annexations/war commitments, preserving independent Klingon political agency beyond Founder manipulation.
Assertions: Gowron is exonerated as the suspected changeling in this work; apparent Martok is a Founder impersonator distinct from the real Martok. Odo's prior Link-derived belief was sincere but false, a core regression case against promoting anomalous memory directly to identity fact.

## The Ship
TS01 A Federation survey team finds a crashed Jem'Hadar ship and claims salvage while five personnel are already dead/wounded from the crash/attack sequence.
TS02 Vorta Kilana demands return of the ship and repeatedly withholds the reason, while Sisko refuses because the vessel has high intelligence value and he distrusts her after deception.
TS03 Kilana sends Jem'Hadar aboard despite negotiations, reinforcing Sisko's distrust and producing a cycle in which each side treats disclosure as too risky.
TS04 Muniz dies from his wounds while Starfleet remains trapped in the ship.
TS05 A hidden/dying changeling is discovered aboard. Sisko realizes this Founder, not the ship itself, was the Dominion's essential objective and explains why Jem'Hadar refrained from destroying the vessel.
TS06 Kilana's Jem'Hadar kill themselves after allowing the Founder to die; Kilana retrieves remains and leaves.
TS07 Sisko and Kilana explicitly recognize that mutual distrust/strategic withholding contributed to deaths that might have been avoidable, while neither side had reliable grounds to trust the other at the outset.
Assertions: the hidden Founder and ship are separate strategic objects; casualty responsibility is multicausal rather than reducible to one negotiation refusal. Kilana's first-mission claim is false testimony, while her stated concern for the Founder is later corroborated by action.

## Looking for par'Mach in All the Wrong Places
PM01 Grilka returns and Quark develops genuine romantic interest beyond their earlier inheritance marriage; Worf separately becomes strongly attracted to Grilka.
PM02 Tumek rejects Worf as an acceptable suitor because Worf's discommendation/House status makes him socially unsuitable, while Grilka does not independently choose Worf.
PM03 Quark seeks Klingon courtship guidance; Worf provides it despite jealousy, and Dax interprets Worf's fixation while herself signaling attraction toward Worf.
PM04 Worf covertly controls Quark's bat'leth movement through a remote/VR system during Thopok's challenge. Quark's apparent combat skill is therefore partly Worf's performance, not Quark's unaided martial ability.
PM05 When the control system fails, Quark stalls through invented Ferengi ritual until Worf resumes control. Worf ultimately refuses to deliver a fatal finishing blow through Quark.
PM06 Grilka/Quark pursue their relationship. Worf/Dax separately fight and then begin their own intimate relationship.
PM07 Kira's pregnancy continues to strain Miles/Kira boundaries; the O'Briens and Kira explicitly recognize emotional complications and temporarily create distance rather than reclassifying parentage/romance.
Assertions: Quark/Grilka's present attraction is distinct from their prior coercive legal marriage; Worf's hidden motor control means observed Quark combat is collaborative/deceptive performance; Worf/Dax relationship emerges independently rather than as a transfer of Worf's attraction from Grilka by rule.
