## Rapture
RA01 A holosuite electrical accident alters Sisko's neural activity and is followed by unusually vivid visions/insights.
RA02 Sisko uses the vision state to locate the twenty-thousand-year-lost city of B'hala, materially corroborating at least some informational content beyond ordinary prior knowledge.
RA03 Sisko experiences visions spanning past/future imagery, including locusts moving toward Cardassia and a conviction that Bajor will be destroyed if it joins the Federation at that time.
RA04 Bashir identifies progressive neural depolarization and warns Sisko continued visions may kill him. Sisko consciously refuses surgery while competent because he values completing the experience.
RA05 When Sisko later becomes incapacitated, Bashir says he cannot override the refusal without consent of Sisko's closest relative. Jake authorizes surgery because he does not want his father to die.
RA06 Surgery restores Sisko's neural state and ends the visions; Sisko grieves the lost access but retains conviction in the warning.
RA07 Bajor's Council delays Federation accession based on Sisko's Emissary warning. Admiral Whatley objects but does not remove Sisko, partly because doing so could damage Federation-Bajor relations.
RA08 Winn, previously skeptical of Sisko's Emissary status, changes position after the B'hala discovery and accepts his spiritual authority, while her political relationship to that authority remains complex.
Assertions: Sisko's visions are neurologically associated with the accident yet include information that leads to independently verified B'hala; neither “mere pathology” nor complete prophetic omniscience exhausts the evidence. His refusal of treatment is explicit while competent, whereas Jake's later authorization occurs after incapacity. Bajor's delayed accession is a real political consequence of source-relative prophetic interpretation. The locust/Cardassia vision remains prospective imagery whose full referent is unresolved in this work.

## The Darkness and the Light
DL01 Former members of Kira's Shakaar resistance cell are murdered sequentially through remote/proxy attacks followed by messages directed at Kira.
DL02 Victims include people with different wartime roles, such as Fala, who secretly supplied information rather than fighting openly, complicating a simple “armed combatants only” victim set.
DL03 Kira investigates despite pregnancy and identifies Silaran Prin, a Cardassian civilian servant permanently injured in a Shakaar-cell bombing of Gul Pirak's residence.
DL04 Prin says Kira's cell treated all Cardassians on Bajor as legitimate targets regardless of military role and frames his murders as justice/revenge for civilians killed or maimed in that bombing.
DL05 Kira explicitly defends her Resistance-era position that Cardassian occupiers, armed or not, were part of the occupation and legitimate targets; Prin rejects this as indiscriminate murder.
DL06 Prin nevertheless murders Kira's former comrades in the present, including people not presently attacking him, while claiming a principled distinction between guilty and innocent.
DL07 Prin intends to kill Kira but preserve/remove the unborn Kirayoshi O'Brien because he regards the child as innocent.
DL08 Kira's pregnancy herbs neutralize the sedative Prin expects to incapacitate her. She surprises him, gains control of his weapon and kills him.
DL09 Kira's closing reflection rejects Prin's clean separation of darkness/light and acknowledges that innocence/guilt categories can be invoked self-servingly.
Assertions: Prin's account of the Pirak bombing and his injuries supplies victim testimony and motive, while Kira supplies combatant/occupation framing; the work intentionally contests categorical innocence/guilt rather than resolving the Resistance as uniformly justified or criminal. Prin's current murders are independently depicted and cannot be excused as mere historical testimony. Kirayoshi remains physically Kira's gestated but biologically/socially O'Brien child; Prin's planned extraction does not alter parentage.

## The Begotten
BG01 Quark sells Odo an infant Changeling damaged by tetryon radiation; Bashir confirms it is alive but its morphogenic matrix is unstable.
BG02 Odo assumes parental/teacher responsibility and initially rejects Mora's coercive laboratory methods because of his own painful developmental history.
BG03 Mora argues some controlled discomfort is necessary to induce shapeshifting; Odo eventually uses a low-level field and the infant begins forming simple shapes, then a face toward Odo.
BG04 Odo and Mora openly revisit their history. Mora admits he initially did not know Odo was a lifeform, while Odo acknowledges Mora's work enabled his development; Mora later concedes Odo formed a connection he himself never achieved.
BG05 The infant's matrix ultimately destabilizes from previously undetected radiation damage. Bashir/Mora cannot save it.
BG06 As it dies, the infant integrates into Odo's body and restores Odo's morphogenic matrix/shapeshifting ability.
BG07 Mora interprets the restoration as a gift from the infant; this is an emotionally plausible interpretation supported by timing/connection but the infant cannot verbally confirm intent.
BG08 Odo says the experience helps him understand what he meant to Mora and what Mora suffered when Odo left.
BG09 In the parallel birth plot, Kira delivers Kirayoshi O'Brien and experiences genuine attachment/grief around ending her gestational role while parentage remains with Keiko/Miles.
Assertions: the infant Changeling is a distinct local lifeform whose death causally restores Odo's shapeshifting by integration; exact intentionality of the “gift” remains interpretive rather than directly stated by the infant. Odo's restored morphology returns prior capacity without erasing his solid-period memories/relationships. Odo/Mora reconciliation revises each person's interpretation of past coercive teaching without making those methods retrospectively non-coercive.

## For the Uniform
FU01 Eddington continues Maquis operations and deliberately manipulates Sisko through personal rivalry, false signals and a cascade virus left in Defiant systems.
FU02 The Maquis use cobalt diselenide biogenic weapons to render Cardassian-colonized planets lethal to Cardassians while remaining habitable to humans, forcing evacuations.
FU03 Sisko recognizes Eddington's self-conception through `Les Misérables` and deliberately decides to occupy the villain role Eddington expects.
FU04 Without prior Starfleet authorization, Sisko launches trilithium-resin torpedoes into Solosos Three, making it uninhabitable to humans for decades while Cardassians can inhabit it.
FU05 Sisko threatens to repeat the tactic against additional Maquis colonies; Eddington surrenders himself and the remaining biogenic weapons to stop him.
FU06 Resettlement swaps displaced Maquis/Cardassian populations onto planets made habitable to the opposite population; the situation remains unstable.
FU07 Dax explicitly asks whether Sisko cleared his plan with Starfleet; he admits he did not.
Assertions: Eddington and Sisko both deliberately weaponize planetary habitability against civilian settlements in this conflict, though with different agents/targets and strategic aims. Sisko's action succeeds in forcing surrender but success does not itself settle legality/ethics. The Maquis grievance, Eddington's betrayal and Sisko's escalating pursuit remain separate propositions rather than one moral verdict.

## In Purgatory's Shadow
PS01 Garak receives a hidden message traced to Enabran Tain and travels with Worf toward a Dominion internment site; they are captured.
PS02 The camp contains the real General Martok, who says he was abducted roughly two years earlier. This establishes that the Martok active in Alpha Quadrant events during that period was a Changeling replacement.
PS03 The camp also contains the real Julian Bashir, who says he was abducted over a month earlier while attending a medical conference.
PS04 Blood testing and shared captivity support the prisoner's self-identification as Bashir, while the DS9 doctor still operating on the station is therefore a separate Changeling impersonator.
PS05 Tain reveals/acknowledges Garak as his son during his final illness, moving their father-son relation from long-suspected/subtextual status to direct source testimony; Tain dies in the camp.
PS06 Prisoners plan escape while a large Dominion fleet approaches the wormhole.
PS07 On DS9, Sisko decides to seal the wormhole rather than allow invasion, accepting that Worf/Garak may be trapped in the Gamma Quadrant.
PS08 Changeling-Bashir has infiltrated the technical effort and sabotages the graviton emitter array.
PS09 The attempted sealing fails and the wormhole opens, allowing the Dominion fleet to enter the Alpha Quadrant.
PS10 Ziyal independently refuses Dukat's demand that she leave DS9 for Cardassia and chooses to wait for Garak, preserving her adult agency despite family conflict.
Assertions: real Bashir and Changeling-Bashir are distinct local entities with overlapping social-role occupancy; Martok likewise requires separation from the prior Founder-Matok impersonator. Tain/Garak paternity is directly acknowledged before Tain's death. The failure to seal the wormhole is sabotage by Changeling-Bashir, not evidence the underlying scientific method was necessarily impossible. Blood tests help here but are themselves treated by prisoners as imperfect authentication, preserving prior security uncertainty.
