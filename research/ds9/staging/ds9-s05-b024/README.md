## Ferengi Love Songs
FLS01 Quark returns to Ferenginar while still blacklisted by the Ferengi Commerce Authority and discovers Grand Nagus Zek and Ishka are secretly in a romantic relationship.
FLS02 Zek says Ishka first helped his tongo play and then became a regular correspondent and partner; Ishka is demonstrably supplying economic advice while their relationship remains hidden from the public.
FLS03 Brunt argues that Ishka's influence threatens Ferengi male political/economic dominance and recruits Quark to destroy the relationship, offering restoration of Quark's business license as payment.
FLS04 Quark deliberately feeds Zek false claims that Ishka kept illicit profits and plans female political domination. Zek ends the relationship; Brunt restores Quark's license.
FLS05 The separation exposes that Zek's memory is failing and that Ishka had been supplying substantial financial guidance. Quark's first day as First Clerk coincides with a 199-point market decline while Zek struggles to remember figures and decisions.
FLS06 Brunt's underlying plan is to expose Zek as cognitively failing and position himself to become Nagus; Quark recognizes that Brunt is willing to destabilize the economy for personal political gain.
FLS07 Quark reverses his earlier sabotage, reunites Zek and Ishka, and reveals that his successful financial advice to Zek came from Ishka. Zek acknowledges memory problems and agrees to seek Vulcan medical treatment.
FLS08 Zek retains Quark as First Clerk but Ishka becomes the acknowledged source of the financial advice. Ishka states support for female equality while also affirming her personal loyalty to Zek.
FLS09 In the parallel Rom/Leeta dispute, Rom initially insists Leeta sign the Ferengi Waiver of Property and Profit; Leeta refuses and the wedding is cancelled.
FLS10 Rom resolves the dispute by giving his latinum to the Bajoran War Orphans Fund, making his demand to protect his property moot; he and Leeta reconcile and proceed toward marriage without her accepting the waiver.
Assertions: Ishka's role is real informal economic governance but is not silently promoted to a formal office. Zek's memory impairment is directly depicted and acknowledged by Zek, while diagnosis and long-term prognosis remain outside this work. Quark knowingly manipulates Zek/Ishka for personal gain and later knowingly undoes the scheme; neither action erases the other. Ferengi female property restrictions are institutional rules represented as contested within the story, not neutral biological facts.

## Soldiers of the Empire
SE01 Martok remains affected by two years of Dominion imprisonment, explicitly says his reflexes have dulled, rejects an artificial replacement for his lost eye, and seeks dangerous training with disabled holosuite safeties.
SE02 Martok receives command of the Rotarran to locate the missing B'Moth and asks Worf to serve as First Officer because he trusts Worf after their prison-camp experience.
SE03 Worf recounts that during imprisonment he once intended to let the Jem'Hadar kill him, but Martok recognized the intention in a moment of `tova'dok`; being seen by Martok caused Worf to continue fighting.
SE04 The Rotarran has suffered repeated defeats for seven months. Crew records, conversation, fatalism, drinking, and interpersonal violence all support severely degraded morale rather than a single homogeneous Klingon attitude.
SE05 Martok passes up an apparently favorable engagement with a Jem'Hadar patrol, prioritizing the search mission and risk avoidance. Dax and several crew members interpret this as worsening the morale crisis.
SE06 After finding the B'Moth disabled inside Cardassian territory, Martok refuses a rescue despite evidence of possible survivors, citing High Council orders and the likelihood of a Jem'Hadar trap.
SE07 Worf invokes Klingon command custom and challenges Martok as unfit. The challenge becomes physical; Worf's action functions as both a legal command challenge and an attempt to force Martok to re-engage with warrior duty.
SE08 Martok returns to combat leadership, the Rotarran destroys a Jem'Hadar ship and rescues thirty-five Klingon survivors, and the High Council later commends the action despite the border violation.
SE09 Martok later recognizes Worf's challenge as an attempt to remind him of duty rather than simple disloyalty and explicitly connects it to Martok's earlier intervention that kept Worf fighting in the prison camp.
SE10 Martok invites Worf into the House of Martok as warrior and brother; Worf accepts the new familial/social affiliation without erasing his prior House-of-Mogh history.
Assertions: Martok's choices are evidence of trauma- and defeat-shaped command behavior, but a global diagnosis or simple essential label of cowardice is not required. Worf's challenge is simultaneously coercive, institutionally legible within Klingon command practice, and remedial in intent. House-of-Martok membership is a new social/legal relation, not an identity replacement.

## Children of Time
CT01 The Defiant encounters Gaia and approximately eight thousand inhabitants who identify themselves as descendants of the Defiant crew after a temporal accident that, from their history, strands the ship two centuries in the past.
CT02 Yedrin identifies himself as Yedrin Dax and carries the Dax symbiont, which has passed through Jadzia's descendants for multiple generations; he possesses memories of the stranded crew through symbiont continuity.
CT03 Genetic and historical evidence within the work supports multiple descendant lines, including O'Brien and Worf-linked communities. The Sons of Mogh include both descendants by blood and members by cultural choice.
CT04 In the Gaia history, Kira dies weeks after the crash because the stranded Defiant lacks the treatment needed for her neural injury.
CT05 Yedrin proposes a quantum-duplication solution that would send a duplicate Defiant into the past while allowing the present ship to leave. Dax later discovers he fabricated the original sensor logs: no duplicate would be created and the present crew would actually be stranded.
CT06 Yedrin admits the deception and argues that preserving eight thousand existing descendants justifies recreating the accident. Sisko rejects Yedrin's unilateral attempt to choose Kira's death and the crew's permanent separation from their families.
CT07 Kira interprets the situation through her religious beliefs, concluding that her path with the Prophets may end on Gaia and later volunteering to accept death if doing so preserves the descendants. O'Brien explicitly rejects reliance on the Prophets for his family's welfare.
CT08 After interacting with the community, the crew collectively decides to recreate the accident despite the personal costs. The decision therefore changes after direct engagement rather than being forced solely by Yedrin's deception or Kira's religion.
CT09 The Defiant unexpectedly veers away from the temporal anomaly; Gaia and its inhabitants disappear from the ship's observable present.
CT10 Present Odo reports that future-Gaia Odo linked with him before departure, transferring knowledge/memories, and that future Odo secretly changed the flight plan specifically to prevent Kira's death.
CT11 Kira challenges the morality of sacrificing eight thousand Gaia inhabitants for her; present Odo does not claim certainty that future Odo's choice was right.
Assertions: Yedrin Dax is a distinct local entity whose Dax continuity is evidence-rich but must not be globally merged with Jadzia or other hosts by the series worker. Future Odo and present Odo are temporally divergent local entities; a later Link transmits memory/knowledge without by itself establishing numerical identity. Kira's statements about destiny and the Prophets are religious interpretation, not independent proof that her death was cosmically mandated. Gaia's erased/overwritten temporal existence remains a temporal-ontology problem rather than being flattened into either “never existed” or ordinary historical survival.

## Blaze of Glory
BG01 A coded Maquis message claims cloaked missiles have been launched toward Cardassia. Klingon-provided cloaking devices and known Maquis weapons capability make the threat operationally plausible to Sisko and allies.
BG02 The apparent missile threat is initially supported only by the intercepted message and Eddington's deliberate maintenance of the story; repeated searches do not locate the weapons.
BG03 Eddington, imprisoned after the Maquis collapse, describes the Dominion's destruction of the Maquis and alternates between blaming Sisko/Federation policy and blaming himself for leading followers toward an unwinnable conflict.
BG04 Sisko brings Eddington into the Badlands because he believes Eddington can locate the launch site and abort the missiles. Eddington eventually agrees to guide and assist while preserving hostility toward Sisko.
BG05 On Athos IV, Sisko and Eddington discover Maquis bodies and later living survivors, including Eddington's wife Rebecca Sullivan, at a fallback settlement under Jem'Hadar attack.
BG06 Rebecca explicitly reveals that no missiles were launched. The transmission was a deception intended to let Eddington know the survivors had reached the fallback position; Eddington needed Sisko's help to evacuate them.
BG07 The false missile claim therefore generated real Federation/Klingon strategic concern but never described an actual retaliatory launch. It must remain manufactured testimony rather than retroactively becoming a failed world-state event.
BG08 Eddington remains behind to cover the survivors' escape and is killed fighting the Jem'Hadar. His death follows his explicit self-identification with the Maquis cause and his refusal to return to prison.
BG09 Sisko's closing assessment is deliberately mixed: he calls Eddington complicated, recalls having called him a traitor, and also characterizes him as profoundly loyal to the Maquis through the end.
BG10 In the secondary station thread, Nog confronts Klingon personnel who had been dismissing him; his willingness to stand up to them changes their treatment of him. This is social interaction evidence, not a universal rule about earning Klingon respect.
Assertions: the Maquis missile strike is a fabricated threat, not a STABLE attack fact. Eddington's deception has a rescue objective but still manipulates Sisko and the Federation response. Sisko's final “loyal” judgment is evaluative testimony and does not erase Eddington's Starfleet betrayal, earlier Maquis violence, or the factual rescue sacrifice.

## Empok Nor
EN01 O'Brien leads a mixed engineering/security salvage team to abandoned Cardassian station Empok Nor to recover a non-replicable plasma distribution manifold needed by DS9.
EN02 Garak accompanies the team because Cardassian facilities are commonly booby-trapped against non-Cardassians and his Cardassian biology/experience provides access advantages.
EN03 The team discovers stasis tubes from the Cardassian Third Battalion. Two soldiers awaken, destroy the runabout, and begin attacking the salvage party.
EN04 Garak discovers that a soldier received a massive dose of a psychotropic compound designed to amplify Cardassian xenophobic tendencies. He infers a military experiment intended to increase aggression that became uncontrollable.
EN05 Garak is also exposed to the compound. His behavior escalates from suspiciousness and aggressive interpretation to active pursuit and violence against the team, while he increasingly frames killing as exhilarating and revealing of “true nature.”
EN06 Garak repeatedly invokes O'Brien's Setlik III combat history, arguing that O'Brien and Garak are both fundamentally killers. O'Brien explicitly rejects that identity claim and identifies himself as an engineer rather than a soldier.
EN07 O'Brien recognizes that Garak is drug-affected yet also concludes that Garak cannot presently be reasoned with and may have to be stopped lethally to protect the remaining team.
EN08 O'Brien defeats Garak without killing him. Bashir later neutralizes the psychotropic compound and says the drug allowed Garak's worst parts to take over and that Garak was not in control of his actions.
EN09 Garak later expresses regret for Amaro's death and expects an inquest. O'Brien agrees to convey Garak's regret to Amaro's wife rather than declaring the event morally or legally resolved.
EN10 The salvage mission nevertheless succeeds in recovering the plasma manifold for DS9.
Assertions: the psychotropic influence is directly supported, but the episode contains competing interpretations of what that influence means: intoxicated Garak calls violence his true nature while Bashir medically describes impaired control. The worker therefore does not convert either statement into a totalizing identity fact. O'Brien's Setlik III history is real prior combat experience; his present refusal of “killer” as defining identity is equally real self-concept evidence. Garak-under-drug remains causally continuous with Garak while responsibility for specific acts is appropriately preserved for later ethical/legal reconciliation rather than declared absent.

## Cross-work neutral observations
- Institutions repeatedly shape choices without determining them: FCA gender/property law, Klingon command custom, temporal-community obligation, Starfleet/Maquis conflict, and Cardassian military experimentation all create pressures that characters can resist, exploit, or reinterpret.
- Several apparently authoritative states are overturned by later evidence: Zek's public competence masks memory decline and informal assistance; Yedrin's sensor logs are fabricated; the Maquis missile launch never occurred; Garak's “true nature” claim is made under a cognition-altering compound.
- Identity continuity is repeatedly multidimensional rather than binary: Dax across descendants/hosts, Odo across temporal divergence and Link-memory transfer, O'Brien as former soldier/current engineer, Garak under pharmacological alteration, and Ferengi social roles under contested gender rules.
