## In the Cards
IC01 Station morale is depressed by the Dominion war threat. Jake seeks an old Willie Mays card specifically as a gift to improve Sisko's mood; Nog reluctantly finances the attempt.
IC02 Elias Giger acquires the card and describes a cellular-regeneration/entertainment theory intended to defeat death. The work depicts his experiment and conviction, not independent validation of the theory.
IC03 Jake and Nog perform a chain of favors and trades to obtain Giger's requested equipment. Their actions incidentally improve the moods or circumstances of multiple station residents.
IC04 Giger disappears. Jake and Nog make increasingly speculative inferences about Winn before learning that Weyoun detained Giger and them because their unusual activities looked like a possible security/intelligence operation.
IC05 Jake invents a Starfleet-Intelligence/time-travelling-Willie-Mays cover story under questioning. Weyoun rejects it and ultimately accepts the mundane gift explanation, returning Giger and the card.
IC06 Winn consults Sisko in his capacity as Emissary about Dominion nonaggression negotiations. Sisko advises delaying rather than presenting a prophetic revelation.
IC07 Sisko's closing reflection says the strategic situation has not improved, but Jake's gift and the web of small favors have improved station morale.
Assertions: Giger's immortality mechanism remains personal scientific testimony rather than established fact. Jake's time-traveller story is deliberate false testimony under pressure. Sisko's advice to Winn is political/religious-role counsel, not independent evidence of Prophet instruction. Morale improvement is depicted without being promoted to a military turning point.

## Call to Arms
CA01 Dominion reinforcement traffic through the wormhole increases while the Romulans sign a Dominion nonaggression pact. Starfleet decides to block reinforcement by mining the wormhole entrance.
CA02 Rom designs self-replicating cloaked mines whose replication makes ordinary clearing impractical; Defiant crews deploy the field while DS9 prepares for attack.
CA03 Sisko and Weyoun negotiate, but Sisko explicitly treats the exchange as delaying theater while both sides prepare for war.
CA04 Sisko uses his Emissary standing to urge Bajor to sign a Dominion nonaggression pact because the Federation cannot presently protect Bajor. Bajoran personnel are ordered off DS9 before battle.
CA05 Rom and Leeta marry before evacuation. Rom remains to assist O'Brien and later states that he is secretly working for Starfleet during the coming occupation.
CA06 The Dominion/Cardassian assault damages DS9 while the minefield is completed and activated. Sisko evacuates Federation forces rather than hold the station after the strategic objective is met.
CA07 A Starfleet-Klingon task force simultaneously destroys Dominion shipyards at Torros III, making the DS9 battle part of a wider operation rather than an isolated defeat.
CA08 Jake chooses to remain on the occupied station as a reporter. Sisko refuses to endanger the entire Defiant crew to retrieve his adult son and explicitly recognizes Jake's capacity to make that choice.
CA09 Dukat, Weyoun and Dominion forces occupy the station. Odo remains Chief of Security, Kira remains under Bajor's nonaggression arrangement, and the Dominion makes dismantling the minefield a priority.
Assertions: Bajor's pact is a survival policy made under constrained protection, not evidence of ideological Dominion alignment. Jake's remaining is his own adult choice, not Sisko's abandonment decision. Occupation authority is divided among Cardassian, Dominion, Bajoran-treaty and Odo security structures rather than one undifferentiated regime.

## A Time to Stand
ATS01 Three months into the war, Federation casualties are severe; only fourteen ships of the Seventh Fleet return from an engagement that began with 112.
ATS02 On occupied DS9, Dominion officials state that Bajor's treaty remains in force while Cardassian/Dominion policies curtail station security autonomy. Dukat refuses to rearm Bajoran security officers because he does not trust them.
ATS03 Weyoun restricts Jake's ability to transmit war reporting and presses him about balance. The source supports censorship/pressure; Weyoun's statement that he does not want propaganda is his own characterization of the policy.
ATS04 Kira and Odo initially pursue accommodation and incremental leverage. Odo uses Founder status to regain Bajoran security authority and accepts a seat on the station ruling council despite Kira's warning that his presence legitimizes Dominion control.
ATS05 Starfleet assigns Sisko's crew a captured Jem'Hadar attack ship for a covert strike on an Alpha Quadrant ketracel-white storage facility.
ATS06 The crew operates the alien vessel under severe ergonomic/technical constraints. Garak joins because the mission enters Cardassian territory; human use of the Jem'Hadar neural interface causes pain and adaptation problems.
ATS07 USS Centaur mistakes the disguised craft for an enemy ship and attacks. Sisko avoids destroying the Federation ship while protecting the mission and then allows Centaur to flee approaching Jem'Hadar.
ATS08 The crew infiltrates the storage facility's supply exchange by substituting an explosive canister. The explosion destroys the facility and lowers the security net, but also damages the captured ship and destroys its warp capability.
Assertions: Dominion statements about treaty friendship remain political testimony measured against depicted coercive policies. Odo's council membership simultaneously produces concrete security gains and symbolic regime validation. The captured-ship mission depends on intentional identity/signature deception without making the Starfleet crew Jem'Hadar entities.

## Rocks and Shoals
RS01 The damaged captured Dominion ship crashes after entering a dark-matter nebula; Dax suffers serious injuries and the stranded crew must survive without normal ship support.
RS02 A separate Jem'Hadar unit is also stranded and has nearly exhausted its ketracel-white. Their Vorta, Keevan, is critically wounded.
RS03 Bashir treats Keevan under a temporary exchange. Keevan then offers Sisko his communications system and surrender in exchange for exact information that will allow Starfleet to kill Keevan's own Jem'Hadar before their drug deprivation becomes uncontrollable.
RS04 Sisko accepts defensive preparations but continues seeking another outcome and offers the Jem'Hadar surrender, sedation and medical stasis.
RS05 Remata'Klan reveals that he already suspects Keevan's deliberate tactical betrayal. He nevertheless refuses surrender because he understands loyalty to the Vorta/Founders as constitutive from conception and says his life is not his to give up.
RS06 The Jem'Hadar advance knowingly into the ambush and are killed; one Starfleet servicemember, Gordon, also dies. Sisko orders burial rather than treating the deaths as a simple triumph.
RS07 On occupied DS9, Vedek Yassim confronts Kira for accommodating Dominion rule and later commits a public fatal protest while declaring that evil must be opposed.
RS08 Kira reassesses her own role, calls herself a collaborator, and decides to begin active resistance. Odo initially warns of crackdown but then agrees to help plan discreetly.
Assertions: Keevan's betrayal and Remata'Klan's obedience are separate agency records. Jem'Hadar conditioning/loyalty structures constrain choice but do not make their expressed awareness disappear. Yassim's self-killing is evidence of her protest and its effect on Kira, not proof that self-sacrifice was required or divinely sanctioned. Kira's earlier accommodation and later resistance are sequential choices under changing moral appraisal.

## Sons and Daughters
SD01 Alexander joins the Rotarran as a young Klingon warrior. Worf discovers his son aboard and immediately judges him unprepared for combat; Alexander states that he did not enlist to please Worf.
SD02 Their conflict reopens earlier family history. Alexander explicitly frames Worf as having sent him away and Worf's training becomes entangled with shame over Alexander's poor martial performance.
SD03 Martok challenges Worf's treatment of Alexander and points toward Worf's own paternal absence rather than accepting a simple deficient-son explanation.
SD04 Alexander continues serving despite errors, participates in emergency repairs during combat, and the crew's treatment of him changes from ridicule toward affiliation.
SD05 Worf and Alexander acknowledge mutual obligations rather than resolving the conflict by declaring one wholly at fault. Worf says he will teach Alexander to be a warrior and Alexander will teach Worf to be a father.
SD06 Alexander is welcomed into the House of Martok, creating a new social/familial affiliation without erasing his biological relation to Worf or prior family history.
SD07 On occupied DS9, Ziyal returns and is openly happy to see both Dukat and Kira. Her art and self-description emphasize bridging Bajoran/Cardassian worlds.
SD08 Kira refuses Dukat's attempt to convert Ziyal's affection into a bond between Kira and Dukat. Kira later tells Ziyal she cannot attend Dukat's party; Ziyal asks not to be forced to choose between them.
SD09 Kira accepts that Dukat is Ziyal's father and promises to stand with Ziyal without requiring Ziyal to sever that relationship.
Assertions: Alexander's House-of-Martok membership is a new affiliation, not replacement identity. Worf's paternal failure and Alexander's combat limitations are both source-supported without reducing either person to them. Ziyal's simultaneous love for Dukat and attachment to Kira is not logically inconsistent; Kira distinguishes support for Ziyal from reconciliation with Dukat.

## Cross-work observations
- The war transition repeatedly creates nested authorities rather than simple binaries: Bajoran treaty neutrality, Dominion occupation, Cardassian ambition, Odo's Founder/security leverage, Starfleet clandestine operations, Klingon command law and journalistic autonomy overlap.
- Source framing repeatedly matters. Weyoun's statements about balanced reporting, Giger's immortality theory, Sisko's Emissary advice, Remata'Klan's account of loyalty and Jake's reporting each establish different kinds of evidence rather than interchangeable world-state facts.
- Family and social identity remain multidimensional under conflict: Sisko/Jake adult autonomy, Worf/Alexander paternal repair, House-of-Martok affiliation, and Kira/Ziyal chosen attachment coexist with institutional and biological relations.
