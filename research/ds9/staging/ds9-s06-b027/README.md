## Statistical Probabilities
SP01 Jack, Lauren, Patrick and Sarina are genetically engineered adults whose childhood procedures produced severe unintended impairments. Bashir is also genetically enhanced but has a different developmental history and functioning.
SP02 The group detects fine-grained behavioral and political patterns from broadcast material and develops a probabilistic model of the Dominion war. Their analysis predicts Federation defeat, approximately 900 billion deaths in a long war, and a much lower casualty total after early surrender followed by later successful rebellion.
SP03 Bashir becomes persuaded by the model and presents surrender recommendations to Starfleet. Sisko rejects the proposal because its assumptions cannot justify abandoning agency, obligations and unmodeled possibilities.
SP04 After Starfleet rejects surrender, Jack/Lauren/Patrick plan to give Dominion officials classified Starfleet battle plans to force a rapid conquest. Bashir refuses and calls the act treason.
SP05 Sarina, previously minimally communicative, independently acts to help Bashir stop the information transfer. Her choice is a material variable the predictive group failed to model.
SP06 Bashir recognizes that he overidentified with proving that the group could contribute and allowed confidence in prediction to outrun the model's limits.
SP07 Sisko nevertheless concludes their work has usefully challenged Federation complacency about the Dominion. The group leaves still interested in finding strategies to win rather than simply being dismissed as useless.
Assertions: statistical prediction is evidence-backed inference, not future fact. High confidence plus large-scale modeling does not erase individual agency or unknown variables. Sarina's intervention is direct disconfirming evidence against the model's practical certainty. Genetic enhancement, disability, legal restriction and moral agency remain separate dimensions.

## The Magnificent Ferengi
MF01 Ishka is captured by the Dominion, and the Grand Nagus authorizes Quark to assemble a Ferengi rescue team in exchange for substantial payment.
MF02 Quark recruits Rom, Nog, Leck, Gaila and Brunt. Their motives vary materially: family obligation, Starfleet duty, challenge-seeking, money and self-interest are not interchangeable.
MF03 Sisko provides captured Vorta Keevan for a prisoner exchange. Keevan cooperates because returning to the Dominion may improve his prospects, but he repeatedly looks for escape opportunities.
MF04 The team initially attempts military training and performs poorly. They switch to a plan built around Ferengi strengths: negotiation, deception, engineering and controlled exchange conditions.
MF05 Keevan attempts escape and is shot/killed. Nog verifies death. The team uses neural stimulators to animate Keevan's corpse sufficiently to create the appearance that he can walk during the exchange.
MF06 Quark forces Vorta Yelgrun to remove Jem'Hadar support and move the exchange to Ferengi-chosen terrain. The staged Keevan body breaks down during the exchange, but the Ferengi recover Ishka and capture Yelgrun.
MF07 The team returns Yelgrun to Starfleet as a prisoner rather than treating the rescue as authorization for private execution.
Assertions: Keevan's animated corpse is not a restored living Keevan; the source explicitly distinguishes reflexive neural stimulation from life. Ferengi success depends on changing tactics rather than proving an essential species military trait. Individual motives remain source-relative rather than merged into a single heroic intention.

## Waltz
WZ01 Sisko transports captured Dukat for a war-crimes proceeding when their ship is attacked and crashes. Sisko and Dukat are isolated while rescue attempts proceed.
WZ02 Dukat converses repeatedly with apparent Weyoun, Damar and Kira figures who are not externally present. These interlocutors articulate competing aspects of his self-justification and hostility; the source frames them as Dukat's hallucinated/subjective companions rather than independent witnesses.
WZ03 Dukat repeatedly seeks Sisko's recognition that he was a benevolent or misunderstood administrator of occupied Bajor, while minimizing or rationalizing executions and coercion.
WZ04 Dukat acknowledges ordering mass retaliatory executions and argues these were justified responses to resistance violence. Sisko does not accept Dukat's moral reframing.
WZ05 Under pressure, Dukat explicitly admits hatred of Bajorans, asserts Cardassian racial/cultural superiority and says he should have killed every Bajoran rather than trying to gain their appreciation.
WZ06 Sisko's strategy deliberately pushes Dukat toward this admission. Dukat subsequently escapes and announces that Bajor is now his enemy and that he has unfinished business there.
Assertions: the Weyoun/Damar/Kira interlocutors are subjective manifestations unless separately corroborated. Dukat's statements are direct self-revelatory testimony and plans, while his causal/moral history of the occupation still requires independent event evidence. Sisko's final judgment of Dukat as evil is an evaluative assertion grounded in the encounter, not a replacement for event-level records.

## Who Mourns for Morn?
WM01 Morn is initially reported dead after an apparent accident. Quark is told that Morn left him property and becomes entangled with several people claiming prior relationships and rights to a hidden thousand-brick latinum fortune.
WM02 Quark receives mutually inconsistent testimony from Larell, the brothers Hain/Nahsk and others about past robbery, betrayal and ownership. Their claims are self-interested and not accepted solely because repeated.
WM03 The group pressures and threatens Quark because his inheritance status makes him necessary to retrieve the bank shipment. Quark attempts to survive by negotiating a five-way split.
WM04 Odo intervenes when the claimants turn on one another. The shipment contains only gold with the latinum removed.
WM05 Morn returns alive and reveals the reported death was staged. Quark infers that Morn used the false death/inheritance setup to keep former associates off balance until law enforcement could remove them.
WM06 The missing latinum has been stored inside Morn's second stomach, confirming that the bank shipment was never the actual remaining fortune.
Assertions: the episode's initial death state is false information within a deliberate deception. Morn's hologram at Quark's bar is a non-interactive representation and not Morn. Ownership claims remain contested testimony until the scam structure is revealed. Staged death does not create a duplicate or replacement person.

## Far Beyond the Stars
FB01 After war losses and exhaustion, Sisko experiences a prolonged alternate/visionary life as Benny Russell, a Black science-fiction writer in 1950s New York. Bashir later reports abnormal neural patterns during the episode and normal patterns after it ends.
FB02 Benny's workplace reproduces DS9 familiar faces in different social identities, but the work does not establish ordinary physical travel or literal global identity between these figures and Sisko's contemporaries.
FB03 Benny writes a story centered on Benjamin Sisko as a Black commander of a future space station. Editors/corporate gatekeepers resist publication because the protagonist is Black and because Benny himself is Black.
FB04 The episode depicts systemic racism through exclusion from author photographs, editorial suppression, police violence and employment retaliation. These are events within the Benny frame, not neutral comments outside it.
FB05 Benny's story is initially accepted only through a framing device, then the magazine issue is pulped and Benny is fired. He insists that destroying publication cannot destroy the idea/future he created.
FB06 A preacher-like figure within Benny's experience says he speaks with the Prophets and calls Benny both dreamer and dream. This is anomalous/vision-frame testimony rather than an independent external narrator.
FB07 Sisko awakens after only minutes of external elapsed time despite experiencing an extended Benny narrative. His neural patterns return to normal without a conventional mechanism being established.
FB08 Sisko explicitly wonders whether Benny was more than a dream and whether DS9 reality might instead be Benny's imagination. The episode ends without resolving the ontological direction.
Assertions: preserve the Benny frame as an altered/visionary reality with unresolved ontology. Sisko's final speculation is genuine epistemic uncertainty, not license to promote Benny-as-objective-creator or DS9-as-fiction-within-fiction to stable truth. Social events inside the Benny frame remain real evidence about that experienced frame even when its cosmological status is unresolved.

## Cross-work observations
- Predictive certainty, hallucinated dialogue, fraudulent death, corpse animation and visionary experience all demonstrate why source frames must remain explicit. Surface vividness is not the same thing as world-state authority.
- Individual agency repeatedly defeats simple system models: Sarina disrupts predictive surrender, Ferengi rescue succeeds by abandoning imposed tactics, Sisko elicits Dukat's own admission, Morn exploits others' assumptions, and Benny insists on the persistence of an idea despite institutional suppression.
- Institutional exclusion appears in very different forms and must not be conflated: genetic law, Dominion conflict, occupation responsibility, criminal opportunism and 1950s racism have distinct mechanisms and evidence.
