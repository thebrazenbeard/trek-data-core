## One Little Ship
OLS01 Defiant investigates a subspace-compression phenomenon while Rubicon, carrying Dax, O'Brien and Bashir, enters the anomaly and is physically miniaturized; the script explicitly says even spacing within their subatomic structures shrinks.
OLS02 The miniaturized crew retain autobiographical memory, agency, relationships and bodily continuity. Scale alteration is therefore a material-state change, not evidence of replacement persons.
OLS03 Jem'Hadar board and capture Defiant after the anomaly masks their approach. Sisko's bridge crew and other survivors are confined while the tiny Rubicon remains undetected.
OLS04 Alpha-quadrant-bred Jem'Hadar under Kudak'Etan clash with Gamma-quadrant veteran Ixtana'Rax. Gelnon speaks of a new race potentially supplanting the old guard; Ixtana'Rax says superiority has not been proven.
OLS05 Claims of Alpha/Gamma Jem'Hadar superiority are competing institutional/species testimony, not an objectively settled biological hierarchy in this work.
OLS06 The tiny crew infiltrate Defiant through ship systems/plasma ducts, coordinate with prisoners, sabotage Jem'Hadar control and materially assist the ship's recapture.
OLS07 Rubicon later traverses the anomaly appropriately and the crew return to normal scale. Restoration is physical reversal of the scale condition, not identity restoration after death/replacement.
Assertions: miniaturized and restored Dax/O'Brien/Bashir remain causally continuous local persons. Alpha-versus-Gamma Jem'Hadar superiority remains contested testimony. Physical scale must not be mistaken for a personhood or identity dimension unless later evidence makes it relevant.

## Honor Among Thieves
HAT01 O'Brien operates undercover as `Connelly` for Starfleet Intelligence to identify an Orion Syndicate mole after multiple previous agents are killed.
HAT02 Bilby accepts O'Brien into his organization and personally vouches for him, making Bilby liable under Syndicate rules if O'Brien betrays the group.
HAT03 Bilby kills Yint over defective disruptors, directly establishing lethal criminal enforcement rather than merely O'Brien's intelligence characterization of the Syndicate.
HAT04 Bilby reveals that a Starfleet weather-control employee sold the identities of undercover agents. The conspiracy therefore includes an actual Federation insider rather than only external infiltration.
HAT05 The Syndicate cooperates with Dominion/Vorta interests on a plan to assassinate a Klingon ambassador using Klingon weapons so the killing can be attributed to an internal Klingon political rival and strain the Federation-Klingon alliance.
HAT06 Starfleet handler Chadwick arranges for Klingon forces to be ready for the assassins; O'Brien recognizes this is likely to kill Bilby as part of closing the operation.
HAT07 O'Brien reveals his true identity to Bilby and attempts to save him. Bilby refuses escape/surrender because he believes the Syndicate would retaliate against his family; he chooses to enter the Klingon trap so Raimus will believe he died ignorant of O'Brien's betrayal.
HAT08 Bilby's choice is meaningful but materially constrained by a credible threat to his family; it should not be coded as unconstrained voluntary suicide or simple obedience.
HAT09 O'Brien later says he killed Bilby as surely as if he pulled the trigger. Bashir rejects that self-attribution and invokes duty/context. These are competing moral interpretations, not evidence that O'Brien literally fired the fatal shot.
Assertions: O'Brien's undercover identity is operational deception, not a second person. The attempted ambassador assassination is a Dominion/Syndicate false-attribution operation, not a Gowron order. Bilby's death has distributed causal responsibility across Syndicate coercion, Starfleet counteroperation, Bilby's protective choice and the Klingon ambush; the source does not require one exclusive moral cause.

## Change of Heart
COH01 Worf and Dax are assigned a covert rendezvous with Cardassian defector Lasaran, whose intelligence is expected to be strategically important.
COH02 Dax is gravely wounded en route and requires surgery. She explicitly tells Worf to continue without her because the mission/duty outranks their marriage and says she understands the consequences.
COH03 Worf initially continues toward the rendezvous but reverses course and carries Dax to safety, choosing his spouse over completion of the mission.
COH04 Lasaran is killed while attempting to reach the rendezvous. Sisko later confirms Worf could have completed the mission and says the lost intelligence might have saved millions of lives.
COH05 Sisko formally treats Worf's decision as professionally wrong, records it in Worf's service record, predicts it may prevent independent command, and orders Worf/Dax not to undertake future solo missions together.
COH06 Sisko separately tells Worf that as a widower he understands the choice and believes he might not have left Jennifer either. Institutional judgment and personal empathy therefore coexist without cancelling one another.
COH07 Dax survives and recovers. Her survival is a consequence of Worf's choice, while the precise counterfactual value of Lasaran's intelligence remains unknowable despite Sisko's estimate.
Assertions: Worf knowingly violates mission priority to save Dax; this is direct agency evidence. Sisko's judgment is Starfleet command assessment, not metaphysical proof of the uniquely correct moral action. Dax's request to be left establishes informed testimony/consent to mission continuation but does not compel Worf's compliance as a matter of identity or personhood.

## Wrongs Darker Than Death or Night
WD01 Dukat contacts Kira on the anniversary of her mother Meru's death and claims that Meru was his lover during the Occupation.
WD02 Kira enters an Orb-of-Time experience and lives within an Occupation-era Terok Nor frame under the name `Luma`. Preserve this as an Orb-mediated temporal/vision experience unless later governed reconciliation resolves mechanism.
WD03 Kira encounters Meru among Bajoran women selected under coercive occupation conditions for Cardassian officers. Meru is separated from Taban and her children under severe material pressure.
WD04 Dukat becomes Meru's patron/lover. The script depicts both coercive structural circumstances and Meru developing attachment to comfort, security and Dukat; neither dimension erases the other.
WD05 Meru obtains food/medicine/resources for Taban and the children. A message from Taban says the family is thriving because of what she provided, credits her with saving their lives and expresses continuing love.
WD06 Kira initially judges Meru as a collaborator and plans to kill her with a bomb in Dukat's quarters, telling a resistance contact that collaborators deserve the consequences.
WD07 After hearing Taban's message, Kira's appraisal changes. She rescues both Meru and Dukat before the bomb explodes rather than carrying through the intended killing.
WD08 The Orb experience gives Kira experiential evidence relevant to her family history, but it does not automatically establish an omniscient transcript of every Occupation event or remove uncertainty about temporal mechanism.
Assertions: Meru's conduct must be coded across coercion, survival strategy, material benefit to family, emotional attachment and collaboration accusations rather than one flattening label. Kira's changed judgment is a character-state revision produced by new experiential evidence. `Luma` is an operational identity within the Orb frame, not grounds for creating an unrelated global person.

## Inquisition
INQ01 Sloan arrives claiming Internal Affairs authority and subjects Bashir to an intensive investigation focused on his Dominion captivity, genetic concealment and possible recruitment by the Dominion.
INQ02 Bashir is sleep-deprived and pressured through increasingly accusatory questioning. The investigative frame initially appears to involve actual DS9 colleagues and Starfleet procedures.
INQ03 A later sequence in which Bashir appears rejected by colleagues, encounters a defecting Weyoun and escapes is revealed to be a constructed holosuite simulation rather than external events.
INQ04 Bashir detects the simulation through an inconsistency in O'Brien's remembered shoulder injury. The apparent crew hostility and Weyoun interaction must therefore remain simulation-frame evidence, not promoted to world-state actions by those people.
INQ05 Sloan states that Bashir was beamed from his quarters while asleep and monitored through a neural-interface/biochip system while the scenario was generated.
INQ06 Sloan identifies himself as part of `Section 31`, which he characterizes as an autonomous branch of Starfleet Intelligence dating to the original Starfleet Charter, operating without ordinary approval/reporting and addressing threats quietly.
INQ07 Those claims establish Sloan's institutional testimony and demonstrated covert capability. This episode alone does not independently validate every historical/legal assertion he makes about Section 31.
INQ08 Sloan attempts to recruit Bashir; Bashir rejects the ends-justify-means framework and the recruitment.
Assertions: simulated Sisko/O'Brien/Weyoun interactions remain representations generated for intelligence screening, not actions of the corresponding local persons. Sloan's existence and operation are depicted; his broader account of Section 31's charter/authority remains testimony pending corroboration. Bashir remains the same local person through sleep deprivation, simulation and awakening; no identity duplication is implied by the scenario.

## Cross-work observations
- Identity-adjacent transformations repeatedly preserve causal continuity: miniaturization/restoration, undercover names, Orb-mediated role experience and simulation frames alter context without requiring new global persons.
- Institutional duty conflicts with relational obligation in O'Brien/Bilby and Worf/Dax cases; the source supplies choices and consequences rather than one universal moral calculus.
- Intelligence operations manufacture appearances at multiple levels: undercover identity, false assassination attribution and a complete simulated social world. Provenance must therefore remain attached to each claim.
