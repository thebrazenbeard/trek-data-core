## In the Pale Moonlight
IPM01 Sisko records a private log to reconstruct and morally assess an operation he initiated after repeated war casualties and the fall of Betazed convinced him that Romulan entry into the war was strategically necessary.
IPM02 Garak's initial attempts to obtain genuine Dominion attack plans through Cardassian contacts result in those contacts' deaths. Sisko then accepts a plan to manufacture evidence instead.
IPM03 Criminal holographer Tolar creates a forged recording on an authentic Cardassian optolythic data rod depicting Dominion leaders planning an attack on Romulus. Sisko knowingly obtains restricted materials, suppresses Tolar's assault arrest, and participates in presenting the forgery.
IPM04 Romulan Senator Vreenak inspects the evidence and determines it is forged. The fabricated recording therefore fails as direct persuasion.
IPM05 Vreenak's shuttle later explodes. Tal Shiar investigators attribute the apparent sabotage to the Dominion and recover the damaged rod, whose flaws are interpreted as explosion damage; Romulus enters the war against the Dominion.
IPM06 Garak admits he planted the explosive and arranged Tolar's death, explaining that the assassination and damaged rod were his actual plan once Vreenak could expose the forgery.
IPM07 Sisko physically attacks Garak after learning this but ultimately recognizes that he knowingly created conditions in which Garak could act and that the result achieved Sisko's strategic objective.
IPM08 Sisko repeatedly frames the narrative as personal justification, ends by saying he can live with it, and orders the personal log erased. The deleted log is self-testimony, not an external neutral record of motive.
Assertions: the Dominion planning recording is deliberate fabricated evidence. Vreenak's assassination is Garak's act, while Sisko's causal/moral involvement is broader than literal execution and is explicitly contested by Sisko's own retrospective reasoning. Romulan attribution to the Dominion is a successful false provenance inference built around real physical sabotage.

## His Way
HW01 Vic Fontaine is a self-aware interactive hologram in Bashir's 1960s Las Vegas program and explicitly understands himself as a hologram.
HW02 Odo seeks Vic's advice about his feelings for Kira. Vic uses simulated social situations and a Kira-based holographic model to help Odo practice confidence and intimacy.
HW03 Vic later arranges a dinner/dance involving the actual Kira while telling Odo he has improved the Kira hologram. Kira simultaneously believes she is on a real first date with Odo.
HW04 Odo's ease during the encounter depends materially on his mistaken belief that Kira is simulated. Kira's responses are nevertheless those of the actual Kira, not a generated representation.
HW05 Vic reveals the deception after Odo's assumptions make the situation untenable. Both Odo and Kira react to having been manipulated.
HW06 After separate reflection, Kira approaches Odo on the Promenade; they openly discuss the relationship and mutually kiss, beginning a real romantic relationship outside the holosuite frame.
Assertions: Vic, holographic Kira-model and actual Kira remain distinct local entities/representations. Vic's successful manipulation does not convert a simulated rehearsal into evidence about actual Kira until the real encounter occurs. Odo/Kira's later relationship is mutually enacted after the deception is exposed, rather than being reducible to Vic's programming.

## The Reckoning
TR01 Sisko retrieves an ancient Bajoran tablet from B'hala and studies its prophecy inscriptions. Translation uncertainty is explicit, including ambiguous ideograms; the text is not treated as perfectly transparent evidence.
TR02 Sisko experiences an urge and shatters the tablet. He later interprets that urge as something the Prophets wanted, while Dax notes the difficulty of substantiating that interpretation.
TR03 Energy released from the tablet enters Kira. The resulting Kira/Prophet speaks and acts as a distinct occupying presence while stating that Kira is a willing vessel.
TR04 The Prophet announces a coming confrontation with Kosst Amojan/Pah-wraith and says Bajor will be reborn. Winn connects this to a textual interpretation predicting a thousand-year Golden Age if the Pah-wraith is destroyed.
TR05 A Pah-wraith later enters Jake, producing a second possessed/vessel state. Sisko offers himself instead, but the Pah-wraith refuses and frames his offer as wavering faith.
TR06 Sisko chooses to permit the confrontation despite danger to Jake and Kira because he accepts the possibility that the event is the prophesied Reckoning and future good for Bajor.
TR07 Winn activates chroniton radiation despite Sisko's refusal to do so. The Prophet and Pah-wraith leave their vessels before the confrontation resolves; Kira and Jake survive.
TR08 Winn acts partly from fear that the predicted Golden Age would eliminate religious offices including Kai/Emissary roles. Her motives and her reading of prophecy are source-supported but do not prove what the interrupted counterfactual future would have been.
Assertions: Kira/Prophet and Jake/Pah-wraith are temporary possession/vessel states, not global identity mergers. The predicted Golden Age remains prophecy/interpretation because the battle is interrupted. Sisko's sense that the Prophets caused him to break the tablet is his interpretation unless separately corroborated.

## Valiant
VA01 Jake and Nog encounter the Defiant-class USS Valiant operated almost entirely by Red Squad cadets after the original commissioned officers were killed. Cadet Tim Watters says Captain Ramirez gave him a battlefield commission and authority to promote others before dying.
VA02 The cadets have kept the ship on its original intelligence mission for months rather than returning to Federation lines. Their institutional legitimacy therefore rests on an emergency commission plus their own continuation of mission authority.
VA03 Nog is strongly impressed by Red Squad, repairs a major engineering problem, accepts a promoted role as chief engineer and begins identifying with the crew. Jake remains skeptical of the command culture and increasingly risky choices.
VA04 Watters chooses to attack a new Dominion battleship after intelligence suggests a structural vulnerability, despite the crew's original reconnaissance mission and Jake's objections.
VA05 Group identity and exceptionalism are repeatedly invoked to overcome doubt. Watters' declaration that Red Squad can do anything helps convert uncertainty into unanimous enthusiasm rather than supplying new technical evidence.
VA06 The attack hits the intended structural target but the predicted vulnerability does not destroy the Dominion ship. The Valiant is then destroyed and Watters dies.
VA07 Jake, Nog and cadet Collins survive. Nog removes his Red Squad insignia and ultimately tells Collins that Watters may have been heroic or personally great but was a bad captain.
Assertions: emergency battlefield authority and competent prior performance do not prove the wisdom of Watters' later tactical decision. Red Squad exceptionalism is group self-concept testimony and social pressure. The failed vulnerability hypothesis is direct disconfirming evidence against treating intelligence-based prediction as certainty.

## Profit and Lace
PAL01 Zek is deposed after amendments expanding Ferengi female economic/property participation trigger political and market backlash; Brunt becomes acting Nagus.
PAL02 Zek/Ishka's restoration strategy depends on convincing FCA Commissioner Nilva that female participation can be profitable and politically survivable.
PAL03 Ishka becomes temporarily unavailable, so Quark undergoes substantial medical/hormonal/body alteration and presents as the female financial adviser `Lumba` to sustain the political demonstration.
PAL04 Quark-as-Lumba uses a constructed social identity for a specific deception. The bodily changes are real temporary medical changes; `Lumba` is not an unrelated person created by surgery.
PAL05 Nilva is attracted to Lumba and attempts intimacy while unaware of the deception. Brunt later exposes Quark's prior male identity; the encounter therefore contains consent-relevant information asymmetry produced by the impersonation.
PAL06 Nilva nevertheless accepts the economic case and supports Zek's reform program; the political strategy succeeds in restoring the reform faction rather than demonstrating that Quark's deception was ethically neutral.
PAL07 Quark later undergoes reversal/recovery from the temporary bodily alteration. His experience produces some stated new awareness of Ferengi women's treatment, while his longer-term attitudes remain a separate question for later evidence.
PAL08 The episode also depicts Quark's own prior workplace sexual behavior toward women, making his temporary role experience part of an existing institutional/gender conflict rather than proof of a complete personal transformation.
Assertions: Quark and Lumba remain one causally continuous local person under temporary medical alteration plus deliberate social impersonation. Gendered legal restrictions are institutional facts, not biological necessities. Nilva's support is evidence of political persuasion under deception and should not be cleansed of the information asymmetry that enabled it.

## Cross-work observations
- Fabricated representations can produce real consequences: Tolar's recording changes Romulan inference, Vic's deception changes Odo/Kira interaction, Lumba affects Ferengi politics. Effects do not make the representations factually authentic.
- Prophecy and predictive intelligence both contain uncertainty that actors may ignore under pressure. `The Reckoning` and `Valiant` supply explicit counterevidence against treating interpreted future claims as guaranteed outcomes.
- Temporary embodiment/possession frames differ materially: Kira/Prophet and Jake/Pah-wraith involve occupying entities; Quark/Lumba is bodily alteration plus role impersonation; holographic models remain simulations.
