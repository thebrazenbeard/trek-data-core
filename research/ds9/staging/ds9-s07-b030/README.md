## Time's Orphan
TO01 Eight-year-old Molly O'Brien falls through an ancient time portal and is retrieved from the planet's past approximately ten subjective years later as an eighteen-year-old who survived alone with little/no human contact.
TO02 DNA confirms Molly-18 is Molly. Her changed language, behavior and memories follow prolonged isolation rather than evidence of a duplicate or unrelated person.
TO03 Bashir explicitly explains that retrieving little Molly from the earlier point would remove the causal history that produced Molly-18; Keiko initially refuses to erase the older Molly's existence simply to recover the child they remember.
TO04 Attempts to reintegrate Molly-18 into station society become increasingly harmful. After violence/legal confinement becomes likely, Miles and Keiko decide to return her to the environment/time where she had adapted rather than institutionalize her.
TO05 Returned to the past, Molly-18 encounters her newly stranded younger self. She recognizes her, directs little Molly through the portal to her parents and then becomes transparent/disappears as the temporal history that produced her is undone.
TO06 Little Molly emerges in the present and is reunited with her parents.
Assertions: little Molly and Molly-18 are temporally related local states/entities whose exact global numerical-identity relation should not be reduced to a database overwrite. Molly-18's final disappearance is explicitly tied to temporal-matrix destabilization after changing her own past. The episode depicts a history rewrite in which one developed life is lost while the younger child is restored; it is not merely ordinary medical reversal.

## The Sound of Her Voice
SHV01 Defiant receives a distress transmission from Captain Lisa Cusak, stranded after a crash. Because rescue will take days, crew members maintain prolonged audio contact with her and form distinct personal relationships through conversation.
SHV02 Cusak provides advice and emotional support to Sisko, O'Brien, Bashir and others while separately disclosing her own isolation, career concerns and fear of dying before rescue.
SHV03 The communications are treated as live by both sides; crew testimony and Cusak's responses demonstrate interactive exchange rather than a static recording.
SHV04 On arrival, the crew finds Cusak's remains and determines she died approximately three years and two months earlier.
SHV05 The planet's unusual energy field/time displacement accounts for transmissions crossing temporal offset, allowing the Defiant crew to communicate with Cusak shortly before her death even though they physically arrive years later in her local chronology.
SHV06 The crew holds a wake and explicitly recognizes that a person they never physically met nevertheless affected their present relationships and choices.
Assertions: Cusak's voice is not a ghost or simulation; the work supports real bidirectional communication across a temporal offset. Her dead body and earlier conversational self belong to different points in one causal biography, not two globally merged entities. Emotional relationship evidence does not require physical co-presence.

## Tears of the Prophets
TP01 Starfleet plans an offensive into Cardassian/Dominion territory. Before departure Sisko receives an anomalous Prophet warning through an Orb/vision frame urging the Emissary not to undertake the action.
TP02 Sisko chooses his Starfleet command responsibility over the warning and leads the operation. The assault on the Chin'toka system succeeds militarily after the attacking force exploits the orbital weapons platform power system.
TP03 Dukat, increasingly focused on Bajoran religion/Pah-wraiths, uses a recovered artifact to become a vessel for a Pah-wraith and enters the Bajoran shrine on DS9.
TP04 Jadzia, separately at the shrine and hopeful about having a child with Worf, encounters the possessed Dukat. The Pah-wraith attacks her and the Orb; Jadzia is critically injured.
TP05 Bashir cannot save Jadzia's body/person, but the Dax symbiont survives and is removed for transfer. Jadzia Dax's death and Dax's continued biological continuity therefore must not be collapsed into either “Dax died” or “Jadzia survived.”
TP06 The attacked Orb darkens and the wormhole/Prophets become inaccessible. Bajorans experience the loss as spiritual catastrophe; the physical/phenomenological loss is direct while theological meaning remains frame-relative.
TP07 Sisko later tells Jadzia's body that Curzon was his mentor while Jadzia was his friend, explicitly differentiating his relationships to successive Dax hosts.
TP08 Sisko blames himself for not obeying the Prophet warning and leaves the station for Earth to reassess both Starfleet and Emissary duties.
Assertions: Pah-wraith-Dukat is a possession/vessel state, not a permanent identity merger. Jadzia's death and Dax symbiont survival require separate local records. Sisko's claim that disobeying the Prophets caused Jadzia's death is grief/religious self-interpretation; causal responsibility for Dukat/Pah-wraith action remains separately recorded.

## Image in the Sand
IIS01 Months after Jadzia's death and the wormhole closure, Sisko remains on Earth and experiences recurring visions/images of a woman's face and a desert location while trying to understand his Emissary role.
IIS02 Joseph initially resists discussing the woman, then identifies her as Sarah: his first wife and Benjamin's biological mother, who left shortly after Benjamin's first birthday.
IIS03 Sarah's old locket carries Bajoran writing, connecting Sisko's family discovery to his religious search without yet explaining how Sarah acquired it.
IIS04 Sisko interprets the accumulating signs as direction toward Tyree and begins preparing to search for an artifact/answer there. His interpretation is motivated by anomalous visions plus physical family evidence rather than a fully explicit Prophet instruction at this stage.
IIS05 On DS9, Romulan allies install weapons on Bajoran moon Derna while characterizing them as defensive against Dominion attack. Kira treats the deployment as a violation/threat to Bajoran sovereignty and confronts the Romulan force.
IIS06 The Romulan/Bajoran standoff reaches combat readiness but ultimately resolves without the alliance breaking; the incident demonstrates allied strategic interests can conflict sharply without retroactively making either side Dominion-aligned.
IIS07 Ezri Dax arrives in Sisko's Earth/Tyree storyline as a newly joined Dax host with marked confusion about inherited memories and preferences.
Assertions: Sarah is established as Benjamin's human biological mother; her connection to the Prophets remains unresolved until later evidence. Ezri and Jadzia are distinct local persons/hosts sharing Dax continuity, not resurrection. The Romulan moon deployment is an alliance-sovereignty dispute, not proof of a single stable “friend/enemy” relation.

## Shadows and Symbols
SS01 Ezri explains that she was not trained for joining and now experiences intrusive/conflicting Dax-host memories and preferences. She accompanies Sisko, Jake and Joseph to Tyree while retaining her own stated likes/dislikes.
SS02 Sisko follows vision/location cues into the Tyree desert and excavates the Orb of the Emissary. During the search he also experiences a Benny Russell psychiatric-institution frame urging him to abandon the story/search.
SS03 A Sarah-appearing wormhole alien later characterizes the Benny interruption as a false vision from Kosst Amojan/Pah-wraith opposition. Preserve that explanation as direct alien testimony plus the depicted competing vision frame, not as evidence that every Benny experience across the series shares the same source.
SS04 Sisko opens the Orb. The wormhole reopens and the Prophets/Orbs become accessible again, directly tying this artifact event to restoration of the prior anomalous state.
SS05 The Sarah-appearing alien says Sarah Sisko was corporeal and that for a time the alien `shared her existence`. Sisko concludes the entity took over Sarah's body, arranged her marriage to Joseph and ensured Benjamin's conception because `the Sisko is necessary`.
SS06 The alien does not simply identify itself as Sarah. The work therefore supports a possession/shared-existence episode involving human Sarah and a Prophet/wormhole alien, not replacement of Sarah by a timeless alien throughout her life.
SS07 Separate Klingon action aimed at honoring Jadzia's path to Sto-Vo-Kor occurs during this period; the crew's religious-cultural interpretation of Jadzia's afterlife is sincere but remains distinct from directly observed biological death/symbiont survival.
SS08 Sisko returns to DS9 as Emissary after restoring the Orb/wormhole. Ezri also returns, opening a new Dax-host phase without negating Jadzia's death.
Assertions: Benjamin's conception is depicted as intentionally engineered by a Prophet through possession/shared existence with Sarah; this establishes causal intervention, not a clean identity equation `Sarah = Prophet`. Benny/Tyree competing visions require explicit frame provenance. Ezri's joining activates Dax memory continuity while preserving host-level personal difference.

## Cross-work observations
- Temporal continuity repeatedly resists overwrite logic: Molly-18 changes her own past; Cusak communicates across offset time; Dax survives Jadzia biologically while a new host later inherits memory continuity.
- Religious interpretation and direct anomaly evidence are separable. Wormhole closure/restoration and possession are depicted; Sisko's guilt, Bajoran spiritual meaning and afterlife beliefs require their own evidence frames.
- Family identity can include engineered causation without eliminating human participants: Joseph remains Benjamin's father, Sarah his human mother, and the Prophet intervention a separate causal/possession relation.
