## Afterimage
AI01 Ezri arrives with Dax memories despite never having visited DS9 as Ezri. She repeatedly distinguishes `me as Ezri` from `me as Dax` and corrects accidental statements such as `I was killed here` to `Jadzia was`.
AI02 Former friends respond differently to Ezri: Sisko readily uses Dax continuity, Kira experiences her as a relative stranger carrying Jadzia's memories, and Worf initially refuses engagement because of grief and Trill reassociation constraints.
AI03 Ezri's preferences diverge from prior hosts despite memory carryover: she dislikes raktajino/bloodwine impulses she associates with Curzon/Jadzia and explicitly describes needing to learn which urges are hers.
AI04 Garak develops severe panic/claustrophobic symptoms while decoding Cardassian military communications for Starfleet. Ezri's counseling links the attacks to conflict/guilt over aiding the defeat of Cardassians rather than treating the symptoms as evidence of simple physical danger in his shop.
AI05 Helping Garak gives Ezri evidence that her counseling skills remain her own useful capability despite joining-related confusion. She decides to stay on DS9 as counselor rather than retreat solely because the station is full of Jadzia's memories.
Assertions: Ezri Dax and Jadzia Dax are distinct local persons/hosts with Dax memory continuity. First-person pronoun confusion is evidence of integration stress, not grounds for silent merger. Garak's panic symptoms and Ezri's interpretation should remain linked but not converted into a global diagnosis beyond what the source supports.

## Take Me Out to the Holosuite
TMO01 Vulcan Captain Solok renews a long-standing rivalry with Sisko and repeatedly advances claims of Vulcan superiority and human emotional inferiority. Those are Solok's evaluative/species claims, not neutral biological conclusions.
TMO02 Solok's all-Vulcan crew uses a baseball holosuite program; Sisko forms the inexperienced `Niners` from DS9 personnel largely to answer Solok's challenge.
TMO03 Sisko becomes increasingly fixated on winning and temporarily excludes Rom because Rom's poor play threatens that objective. Team members confront Sisko over valuing rivalry above inclusion/fun.
TMO04 Sisko reverses course and restores Rom. During the game the Niners are badly outmatched but celebrate Rom's successful run as a genuine team achievement despite the overall loss.
TMO05 Odo acts as umpire and ejects Sisko after Sisko disputes a call; command rank does not override the game's accepted officiating structure.
TMO06 The Niners lose, then hold a celebration and give Solok an inscribed baseball. Solok's discomfort with their celebratory response undercuts his attempt to define victory solely through score/species superiority.
Assertions: the baseball simulation is a voluntary game frame with real interpersonal consequences but no war-state significance. Solok's Vulcan superiority claims remain testimony countered by character behavior and social outcomes. Sisko's temporary exclusion of Rom is a reversible team decision, not proof of Rom's general worth or competence.

## Chrysalis
CH01 Jack, Lauren, Patrick and Sarina return. Bashir proposes an experimental neural procedure intended to enable Sarina to convert her rich internal cognition into ordinary speech/action.
CH02 The procedure succeeds in producing sustained communication and independent social behavior. Sarina rapidly demonstrates insight and preferences that had been inaccessible to others before treatment.
CH03 Sarina initially becomes quieter around her longtime companions because they are accustomed to her former role; she tells Bashir that silence is easier in that context, demonstrating social adaptation rather than medical regression.
CH04 Bashir develops romantic feelings while simultaneously being Sarina's treating physician and central guide during her newly expanded autonomy. The relationship becomes intimate, creating a material power/boundary complication.
CH05 Sarina later withdraws from Bashir and ultimately chooses to leave for an independent scientific opportunity rather than remain in a relationship structured around his rescue/treatment role.
CH06 Bashir accepts that helping Sarina become autonomous does not entitle him to a romantic outcome. Sarina's post-procedure future remains hers rather than a continuation of Bashir's therapeutic project.
Assertions: pre- and post-procedure Sarina are one causally continuous local person; communicative capacity is not personhood creation. Treatment success and romantic reciprocity are separate questions. Bashir's loneliness/identification with another enhanced person helps explain his actions but does not neutralize clinician-patient boundary concerns.

## Treachery, Faith, and the Great River
TFG01 A Weyoun clone identified as Weyoun Six defects and asks Odo for asylum/protection, saying his loyalty to the Founders has led him to conclude the Dominion war betrays what they should stand for.
TFG02 The serving Dominion Weyoun is a later clone instance and demands Weyoun Six trigger the termination implant built into Vorta brain stems. Clone lineage produces shared memories/role expectations without making the two contemporaneous instances one local person.
TFG03 Weyoun Six reports observing the female Founder physically withering and says she told him the entire Great Link is afflicted by a disease. This is specific insider testimony later relevant to Odo, not automatically a complete medical diagnosis.
TFG04 The female Founder treats Weyoun Six as defective and prioritizes Odo's return. Odo's importance as a Founder produces institutional deference that does not erase his opposition to Dominion conduct.
TFG05 Under attack, Weyoun Six activates his own termination implant, saying this will save Odo and the Dominion. He dies; the contemporaneous Weyoun survives as a separate clone instance.
TFG06 In the parallel `Great Material Continuum` plot, Nog solves shortages through cascading trades whose objects temporarily change hands/locations. Property and logistical substitutions should not be mistaken for identity equivalence between traded items.
Assertions: Weyoun clone succession requires local instance separation plus DERIVED_FROM/clone-line relations later, not SAME_AS by name. Weyoun Six's defection demonstrates individual Vorta judgment can diverge from Dominion institutional loyalty. His self-termination is an intentional constrained sacrifice and does not make the other Weyoun his continuation by default.

## Once More Unto the Breach
OMB01 Kor seeks combat command despite age and declining recent standing. Martok rejects him partly through longstanding class resentment toward aristocrats who once blocked Martok's career.
OMB02 Worf secures Kor a role despite Martok's objections. During battle Kor experiences serious memory/orientation failure and gives orders that endanger the force, leading Worf/Martok to remove him from command.
OMB03 Kor's past reputation and present cognitive impairment coexist. Neither his earlier Dahar-Master history nor his current failure should erase the other.
OMB04 A rear-guard action is required to save the Klingon force from pursuing Jem'Hadar. Worf intends to take the mission, which is effectively suicidal.
OMB05 Kor incapacitates Worf, takes the mission himself and successfully delays the enemy long enough for the fleet to escape. Kor dies in the action.
OMB06 Martok, who had opposed Kor, joins the crew in honoring him as a Dahar Master and warrior. Cultural interpretation that Kor will reach Sto-Vo-Kor remains Klingon belief rather than directly observed postmortem fact.
Assertions: aging/memory impairment changes Kor's present command fitness without globally redefining his prior identity or achievements. His final action is a new competent choice under different tactical conditions, not evidence that the earlier impairment was fabricated. Worf's/Jadzia's afterlife language remains religious-cultural testimony.

## Cross-work observations
- Dax host succession and Weyoun clone succession are structurally different continuity problems: shared symbiont memory across biologically distinct Trill hosts must not be treated as equivalent to cloned Vorta instances.
- Medical or cognitive change does not create personhood: Ezri's joining adjustment, Sarina's neural treatment, Garak's panic symptoms and Kor's decline all require state-sensitive continuity.
- Institutional role claims repeatedly require counterevidence: Solok's superiority narrative, Martok's class grievance and Dominion definitions of a `defective` Weyoun coexist with behavior that complicates them.
