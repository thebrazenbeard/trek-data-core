## The Siege of AR-558
AR01 Federation personnel have held a Dominion communications installation for five months despite regulations expecting shorter frontline rotations; the original force of roughly 150 has fallen to 43, and the survivors display exhaustion/hypervigilance that the source depicts directly.
AR02 Sisko chooses to remain and reinforce the position after seeing conditions rather than treating the supply visit as complete. The decision places his crew into an already prolonged ground-war environment.
AR03 Dominion anti-personnel mines dubbed `Houdinis` phase in/out of subspace and produce unpredictable casualties. Starfleet later adapts their mechanics tactically against advancing Jem'Hadar.
AR04 Nog is severely wounded in the leg. Bashir determines amputation above the knee is necessary to preserve remaining motor nerves; later work explicitly establishes an artificial replacement.
AR05 Quark, previously fearful and critical of war romanticism, kills a Jem'Hadar without hesitation when the attacker breaches Nog's shelter. His action is immediate defense of himself/Nog, not evidence of a general warrior identity transformation.
AR06 Federation defenders hold the position but suffer additional deaths. Sisko's closing survival does not make the prior five-month deployment humane or erase the documented breakdown/strain among soldiers.
Assertions: frontline behavior should be interpreted under prolonged combat exposure rather than converted into permanent character essence. Nog's amputation is a bodily state change with preserved person continuity. Quark's defensive killing and his antiwar/critical views coexist without contradiction.

## Covenant
CV01 Kira is transported against her intentions to Empok Nor, where Dukat leads a Bajoran religious community devoted to the Pah-wraiths and presents himself as spiritually redeemed/authoritative.
CV02 Dukat argues that Pah-wraith worship corrects Bajoran religious error and uses personal charisma, sexual relationships and claimed revelation to sustain the community. His theology is testimony/leadership framing, not independent cosmological proof.
CV03 Mika gives birth to a visibly half-Bajoran/half-Cardassian child. Dukat had promoted the pregnancy/birth as miraculous spiritual evidence but his reaction reveals he knows the biological parentage implicates him.
CV04 Dukat attempts to preserve authority by concealing his sexual relationship and later organizes a collective death ritual using lethal capsules while secretly retaining a harmless capsule for himself.
CV05 Kira exposes the unequal capsules and Dukat's intent not to die with the followers. The community turns against him, directly disconfirming his claim of equal shared martyrdom.
CV06 Dukat escapes rather than accepting the death he prescribed for followers.
Assertions: the child's mixed biology is physical counterevidence to Dukat's miracle narrative, not proof against the existence of Pah-wraiths generally. Consent within the community is materially compromised by deception, authority imbalance and hidden sexual conduct. Religious belief and leader fraud require separate records.

## It's Only a Paper Moon
PM01 Nog returns after AR-558 with a biosynthetic/artificial leg. He reports pain and functional difficulty even where medical evaluation does not identify ordinary continuing tissue damage sufficient to explain the experience.
PM02 Nog withdraws into Vic Fontaine's Las Vegas holosuite and increasingly lives inside the program because the environment feels safer/more controllable than ordinary station life.
PM03 Vic is a persistent self-aware holographic character who can interact over an extended program run. His conversations with Nog have real therapeutic/social effects without making the 1962 Las Vegas environment external physical history.
PM04 Ezri initially tries to move Nog directly toward conventional counseling; Vic argues that Nog needs controlled space and gradually gives him work/responsibility inside the simulation.
PM05 Nog improves but eventually uses the program as avoidance. Vic forces the issue by changing the conditions/closing the refuge rather than allowing indefinite retreat.
PM06 Nog acknowledges fear of returning to combat/death and agrees to re-engage with ordinary life and counseling. Recovery is partial/process-based rather than a claim that trauma has disappeared.
Assertions: phantom/experienced pain is real first-person evidence even when ordinary structural injury has been treated. Holosuite participation is simulation-frame experience with genuine psychological consequences. Vic is not merely identical to his program environment and Nog is not a different person inside it.

## Prodigal Daughter
PD01 Ezri returns to her family on New Sydney while searching for missing O'Brien and investigating his attempt to locate Morica Bilby, widow of Bilby from the Orion Syndicate case.
PD02 The Tigan family mining business is financially entangled with the Orion Syndicate through an emergency loan and subsequent coercive obligations. Family members initially conceal parts of this arrangement.
PD03 Morica received a no-work salary as part of Syndicate pressure, then demanded additional money and threatened exposure. Her death becomes both a criminal investigation and a threat to the family's hidden dealings.
PD04 Ezri's mother Yanas, brother Janel and brother Norvo each occupy different roles in the family pressure system; responsibility for the business's criminal accommodation is therefore not interchangeable among them.
PD05 Evidence ultimately identifies Norvo as Morica's killer after cumulative shame, family pressure and personal despair. His broader emotional distress helps explain but does not erase the homicide act.
PD06 Ezri refuses to suppress the truth merely to preserve family/business reputation. The investigation forces the family to confront criminal consequences rather than resolving them through private loyalty.
Assertions: family-system influence and individual criminal agency are both relevant. Norvo's artistic failure/self-destructive statements are evidence of distress, not deterministic causes of murder. Ezri's prior family identity and present Starfleet/counselor role can conflict without requiring one to supersede the other.

## The Emperor's New Cloak
ENC01 Mirror-universe Ezri arrives in the prime universe with a recorded message from Grand Nagus Zek, who is being held in the Mirror universe. Prime Quark/Rom steal a Klingon cloaking device as ransom/exchange material.
ENC02 The multidimensional transporter moves prime Quark/Rom into the Mirror setting. Counterparts encountered there remain local Mirror entities; shared names/appearances do not establish sameness.
ENC03 Mirror Vic Fontaine appears as a flesh-and-blood person rather than the prime universe's holographic lounge character and is killed during fighting. This is direct counterevidence against globally merging same-name counterparts by appearance/personality role.
ENC04 Mirror political roles differ substantially: Regent Worf leads Alliance forces, the Intendant remains a distinct Mirror Kira, and Mirror Ezri's allegiances/relationships differ from prime Ezri's.
ENC05 Quark/Rom's cloak exchange becomes entangled in rebel/Alliance conflict; rescue and return require opportunistic shifting alliances rather than demonstrating stable moral equivalence between prime and Mirror factions.
ENC06 Prime Quark/Rom return as themselves after the cross-universe episode; counterpart encounters do not overwrite their prime histories.
Assertions: every Mirror figure remains a distinct local entity/counterpart candidate. Mirror Vic's biological embodiment is especially strong evidence that cross-universe correspondence need not preserve substrate. Recorded Zek is a message representation, not a second Zek.

## Cross-work observations
- Trauma and constrained behavior require state context: AR-558 soldiers, Nog, Norvo and cult followers all act under materially different pressures that cannot be flattened into permanent character traits.
- Deceptive authority appears in political, religious, criminal and Mirror contexts; claims must be tied to speaker/institution and tested against physical counterevidence.
- Bodily alteration and counterpart identity remain separate problems: Nog's prosthesis preserves continuity, while Mirror counterparts remain distinct despite names/appearances.
