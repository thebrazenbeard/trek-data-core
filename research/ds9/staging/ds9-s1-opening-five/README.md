# Work-local close-read notes

## Emissary

### Candidate local entities

All labels are local to this work only: Benjamin Sisko; Jake Sisko; Jennifer Sisko as encountered in memory/Orb/contact frames; Kira Nerys; Odo; Jadzia Dax; Curzon Dax as referenced antecedent; Kai Opaka; Quark; Gul Dukat; Jean-Luc Picard; Bajoran provisional government; Cardassian forces; wormhole-contact entities; Rio Grande; Deep Space Nine station.

### Source-relative evidence

- **Depiction / institutional condition:** The station is damaged and stripped after Cardassian withdrawal. Defensive, environmental, commercial, and civilian conditions are unstable. This supports a post-occupation governance and reconstruction problem, not merely a scenic backdrop.
- **Testimony / contested legitimacy:** Kira directly rejects the Federation presence and compares it to prior outside intervention, while Sisko invokes the provisional government's invitation. The source establishes competing political positions rather than a single uncontested account of legitimate authority.
- **Depiction / coercive administration:** Sisko uses Nog's detention and Quark's economic stake to pressure Quark to remain and help keep the Promenade viable. The act can support later analysis of emergency institution-building, bargaining power, and coercion.
- **Testimony / religion:** Kira says Bajoran religion is a major source of social cohesion. This establishes Kira's institutional/cultural assessment, not an objective metaphysical proposition.
- **Ritual/testimony / altered-state frame:** Opaka identifies Sisko as the Emissary, discusses the Prophets, the Celestial Temple, the Orbs, and destiny. These are religious claims and role assignments made within Bajoran religious practice.
- **Vision/altered-state:** Contact with the Orb is depicted through an experience of Sisko's first meeting with Jennifer. The work supports that Sisko experiences or is presented with the scene; it does not by itself establish whether the scene is literal temporal travel, constructed vision, memory activation, or another mechanism.
- **Computer/research report:** Dax correlates Orb history with reports of neutrino disturbances in the Denorios Belt. This is an evidentiary bridge from religious archive material to a testable astronomical search without collapsing either category into the other.
- **Sensor/technical analysis:** Sisko and Dax traverse an apparent stable wormhole. Dax proposes that it may be artificial and may explain the Orbs' arrival. Her proposal remains a technical hypothesis at the point it is offered.
- **Altered-reality/contact frame:** Inside the wormhole, Sisko and Dax experience incompatible perceptual environments and Sisko communicates with entities whose understanding of time differs from his. The safest frame is contact plus altered/perceptual reality, with ontology unresolved at worker level.
- **Testimony / identity:** Odo says he was found in the Denorios Belt, does not know where he came from, and hopes the wormhole may provide answers about others like him. This establishes Odo's self-report and identity uncertainty only. It does not authorize a global Changeling identity merge.
- **Depiction / technical constraint:** Kira and O'Brien attempt to move the station to the wormhole mouth using a modified subspace field and limited thrusters despite substantial structural risk. This supports analysis of command risk, strategic positioning, and material constraints.
- **Testimony/contact:** The wormhole entities describe linear corporeal existence as disruptive and challenge Sisko's account of responsibility under uncertainty. Their statements are evidence of their expressed model, not automatically objective cosmology.
- **Memory/contact frame:** The contact repeatedly returns Sisko to Jennifer's death at Wolf 359. Sisko eventually articulates that he has remained psychologically fixed at that event. This supports an interpretive assertion about unresolved grief and temporal self-conception, grounded in the depicted contact frame.
- **Depiction / deception in conflict:** Kira uses sensor deception and a bluff about station armament against Cardassian forces while waiting for relief. The source distinguishes projected military capability from actual capability.
- **Character-state change:** At the end Sisko asks Picard to disregard his earlier request for replacement. The episode supports a work-local state change from seeking departure toward choosing the DS9 assignment.

### Candidate interpretive assertions

- The work juxtaposes religious testimony, technical investigation, and altered-contact experience without requiring a worker to collapse them into one epistemic category.
- Sisko's Emissary status is initially established as a Bajoran religious designation and Opaka's claim; later events may add evidence, but this episode alone should not be coded as simple omniscient proof of prophecy.
- Sisko's linear-time explanation becomes inseparable from grief: the contact frame treats his inability to move beyond Jennifer's death as a personal failure of linear progression.
- Odo's origin remains explicitly unresolved.
- Kira's politics combine resistance to Federation presence with pragmatic use of that presence to protect Bajoran control of the wormhole. That tension should be preserved rather than simplified into either collaboration or rejection.

## Past Prologue

### Candidate local entities

Benjamin Sisko; Kira Nerys; Tahna Los; Garak; Julian Bashir; Odo; Miles O'Brien; Gul Danar; Lursa; B'Etor; Kohn-Ma; Bajoran provisional government; Cardassian authorities; Federation/Starfleet station administration.

### Source-relative evidence

- **Dialogue / epistemic caution:** Bashir quickly interprets Garak's attention as espionage. Sisko explicitly answers that they do not know Garak is a spy. This is a calibration-grade example of the difference between suspicious behavior, character inference, and established fact.
- **Depiction / asylum:** Tahna Los arrives while being pursued and attacked by a Cardassian vessel in Bajoran space and requests political asylum. Cardassian authorities characterize him as a criminal and Kohn-Ma terrorist. Those accusations are testimony from an interested party until independently supported.
- **Testimony / occupation and resistance:** Kira identifies Tahna as a former comrade and argues that post-occupation Bajor needs to reintegrate people from militant resistance factions. Sisko focuses on whether Tahna will use the station to continue violence.
- **Testimony / Tahna:** Tahna tells Sisko he is tired of killing and implies he wants to leave violence behind. Later evidence contradicts this representation.
- **Institutional conflict:** Kira contacts Admiral Rollman over Sisko's handling of the asylum question. Sisko later warns Kira against bypassing his authority again. This supports a command-boundary assertion without converting either party's position into the uniquely correct one.
- **Depiction / Garak:** Garak steers Bashir toward an overheard exchange involving Lursa, B'Etor, and Tahna, while continuing to deny or obscure any intelligence role. The episode strongly supports covert-information behavior but still does not require a worker to assert an official espionage identity beyond what is evidenced.
- **Depiction/testimony / Kira's moral memory:** Kira tells Odo she has nightmares about raids committed during the resistance and expresses uncertainty about where loyalty now lies. The evidence is memory/testimony about her own moral state; historical details inside that recollection remain source-relative.
- **Depiction / betrayal and competing loyalties:** Tahna reveals that he remains Kohn-Ma, accuses Kira of serving the Federation and provisional government, and pressures her to demonstrate loyalty by aiding him.
- **Depiction / intelligence transaction:** Lursa and B'Etor arrange to supply bilitrium, while Garak covertly passes enough information for Starfleet to infer that Tahna is assembling a high-energy explosive device.
- **Depiction / strategic violence:** Tahna's actual target is the wormhole entrance. His goal is to collapse access so neither Federation nor Cardassian influence can use it to shape Bajor's future. His earlier claim of renouncing violent operations is therefore disconfirmed.
- **Depiction / decision point:** Kira assists Starfleet's effort to stop Tahna and rejects his model of continuing the old struggle. Tahna calls her a traitor. The work establishes incompatible definitions of fidelity to Bajor rather than resolving the political philosophy by fiat.

### Candidate interpretive assertions

- The episode tests the transition from anticolonial resistance to post-occupation governance and asks when former resistance methods become terrorism under changed political conditions.
- Garak is deliberately written as an epistemic problem: strong behavioral evidence invites an espionage inference while explicit dialogue warns against treating the inference as established fact.
- Kira's conflict is not simply Federation versus Bajor. It is a conflict over what loyalty to Bajor requires after occupation ends.
- Tahna's stated nonviolence is contradicted by his concealed plan, providing direct counterevidence that must remain attached to any assertion based on his earlier testimony.

## A Man Alone

### Candidate local entities

Odo; Ibudan; Ibudan's clone victim; later developing clone; Benjamin Sisko; Kira Nerys; Jadzia Dax; Curzon Dax as referenced antecedent; Julian Bashir; Quark; Keiko O'Brien; Miles O'Brien; Jake Sisko; Nog; Rom; station crowd/mob.

### Source-relative evidence

- **Dialogue / Trill continuity:** Sisko shifts between language treating Curzon, Dax, and Jadzia as continuous and language marking the change. Dax says interspecies friendships do not always survive a host change. This is identity-continuity evidence, not permission to flatten host/symbiont identity into a single global person record.
- **Institutional/legal conflict:** Odo wants Ibudan removed because of his prior conduct and Odo's view of justice. Sisko insists that Odo operate within current rules. The scene separates personal/institutional memory of wrongdoing from presently actionable law.
- **Depiction / locked-room evidence:** Ibudan is found murdered in a secured holosuite. Initial evidence appears unusually compatible with a shape-shifter as perpetrator. That inference later proves misleading.
- **Testimony / Odo physiology:** Odo says he must return to his natural state roughly every eighteen hours and was regenerating in a container when the killing occurred. The episode establishes his report and later treats it as relevant to the framing plot.
- **Depiction / conflict of interest:** Sisko removes Odo from the investigation even while stating that he personally doubts Odo is the murderer. The source distinguishes institutional procedure from personal belief.
- **Depiction / prejudice:** Public suspicion escalates into a mob targeting Odo as a 'shape-shifter' and outsider. The crowd's accusations are testimony and prejudice, not corroborating evidence of guilt.
- **Depiction / counterevidence:** Biological material found in Ibudan's quarters develops into a humanoid clone. Later scientific analysis provides a mechanism for the apparent identical-DNA locked-room evidence.
- **Depiction / identity distinction:** The plot treats the newly developed clone as a distinct living individual who can begin a life in Bajoran society. The shared DNA does not lead the source to treat the clone as numerically identical to Ibudan or to the murdered clone.
- **Depiction / framing operation:** Odo confronts Ibudan under another name and accuses him of using cloning knowledge acquired in prison to create and murder a duplicate in order to frame Odo. The resolution is supported by the clone evidence and Ibudan's apprehension, but the exact historical acquisition of the technique remains partly testimony/inference.
- **Institution-building subplot:** Keiko develops a school for station children; Rom initially resists a human female teacher; the negotiation foregrounds cultural difference rather than assuming a homogeneous education model.

### Candidate interpretive assertions

- The murder mystery is an unusually clean case for preserving inference history: evidence that initially points toward Odo is not bad evidence, but it supports a wrong causal model because cloning changes the interpretation.
- The episode presents genetic identity as insufficient for personal identity. Distinct clones can share DNA while occupying different causal and social roles.
- Odo's status as both outsider and security authority creates a two-sided institutional problem: prejudice against him is real, while conflict-of-interest concerns are also procedurally legitimate.
- Trill continuity is explicitly linguistically unstable in the episode and should remain dimension-specific rather than reduced to binary SAME_AS.

## Babel

### Candidate local entities

Miles O'Brien; Benjamin Sisko; Kira Nerys; Julian Bashir; Jadzia Dax; Jake Sisko; Odo; Quark; Rom; Captain Jaheel; Dr Surmak Ren; Dekon Elig as historical subject; Higa Metar resistance cell; infected station population.

### Source-relative evidence

- **Depiction / technical trigger:** While repairing aging station systems, O'Brien unknowingly activates a device associated with the replicator system. The source later links this device to the outbreak mechanism.
- **Medical observation:** O'Brien develops severe language-production/comprehension disruption while other cognitive and physiological tests initially appear normal. Bashir first describes an aphasia-like condition, then revises the diagnosis as more evidence appears.
- **Medical analysis:** Bashir identifies a virus that reroutes established synaptic processing. The later appearance of similar symptoms in Dax and others supports an infectious mechanism rather than individual cranial injury.
- **Depiction / quarantine violation:** Quark keeps operating after closure orders and uses a crew replicator after claiming Rom repaired his own. This later matters because replicated food from the affected system is implicated in transmission.
- **Medical/environmental evidence:** Bashir first infers ingestion, then finds that the pathogen has mutated into an airborne form. The work depicts an evolving epidemiological model rather than a single instantly correct diagnosis.
- **Technical evidence / false lead:** Kira initially calls the device Cardassian sabotage because it uses Cardassian technology. Bashir's molecular analysis later finds the engineered virus lacks characteristic Cardassian construction patterns and instead points toward Bajoran methods. This is a strong counterevidence example.
- **Archive/testimony chain:** Records and interviews link the weapon to the Higa Metar underground, Dekon Elig, and Surmak Ren. The chain mixes archival records, scientific inference, and survivor testimony and should retain those evidence types separately.
- **Depiction / coercion:** Kira violates quarantine by remotely transporting Surmak from his office to her runabout, then informs him that her own infection has now exposed him as well. The emergency motive does not erase the coercive nature of the act.
- **Depiction / competing risk:** Captain Jaheel attempts to flee quarantine, damages his vessel, and creates an explosion risk to the station. His action is a decision under fear and economic loss, not evidence against the quarantine's medical basis.
- **Depiction / cooperation:** Odo and Quark operate the near-abandoned station together during the crisis. Quark admits he has only observed transporter operation, yet successfully transports Odo during the emergency.
- **Medical resolution:** Using Bashir's work, Surmak identifies an antidote and the station begins treatment. This supports a resolution to the outbreak but not a retrospective claim that every earlier diagnostic inference was certain when made.

### Candidate interpretive assertions

- The episode should retain the full inference sequence CARDASSIAN SABOTAGE HYPOTHESIS -> molecular counterevidence -> Bajoran resistance origin, rather than storing only the final answer.
- The outbreak turns old occupation infrastructure and forgotten resistance action into present risk, complicating any simple moral division between occupier technology and resistance violence.
- Kira's abduction/exposure of Surmak is a clear emergency-ethics case involving consent, coercion, and necessity.
- Quark's rule-breaking is both socially harmful during the outbreak and later operationally useful; the source supports both without needing a global moral label.

## Captive Pursuit

### Candidate local entities

Miles O'Brien; Tosk; Benjamin Sisko; Odo; Kira Nerys; Jadzia Dax; Julian Bashir; Quark; hunter leader; hunter group; Tosk vessel; hunter vessel.

### Source-relative evidence

- **Depiction / first contact:** Tosk arrives through the wormhole in a damaged vessel and becomes the station's first depicted visitor from the Gamma Quadrant in this work. O'Brien begins an informal first-contact relationship while repairing the ship.
- **Testimony / self-concept:** Tosk repeatedly answers identity questions with the statement that he is Tosk and resists explaining his purpose. His sparse self-description is itself evidence of role-bound identity, but not enough to infer the entire culture before later contact.
- **Depiction / security breach:** Tosk attempts to defeat station security restrictions around weapons storage. Sisko/Odo detain him while trying to determine whether he is a criminal or fugitive.
- **Testimony / honor:** In confinement Tosk asks to be allowed to die with honor. This is initially opaque until the hunters arrive.
- **Hunter testimony:** The pursuing group says the encounter is a hunt, that Tosk was made sentient and bred for this role, and that captured Tosk are publicly humiliated rather than killed honorably. These statements establish the hunters' account of their institution; the worker should not automatically promote every historical/cultural claim to objective fact.
- **Depiction / corroboration:** Tosk's own responses strongly corroborate that he understands himself as the hunted participant and regards capture as dishonor. The source therefore provides evidence from both sides that the hunt is a mutually recognized cultural institution, while leaving coercion in its creation/history unresolved.
- **Institutional/ethical dispute:** Sisko condemns hunting a sentient being but also says the Federation cannot simply judge and overturn another society's customs. The work stages noninterference against protection of sentient life rather than delivering a frictionless rule.
- **Autonomy:** O'Brien offers asylum as a legal escape. Tosk refuses because accepting protection would negate what he understands himself to be. The refusal matters: rescue against his expressed choice would create a different autonomy problem.
- **Depiction / rule circumvention:** O'Brien decides to reinterpret the 'rules of the game,' engineers a staged escort, assaults a hunter, disables/evades security, and enables Tosk to resume the hunt. This is deliberate interference, not accidental assistance.
- **Command response:** Sisko formally reprimands O'Brien for abandoning duty and interfering, yet the source also depicts security barriers failing to stop an escape Sisko had sufficient information to anticipate. O'Brien explicitly notices that fact. A candidate assertion of tacit command tolerance is reasonable but remains interpretive because Sisko never directly states that he intentionally allowed the escape.

### Candidate interpretive assertions

- Tosk is a personhood/agency problem that cannot be reduced to 'victim rescued from hunters': he is sentient, role-conditioned, and explicitly refuses asylum while embracing the hunt as central to selfhood.
- Hunter claims that Tosk were created for the hunt are relevant to causal identity and possible engineered purpose, but creation for a purpose does not itself settle present autonomy or moral status.
- The episode places cultural relativism, noninterference, personal autonomy, and opposition to sentient blood sport in genuine tension.
- O'Brien's solution preserves Tosk's expressed agency by helping him resume the only life he says he accepts, while simultaneously violating Starfleet duty and interfering in another culture's institution. Both sides of that conflict belong in later semantic analysis.

# Cross-work observations for later hypothesis testing

These are **candidate hypotheses**, not accepted corpus conclusions.

1. **Post-occupation rule legitimacy:** The opening episodes repeatedly distinguish inherited Cardassian systems, Bajoran provisional authority, and Federation administration. The resulting conflicts are not reducible to one legal hierarchy without source-specific context.
2. **Evidence revision is narratively central:** Garak's suspected espionage, Odo's apparent forensic guilt, and the Babel sabotage diagnosis all demonstrate that plausible early interpretations can remain rational while later evidence changes the supported conclusion.
3. **Identity is already multidimensional:** Dax/Curzon/Jadzia continuity, Odo's unknown origin, Ibudan's clones, and Tosk's engineered role create different identity questions that should not be forced into a single sameness predicate.
4. **Self-identification and institutional identification can conflict:** Sisko is named Emissary by Bajoran religious authority before he accepts the role; Kira and Tahna dispute what loyalty to Bajor means; Tosk refuses an offered identity as protected asylum seeker in favor of his culturally embedded self-conception.
5. **Emergency authority is morally mixed:** Sisko's pressure on Quark, Kira's abduction/exposure of Surmak, quarantine enforcement, Odo's recusal, and O'Brien's deliberate breach of duty each require evidence-specific treatment rather than a blanket 'command decisions are justified' assumption.
6. **Counterevidence must be first-class:** Several episodes are structured around reversals where preserving the initial evidence trail is necessary to understand why characters acted as they did.
