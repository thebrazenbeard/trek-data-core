## Source-relative evidence

### Observation, status, and Human-Vulcan tension

- **Depiction / external observation:** A Vulcan ship follows and observes Enterprise's comet investigation. Archer interprets the attention through existing Human frustration with Vulcan supervision.
- **Vanik testimony:** Captain Vanik characterizes Human inexperience and arrogance as the problem rather than Vulcan hostility. This is an interested-party assessment, not an objective species-level diagnosis.
- **Archer decision:** Archer invites Vanik aboard in an attempt to reduce suspicion and demonstrate Enterprise's competence. The social encounter instead preserves substantial mutual condescension.
- **Counterevidence:** Despite the interpersonal tension, T'Pol later recommends asking Vanik for technical assistance and Archer does so when the comet mission exceeds Enterprise's rescue capability.

### Comet science and material constraint

- **Scientific observation:** Enterprise identifies an unusually large comet containing eisilium, creating a research opportunity not present in existing Human experience.
- **Field operation:** Reed and Mayweather land to retrieve core samples and use controlled explosives to reach subsurface material. The mission combines scientific objectives with limited early-Enterprise field technology.
- **Environmental change:** The comet's rotation/exposure changes local conditions, producing fissuring and instability that trap the shuttle/landing party.
- **Rescue limit:** Enterprise's available means are insufficient to free the trapped craft safely. Archer ultimately accepts T'Pol's recommendation to request Vulcan help.
- **Vulcan capability:** The Vulcan vessel's tractor beam succeeds in the rescue. The episode therefore directly demonstrates a capability gap without making Human technology universally inferior in every domain.

### Private communication and information boundaries

- **Encrypted-message inference:** Tucker notices coded Vulcan communication associated with T'Pol and initially treats the encryption as potentially mission-relevant.
- **Privacy breach:** Tucker decrypts/reads the message and discovers that it is personal correspondence. He later acknowledges discomfort and apologizes to T'Pol.
- **T'Pol response:** T'Pol objects that Tucker read a private letter and asks that the matter remain confidential. The source supports both the operational reason Tucker became suspicious and the fact that he crossed a personal boundary.
- **Epistemic distinction:** Encryption establishes concealment from unauthorized readers, not evidence of espionage or disloyalty. The content directly disconfirms the initial security suspicion.

### Marriage, obligation, and agency

- **T'Pol testimony:** T'Pol explains that she was arranged to marry Koss and that his family was offended by her decision to postpone the plan in order to serve on Enterprise.
- **Cultural constraint:** T'Pol describes expectations that a married Vulcan couple reside together for a substantial period, making immediate marriage materially incompatible with simply returning to Enterprise at once.
- **Personal conflict:** T'Pol weighs family/cultural obligations against her desire or decision to continue serving aboard Enterprise. The source does not reduce this to a simple rejection of Vulcan culture.
- **Tucker role:** After the privacy breach, Tucker becomes a confidant and offers possibilities while recognizing that the decision belongs to T'Pol.
- **Final state:** T'Pol decides to remain on Enterprise rather than immediately return to Vulcan for the marriage. This is a work-local choice about timing and service, not proof that the arranged relationship or all familial obligations have permanently ended.

### Character-state changes

- **Archer:** Archer's resentment of Vulcan observation yields to a practical request for help when Reed and Mayweather are endangered. He does not become deferential to Vanik, but he prioritizes crew survival over pride.
- **T'Pol:** T'Pol actively recommends Vulcan assistance and later chooses continued Enterprise service, demonstrating simultaneous commitment to shipboard duty and engagement with Vulcan personal obligations.
- **Tucker:** Tucker moves from suspicion-driven intrusion into T'Pol's private correspondence to apology and guarded confidant status. The work does not erase the breach merely because later trust develops.
- **Vanik:** Vanik assists in the rescue despite prior criticism. The action complicates any reading of Vulcan observation as simple hostility or sabotage.

## Candidate interpretive assertions

1. **Encrypted communication is not evidence of treason.** The episode explicitly turns a suspicious signal into a private personal letter and thereby supplies a clean disconfirmation case.
2. **Privacy and security interests can conflict without one automatically nullifying the other.** Tucker's suspicion has an operational context, but his reading of T'Pol's letter remains a personal-boundary violation acknowledged by him.
3. **T'Pol's agency operates inside cultural obligation rather than outside culture entirely.** Her decision to remain aboard Enterprise postpones or resists immediate marriage demands without establishing total rejection of Vulcan family structures.
4. **Human-Vulcan technological comparison is domain-specific and situational.** Enterprise conducts the science mission, but Vulcan tractor technology supplies the decisive rescue capability.
5. **Archer's pride is constrained by command responsibility.** He dislikes Vanik's posture yet asks for help when refusal would endanger his crew.

## Counterevidence and unresolved points

- Vanik's criticism of Human arrogance is testimony and should not be generalized into a species fact.
- Archer's suspicion about Vulcan motives does not establish that the observing ship intends sabotage; the ship later provides life-saving assistance.
- Tucker's decryption of the letter should not be retrospectively justified as discovering espionage; its actual content disconfirms that premise.
- T'Pol's decision to remain on Enterprise does not establish permanent cancellation of her relationship with Koss; later works may alter or clarify that status.
- The work does not establish a universal Vulcan marriage rule beyond the cultural expectations T'Pol describes in this context.
- Transcript sources are derivative and not direct audiovisual verification.
