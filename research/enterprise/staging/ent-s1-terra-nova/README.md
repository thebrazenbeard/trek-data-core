## Source-relative evidence

### Historical archive and missing-colony problem

- **Archive/history:** Enterprise carries manifests, survey images and status reports from the original Earth colony. These records establish that humans founded Terra Nova and later lost contact; they do not alone explain the disappearance.
- **Historical testimony:** Archer describes the political dispute over Earth's plan to send additional colonists. This establishes remembered/recorded conflict over settlement expansion but not a causal explanation for the later catastrophe.
- **Orbital observation:** Enterprise finds the colony structures and residual radiation but initially detects no population at the original site. The evidence changes the problem from simple disappearance to an unresolved environmental/habitation question.

### Novan testimony and contested history

- **Novan testimony:** Jamin and Nadet describe humans as responsible for a past "poison rain" that killed adults and drove survivors underground. Their account preserves intergenerational memory of catastrophe and hostility toward Humans.
- **Archer counterclaim:** Archer denies that Earth attacked the colony and proposes radiation as the phenomenon remembered as poison rain. At that stage his causal account is a counterclaim, not yet proof of the precise radiation source.
- **Biological evidence:** T'Pol/Phlox identify the Novans as human descendants. Biological ancestry is therefore strongly supported inside the work even though the Novans do not initially identify culturally or historically as Earth humans.
- **Identity conflict:** Jamin rejects Archer's ancestry claim while Nadet later recognizes archival/personal evidence connecting her to the original colonists. The work presents ancestry, remembered history, name, and cultural identity as separable dimensions.

### Memory, records, and Nadet

- **Archive/photo linkage:** Enterprise records include a photograph of a young colonist associated with Vera Fuller. Nadet is confronted with evidence linking her childhood to that colonial family history.
- **Memory/recollection:** Nadet gradually "sees back" and recalls Vera Fuller as her mother and herself as a human child. This is memory evidence linked to external records, not a license to treat every recalled detail as independently verified.
- **Character-state change:** Nadet moves from categorical rejection of Human ancestry toward acknowledging a shared path between Novans and the original colonists.

### Health and environmental constraints

- **Medical report:** Long-term radiation exposure is causing serious illness among the Novans and threatens their survival. Phlox can treat particular conditions but cannot make the existing contaminated environment sustainably safe by medicine alone.
- **Relocation problem:** Archer proposes movement to a safer region rather than compulsory return to Earth. The proposal preserves Novan attachment to Terra Nova while addressing the environmental threat.
- **Material test:** When Jamin and Archer investigate the proposed region, local conditions create practical obstacles and force cooperation during Akary's accident. The episode makes trust partly action-based rather than purely rhetorical.

### Trust and coercion

- **Hostage/injury context:** Reed is shot and held by the Novans. Archer nevertheless returns to negotiate and accepts reciprocal-risk arrangements to get medical help to Nadet while seeking Reed's release.
- **Reciprocal trust:** During Akary's rescue, Archer asks Jamin to return the phase pistol so Archer can cut away the obstruction. Jamin does so, providing a concrete trust transition.
- **Decision outcome:** The Novans agree to a survival plan on their world rather than being forcibly assimilated into Earth society. The work does not present biological ancestry as overriding their political/cultural agency.

### Travis and institutional memory

- **Character investment:** Travis treats Terra Nova as a foundational mystery of Human off-world history and is assigned to prepare the report for Starfleet after the resolution. His role links boomer/off-world identity to institutional historical memory.

## Candidate interpretive assertions

1. **Biological ancestry does not exhaust cultural identity.** The Novans are biologically descended from humans while possessing a distinct lived identity and inherited account of Humans as enemies.
2. **Memory can be both distorted and meaningful.** "Poison rain" preserves a real childhood catastrophe while misattributing its cause to deliberate Human attack.
3. **External records can constrain identity interpretation without erasing lived experience.** Nadet's archival linkage supports continuity with Bernadette/Vera Fuller's family history but does not make decades of Novan identity unreal.
4. **The episode favors negotiated survival over forced repatriation.** Archer's eventual solution seeks environmental safety on Terra Nova rather than treating Earth ancestry as authority to relocate the community to Earth.
5. **Trust changes through reversible, observable acts.** Medical treatment, hostage negotiation, weapon return, and rescue cooperation progressively modify the parties' expectations.

## Counterevidence and unresolved points

- The exact physical origin of the historic radiation is not established here with enough source clarity to promote a precise causal mechanism; preserve it as unresolved unless Librarian/audiovisual verification supplies more.
- Novan claims that Humans intentionally caused the catastrophe are contradicted by Earth records and later evidence but remain important testimony about inherited memory.
- Nadet's recovered memory is supported by archival evidence, but the worker should not infer a complete reconstruction of every missing childhood event.
- Biological human ancestry does not authorize a global SAME_AS relation between Novan cultural categories and contemporary Earth social identity.
- Transcript sources are derivative and not direct audiovisual verification.
