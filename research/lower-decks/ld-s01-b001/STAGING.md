## Neutral close-read staging notes

### S01E01 — Second Contact

- Mariner's statements about earlier service, including prior ships and first-contact experience, are **dialogue/testimony**. They support that she makes those claims; they are not silently promoted to omniscient history.
- Freeman covertly assigns Boimler to monitor Mariner. Boimler later gives Freeman a deliberately protective report that conflicts with what he observed. The report therefore cannot be treated as a transparent world-state record.
- The rage infection and its effects are depicted aboard the Cerritos. The crew's recovery follows discovery of a neutralizing biological material and subsequent treatment. Multiple characters contribute to the resolution.
- Freeman's later captain's-log attribution is narrower than the depicted causal chain. Preserve the log as an in-universe record and the depicted events as separate evidence rather than forcing them to agree.
- A private Freeman/admiral exchange depicts Mariner as their daughter. This is strong episode-local relational evidence but does not authorize this worker to mutate a global identity registry.
- References to earlier Trek people/events are utterances or cultural references unless the episode independently depicts the referenced event.

### S01E02 — Envoys

- Mariner and K'orin describe prior off-the-books familiarity. That history is testimony unless separately depicted or corroborated.
- The apparent Ferengi encounter is later revealed to have been staged by Mariner with Quimp. Earlier behavior in that encounter is therefore performance/deception evidence, not reliable evidence of Mariner's ignorance or inability to identify a Ferengi.
- Rutherford's attempts at Command, Medical, and Security occur in training/simulation contexts. Simulated deaths, casualty counts, and emergency outcomes remain simulation evidence and must not be projected as actual Cerritos events.
- The episode depicts Boimler's loss and recovery of confidence, with Mariner intentionally engineering part of the confidence-restoration outcome. This supports an interpersonal/agency assertion without requiring a global character judgment.

### S01E03 — Temporal Edict

- Freeman learns about informal buffer-time scheduling and imposes exact task quotas. The resulting crew exhaustion, work disruption, and Gelrakian crisis are depicted consequences within the episode.
- Boimler initially favors rigid rule-following, then argues that the crew needs discretion to accomplish work during the crisis. Freeman explicitly reverses policy and authorizes broad rule/protocol flexibility for the emergency.
- The away team's conflict with the Gelrakians follows delivery of the wrong diplomatic object; the away team is captured and subjected to trial by combat. Ransom ultimately defeats Vindor and the immediate conflict resolves.
- Mariner's scar stories and descriptions of earlier adventures are testimony, not automatically verified off-screen history.
- The later `Boimler Effect` becomes a ship policy encouraging buffer time. A far-future instructional scene remembers Boimler as a paradigmatic corner-cutter even though the originating episode depicts him as unusually rule-bound. Treat the far-future account as later institutional memory/history, with explicit counterevidence from the originating event.

### S01E04 — Moist Vessel

- Freeman attempts to pressure Mariner into requesting a transfer by assigning undesirable duties, then by promoting her into senior-officer responsibilities when the first tactic fails. The episode depicts this as an intentional command strategy rather than an ordinary merit promotion.
- Captain Durango's maneuver with the Merced destabilizes the generation-ship operation; terraforming material affects both Starfleet vessels. Mariner's familiarity with the mission brief contributes to the successful mitigation plan.
- O'Connor admits that his earlier ascension persona had been performative. Later, the episode depicts an actual transformative/ascension event after a self-sacrificial act.
- During that altered transformative state, O'Connor reports extraordinary perceptions/cosmological imagery. Those statements are evidence of the reported experience during the event, not automatic objective proof of the universe's structure.
- The episode therefore contains both explicit counterevidence to O'Connor's earlier self-presentation and later evidence that a distinct transformation nevertheless occurs.

### S01E05 — Cupid's Errant Arrow

- Barbara Brinson is directly depicted and her romantic relationship with Boimler is not merely Boimler's invention.
- Mariner cycles through increasingly confident hypotheses that Barbara is an infiltrator, artificial being, shape-shifter, or parasite. Barbara independently suspects Mariner. These are character hypotheses and accusations, not world-state facts.
- A scan/reveal establishes that the actual parasite is attached to Boimler, not Barbara or Mariner. The episode attributes chemically enhanced attractiveness to the parasite. Preserve the earlier confident accusations as disconfirmed evidence rather than deleting them.
- Tendi and Rutherford compete for Vancouver diagnostic equipment, misread a transfer-related reward, and ultimately reaffirm preference for serving on the Cerritos. Ron Docent's later attempt to arrange a swap exposes his own dissatisfaction with Vancouver service.
- The lunar-demolition dispute includes claims from multiple affected parties; those positions should be represented as stakeholder testimony until the episode supplies stronger depiction or instrumentation.

## Cross-episode counterevidence / frame traps identified

- **E01:** in-universe captain's-log attribution versus a broader depicted causal chain.
- **E02:** a staged encounter retroactively changes the evidentiary meaning of earlier behavior; training simulations contain non-actual casualties/events.
- **E03:** far-future institutional remembrance of Boimler conflicts with his depicted originating disposition.
- **E04:** admitted fake ascension self-presentation is followed by a separately depicted transformation; altered-state reports remain source-relative.
- **E05:** multiple confident parasite theories are disconfirmed by the later scan/reveal.

These are strong regression candidates for Lower Decks provenance discipline because comedy and exaggeration do not erase the difference among depiction, testimony, simulation, deception, later historical framing, and altered-state experience.
