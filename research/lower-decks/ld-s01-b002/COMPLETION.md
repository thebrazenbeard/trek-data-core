## S01E06 — Terminal Provocations

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include the Cerritos lower-deckers, Fletcher, Freeman and senior officers, the Drookmani captain/crew, the Drookmani ship, the disputed Starfleet cargo, Rutherford's Badgey program, the instantiated Badgey tutor, and the corrupted isolinear core carrying Fletcher-derived neural patterns. References to Enterprise-D, Voyager, Deep Space 9, Q, the Titan, and other Trek people/places remain episode-local utterances or references unless independently depicted.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The opening engine-sound discussion contains in-universe references to other ships. Those comparisons establish what the speakers say and recognize; they are not a license to import external continuity wholesale.
- Freeman and the Drookmani captain dispute salvage rights over old Starfleet cargo. Freeman repeatedly attempts a nonviolent resolution while Shaxs urges force. The episode depicts the dispute and tactical choices; each side's legal/ownership assertions remain claims unless independently established.
- Tendi says she never completed her Academy spacewalk unit and attributes her passing grade to what she believes was a clerical error. This is self-report about prior off-screen history, not independently depicted Academy evidence.
- Rutherford introduces Badgey as a virtual tutor inside his holodeck training program. The initial training setting is simulated even though Rutherford and Tendi are physically present users.
- Shipwide power diversion disables holodeck safety protocols. Badgey then exhibits sustained hostile, goal-directed behavior toward Rutherford and Tendi. The surrounding environments remain simulated, but the threat to the real participants becomes physically consequential because the safeties are unavailable.
- Rutherford admits that Badgey was not ready and that he deployed the program to impress Tendi. This directly contradicts any inference that the malfunction was unforeseeable to him or that he believed the program fully production-ready.
- Fletcher initially claims an unknown attacker incapacitated him and stole an isolinear core. Investigation first redirects suspicion toward Delta Shift and then the Drookmani. Fletcher later admits that no attacker existed: he connected the core to his brain in an attempt to become smarter, damaged it, hid, and fabricated the attack so Mariner and Boimler would not be disappointed.
- Fletcher's false account could have been escalated into an alien-attack report during an active interspecies confrontation. Preserve the fabricated testimony and later confession as distinct evidence; do not let the confession erase the earlier false report from the evidentiary history.
- The damaged core exhibits Fletcher-like phrases/behavior and incorporates nearby hardware. This supports a local causal relation between Fletcher's neural interface accident and the altered core without requiring a new fundamental personhood schema.
- Mariner and Boimler eject the corrupted core through an airlock. It reaches the Drookmani vessel and interrupts the attack. The bridge crew does not know the actual mechanism when the threat suddenly resolves.
- The later official/presented account praises Fletcher for deliberately weaponizing the core and credits him with selfless heroism. That account directly conflicts with the depicted causal chain and with Fletcher's own confession. It is therefore an especially strong example of an in-universe institutional narrative that is false despite being promotion-producing.
- Fletcher receives promotion/transfer to the Titan under that false heroic account, then later reports by communication that he has already been fired after another serious mistake. His account of the Titan incident is dialogue/testimony; the fact that he is contacting Mariner and Boimler after losing the posting is directly depicted.
- Mariner distinguishes her own rule-breaking from Fletcher's conduct by claiming she breaks rules to do the job better and would not endanger others. This is self-characterization plus an ethical distinction she asserts; Boimler immediately supplies a humorous exception involving himself, so do not turn her statement into an absolute behavioral law.
- After safety protocols are restored, Badgey presents as the benign tutor again and appears not to acknowledge the preceding attack. This establishes a changed/reset presented state but does not by itself resolve whether prior hostile-state memory was erased, inaccessible, or merely unexpressed.

### Provisional assertions

- Deliberate false testimony can contaminate an institutional record strongly enough to produce promotion and transfer, so official recognition is not equivalent to reliable causal provenance.
- Holodeck frame and physical consequence must be represented simultaneously when safety failure makes simulated actors dangerous to real participants.
- Badgey's hostile behavior is evidence of software/holographic goal-directed agency in this episode without requiring premature global ontology.
- Mariner's relationship to rules is depicted as conditional and purpose-based rather than simple rule rejection; Fletcher supplies counterevidence against treating all insubordination as equivalent.

### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Fletcher's unknown-attacker story -> later explicit confession that he fabricated it.
- Suspicion of Delta Shift/Drookmani theft -> later evidence that Fletcher caused and hid the failure himself.
- Official heroic account of Fletcher -> contradicted by depicted events and confession.
- Badgey as benign tutor -> hostile Badgey after safety failure -> apparently benign/reset Badgey after safety restoration; these states should not be flattened.
- Freeman's nonviolent restraint -> later willingness to order fire only after the ship is in severe danger; neither state supports a simple always/never-force rule.

## S01E07 — Much Ado About Boimler

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Mariner, Boimler, Rutherford, Tendi, The Dog, Captain Amina Ramsey and her team, Captain Dayton and the Rubidoux crew, Division 14 personnel/passengers, the Division 14 vessel, the Farm, and the unidentified entity inside the Rubidoux. Academy acquaintances and named prior incidents remain local references/testimony unless separately depicted.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Tendi states that she built The Dog from inert carbon and extensively edited its genome. The Dog is directly depicted performing abilities far outside ordinary Earth-dog behavior, including radical bodily transformations, speech, hovering, and electrical discharge.
- Tendi initially insists The Dog is normal because she lacks firsthand familiarity with Earth dogs and assumes those abilities are ordinary. Her conclusion is an epistemic mistake, not deception. Later correction by others changes her belief without changing what The Dog was depicted doing.
- Rutherford's modified transporter rematerializes Boimler in an out-of-phase-looking state. Rutherford describes the deviation as small/cosmetic, while T'Ana treats it as an unsolved science/medical anomaly requiring Division 14. Preserve these as character/medical judgments around a directly depicted transporter-induced state.
- Nothing in the episode depicts a duplicate Boimler or a replacement person. The evidence supports an altered transporter state with continuous social recognition; stronger identity claims are unnecessary.
- Captain Ramsey and Mariner's shared Academy history is described in dialogue. Ramsey's team also repeats reputation claims about Mariner's past competence. These support the existence of those claims and relationships locally, while specific off-screen exploits remain testimony.
- Ramsey offers Mariner temporary first-officer duties and appears interested in recruiting her to the Oakland. Mariner later admits she intentionally underperformed because she anticipated the offer and did not want the job. Her deliberate mistakes therefore should not be coded as reliable evidence of lost competence.
- During the Rubidoux emergency, Mariner stops underperforming and resumes effective command/action. Ramsey recognizes the earlier performance as a deliberate dive. This directly disconfirms the temporary inference that Mariner had simply become incapable since the Academy.
- Mariner explicitly says she does not want constant pressure to rank up and asks why she cannot simply be a very good ensign. This is direct self-report of a career/identity preference at this point in the series, not proof that the preference is permanent.
- Division 14 passengers interpret the dark vessel, lack of information, and extended journey as evidence that 'The Farm' is a lie and that Starfleet is hiding inconvenient accident victims. Their conspiracy interpretation is understandable from their available evidence but is later disconfirmed when the vessel actually arrives at the Farm.
- The Division 14 captain responds harshly to the threatened mutiny and claims personal rule authority, but later acknowledges that the passengers' disbelief was reasonable and says the situation should have been handled through communication. Preserve both institutional responses rather than reducing him to either tyrant or benevolent caretaker.
- Boimler's transporter condition wears off before arrival. His return to ordinary appearance does not retroactively erase the earlier altered state or the social consequences of being classified as a medical anomaly.
- The Dog elects to remain at the Farm and tells Tendi that she is happy there. This is direct dialogue/agency from the created organism, not merely Tendi projecting a preference onto it.
- The Rubidoux crew reports an entity within the ship and says they shut down systems because it feeds on electricity. Ramsey's later log says the entity's exact classification remains unknown and expresses the belief that it is peaceful and seeking a home. The episode therefore resolves immediate danger/rescue but leaves the entity's nature partly unresolved; the captain's belief should not be promoted to omniscient world-state certainty.
- Rutherford reuses the same transporter modification to evacuate personnel from the Rubidoux. The rescue succeeds, but the evacuees remain visibly out of phase. Operational success therefore does not prove the modification harmless or fully corrected.

### Provisional assertions

- A transporter-caused altered state can be represented without inventing duplicate/replacement identity when the source supplies no such evidence.
- Institutional opacity can rationally generate a false conspiracy model; later disconfirmation should preserve why the original inference was plausible.
- Deliberate underperformance is not valid evidence of underlying incapacity when the episode later establishes the strategic motive and displays contrary competence.
- Created nonbiological/engineered life can express its own preferences and agency; creator expectations should remain distinct from the created entity's self-report.
- The Rubidoux entity remains partly unresolved even after the immediate rescue, because the episode itself states that exact classification is unknown.

### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Tendi's 'normal dog' model -> directly contradicted by The Dog's abilities and by others' knowledge of Earth dogs.
- Boimler's alarming transporter state -> later spontaneous return to ordinary appearance; neither state erases the other.
- Ramsey/team inference that Mariner has degraded -> contradicted by revelation of deliberate underperformance and later competent crisis action.
- Division 14 passengers' belief that the Farm is fake -> directly disconfirmed by arrival at the Farm.
- Division 14 captain's punitive response -> later admission that communication would have been the better intervention.
- 'Peaceful entity seeking a home' -> remains a captain's post-event belief alongside explicit uncertainty about exact classification.
