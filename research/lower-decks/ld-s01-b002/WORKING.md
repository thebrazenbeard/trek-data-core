## Neutral close-read staging notes

### S01E08 — Veritas

#### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include the Cerritos lower-deckers, senior officers, Imperium Magistrate Clar, K'Tuevon Prime personnel, the Clicket/Tweerk encounter participants, Romulan mission participants, the stolen Bird-of-Prey, and Q. These remain local occurrences only.

#### Evidence / frame discipline

- The opening prison/trial interpretation is an inference made by Mariner, Boimler, Tendi, and Rutherford from unfamiliar surroundings, restraints, ritual language, and the suspended senior officers. The later reveal establishes that Clar intended a celebration and testimonial ceremony rather than a criminal trial. Earlier fear and accusation remain evidence of the lower-deckers' interpretation, not evidence that a trial actually occurred.
- Clar demands truthful testimony but repeatedly assumes that Starfleet personnel must possess a coherent account of the senior crew's actions. The lower-deckers repeatedly demonstrate that they lack that context. Their uncertainty is itself evidence about information asymmetry aboard the Cerritos.
- Mariner's account of the Clicket/Tweerk encounter contains acknowledged uncertainty, attempts to bluff competence, and a bridge scene in which she and Boimler do not know enough to interpret the captain's request correctly. Treat the testimony as recollection/testimony rather than an omniscient reconstruction.
- Rutherford states that his implant gives him highly accurate memory, but the narrated mission repeatedly shows implant updates/reboots causing blackouts and discontinuous awareness. His ability to recall stored fragments does not erase the depicted gaps in lived continuity.
- Rutherford's recollection includes actions he does not remember initiating while the implant is rebooting. This is a strong identity/agency case: bodily action, stored memory, conscious awareness, and device control should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated agent state.
- Tendi's testimony is deliberately redacted because the mission was classified. She also admits that senior officers mistook her role and that she did not understand the assignment they attributed to her. Her later combat competence does not retroactively prove that she was the intended "Cleaner."
- The Klingon/Romulan/Neutral Zone material appears inside testimony about a classified operation. The episode supports that the testimony is given and that portions are redacted; it does not authorize reconstruction of omitted classified facts.
- Clar's accusation that the witnesses are lying is later undermined by the revelation that he misunderstood why their stories were incomplete. Preserve the accusation and its disconfirmation rather than deleting either.
- The senior crew confirms that Clar had been rescued from Romulan captivity and that the event was a celebration under local custom. Freeman later admits that many operational details remain classified. The reveal resolves the ceremony frame but does not resolve every underlying mission detail.
- Q's appearance after the ceremony is directly depicted. References made during testimony to Picard, Kirk, Crusher, giant Spock, salt vampires, or other prior Trek events are in-universe references/claims and should not be expanded into complete imported continuity assertions by this worker.

#### Provisional assertions

- The episode supports a recurring epistemic pattern in which lower-deck personnel experience major events without being given enough context to form reliable total accounts.
- Institutional ritual can be misclassified by participants when surface cues strongly resemble another familiar institution.
- Memory continuity and agency diverge for Rutherford during implant reboot intervals.
- Classified information creates legitimate evidence gaps; the correct result is unresolved/withheld detail, not invented reconstruction.

#### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Apparent criminal trial -> later revealed celebration.
- Clar's belief that the witnesses are evasive/liars -> later evidence that they genuinely lack context.
- Rutherford's claim of perfect implant memory -> concurrent evidence of blackout periods and missing conscious continuity.
- Tendi being treated as an elite operative -> her own repeated evidence that the role assignment resulted from misunderstanding.

### S01E09 — Crisis Point

#### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Mariner, Freeman, Boimler, Tendi, Rutherford, Migleemo, the Cerritos crew, and their holodeck-generated counterparts, including Mariner's Vindicta persona and the simulated Mariner. Simulated counterparts must not be globally merged with their source persons.

#### Evidence / frame discipline

- The opening planetary dispute and Freeman's Prime Directive response occur outside the holodeck and are depicted events. Mariner's moral and institutional claims during the dispute are character positions, not automatic statements of Federation law beyond what Freeman explicitly invokes.
- Boimler says his program uses years of private crew logs to algorithmically generate highly accurate simulations. That is a claim about the program's fidelity, not proof that every generated line or behavior is a literal record of the real person.
- Mariner rewrites the simulation into an exaggerated feature-film scenario. From that point, destruction of the Cerritos, crew deaths, villain attacks, emergency transports, and related spectacle are simulation/movie events only.
- The program blends source-log-derived character modeling with Mariner's authored scenario and computer-generated connective material. Assertions spoken by simulated characters therefore have mixed provenance and cannot be silently promoted to real-character testimony.
- Mariner uses the Vindicta persona to direct hostility toward a simulated Freeman. Her behavior is direct evidence of what she chooses to enact in the simulation; the simulated Freeman's responses remain generated behavior.
- Tendi explicitly rejects Mariner's use of an Orion-pirate stereotype. Later conversation separates Tendi's own self-description from Mariner's imposed role. This is useful counterevidence against flattening cultural stereotype into individual identity.
- Mariner fights a simulated version of herself. The simulated Mariner articulates interpretations of Mariner's motives, but those lines originate in the program, not an independent witness. Mariner's subsequent reaction and stated realization are stronger evidence of her own self-assessment than the simulation's assertions by themselves.
- Mariner states that she values the Cerritos, her friends, and her mother despite her hostility toward protocol and Freeman. This is direct dialogue/self-report within the simulation session, linked to her behavior in the scenario.
- Boimler discovers the Freeman-Mariner family relationship through the simulation. The simulated Freeman's disclosure is simulation output; it is not independently sufficient to prove the relationship. Separate episode-local evidence already exists elsewhere in the series, but this worker should preserve the simulation frame here.
- After exiting, Mariner changes her behavior toward Freeman and tells Migleemo that the exercise was unexpectedly therapeutic. This supports a character-state/agency assertion about the immediate effect of the exercise without proving a durable psychological transformation.

#### Provisional assertions

- Generated simulations built from personal records are evidence-bearing artifacts but are not equivalent to the people they model.
- A simulated self can function as a vehicle for self-confrontation without becoming an ontologically independent duplicate of the source person.
- The episode contrasts authored stereotype, algorithmic character model, and real person's self-description; those provenance layers must remain distinct.
- Mariner's conflict with Freeman includes both genuine hostility and explicitly stated attachment; neither should erase the other.

#### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Boimler's "perfect simulation" claim -> no warrant to treat generated behavior as exact historical fact.
- Simulated Freeman's family disclosure -> simulation output, not independently objective testimony.
- Vindicta/Mariner pirate characterization of Tendi -> explicitly challenged by Tendi's own identity claim.
- Simulated catastrophic events -> explicitly non-actual holodeck/movie events.

### S01E10 — No Small Parts

#### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include the Cerritos crew, Beta III/Landru participants, Captain Dayton and the Solvang crew, Pakled attackers, Peanut Hamper, Badgey, the Titan crew, William Riker, Deanna Troi, and episode-local references to prior Trek people/events. Cross-series/global identity remains outside this worker's authority.

#### Evidence / frame discipline

- The return to Beta III is directly depicted. Mariner and Boimler discuss earlier Kirk-era intervention and the renewed Landru worship; those historical details are partly dialogue/reference within this episode, while the present-day reversion and intervention are depicted locally.
- Boimler calls the 2260s "TOS" and supplies his own humorous expansion. Treat this as his in-universe expression, not proof that characters possess the real-world television label or production framework.
- Boimler reveals the Freeman-Mariner family relationship in a setting where other crew members learn it, changing how Mariner is treated. The resulting favoritism, deference, and attention are depicted social consequences.
- The Solvang is shown under attack before the Cerritos later encounters its wreckage. Sensor evidence reports no life signs. The episode therefore supplies stronger support for the Solvang's destruction than a later character rumor would.
- The Pakled attack is directly depicted. Cerritos sensors identify weapon systems from many different species, supporting the conclusion that the attackers' capability reflects accumulated stolen technology rather than the senior crew's earlier expectations about Pakled weakness.
- Freeman initially treats the Pakleds through prior assumptions, then explicitly asks Mariner for a dangerous rule-breaking solution. This complicates any simplistic stable assertion that Freeman always opposes Mariner's improvisational methods.
- Badgey is invoked as a training-program/holographic software entity to create a disabling virus. His prior grievance against Rutherford persists when safety restrictions are removed. Keep software/hologram agency distinct from biological crew identity while still recording depicted goal-directed behavior.
- Peanut Hamper is asked to carry the virus because of her exocomp capabilities. She explicitly refuses the risk and beams away. The episode depicts both her Starfleet affiliation and her rejection of the proposed sacrifice; neither fact cancels the other.
- Rutherford boards the Pakled ship with Shaxs. Badgey interferes with virus deployment because of his grievance. Shaxs sacrifices himself by ejecting the warp core and getting Rutherford clear. The event is depicted, not merely memorial testimony.
- Rutherford survives but later shows loss of long-term memory associated with damage to his implant. His statement that he does not remember Tendi is direct evidence of present memory loss, not evidence that their earlier relationship ceased to have occurred.
- The Titan arrives and drives off the Pakled attackers. Riker and Troi are directly depicted as Titan officers in this episode, but global cross-series identity resolution remains a Consolidator task.
- Mariner and Freeman explicitly agree to cooperate while preserving their rule-breaking/command tension. This is a local relational change, not proof that their conflict is permanently resolved.
- Boimler accepts a promotion/transfer to the Titan. The closing material depicts him serving there while Mariner angrily contacts him. The season-ending state is therefore a real assignment change, not a fantasy or simulation.
- Riker's remarks about Archer and the first Enterprise occur as in-universe dialogue. The metatextual joke should not be converted into an assertion that characters possess knowledge of real-world theme lyrics or production history.

#### Provisional assertions

- Institutional assumptions about a supposedly weak or unserious adversary can become operationally dangerous when current evidence is ignored.
- Rule compliance and rule-breaking are situationally negotiated within command practice rather than represented as a stable binary between Freeman and Mariner.
- Rutherford's identity continuity after implant damage remains locally continuous in body/social recognition while memory continuity is disrupted; do not force those dimensions into a single same/different verdict.
- Badgey and Peanut Hamper provide distinct nonbiological agency cases and should remain separate local entities with their own actions, refusals, and causal roles.
- Boimler's promotion produces a real social/assignment discontinuity at the season boundary.

#### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Prior belief that Pakleds are harmless/joke-like -> directly contradicted by current multi-species armament and destructive capability.
- Freeman's usual resistance to Mariner's methods -> Freeman orders a dangerous, code-breaking Mariner-style solution under crisis conditions.
- Rutherford's lost memory of Tendi -> does not negate depicted pre-injury shared history.
- Peanut Hamper's Starfleet membership -> does not imply willingness to accept lethal mission risk.
- Riker's metatextual-sounding dialogue -> remains character dialogue, not imported production-world truth.
