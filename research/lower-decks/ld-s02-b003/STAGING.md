## S02E01 — Strange Energies

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Mariner, Freeman, Ransom, Tendi, Rutherford, Jennifer, Stevens, the Apergosian high leader and constituents, the Cerritos crew, and the holodeck-generated Cardassian interrogator and holographic Boimler. The holographic Boimler is a generated simulation occurrence, not a global merge with the absent physical Boimler.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The opening Cardassian imprisonment/interrogation is later explicitly revealed as Mariner's holodeck exercise. The apparent prison break, torturer, and captive Boimler therefore remain simulation events even though Mariner's choices inside the program are evidence of her behavior and concerns.
- Holographic Boimler discusses the real Boimler's Titan departure, but those lines are generated simulation dialogue. They are not independent testimony from the physical Boimler.
- Freeman and Mariner each privately express frustration with their new mother/daughter command partnership while outwardly accommodating it. Later they explicitly admit that they dislike sharing command. Preserve both the cooperative behavior and the conflicting private/self-reported attitudes.
- Mariner's unauthorized cleaning exposes ancient material and directly precedes the strange-energy discharge that strikes Ransom. Freeman had authorized Mariner's side activity despite it being outside ordinary mission priorities. This creates a causal/command chain without implying that either woman anticipated the anomaly.
- T'Ana describes Ransom's condition through a comparison to Gary Mitchell. That comparison is medical/in-universe dialogue and a historical reference; this worker does not import the referenced prior work as independently established global evidence.
- Ransom is directly depicted developing telepathic/telekinetic and large-scale reality-altering abilities after the energy exposure. His later claim that his ego, rather than the energy alone, made him monstrous is retrospective self-assessment linked to depicted behavior rather than an omniscient causal statement.
- Tendi diagnoses Rutherford with Synthetic Memory Degradation after noticing changed preferences and memories, then subjects him to escalating interventions. Rutherford later establishes that the diagnosis is false. Preserve the diagnosis as a mistaken medical hypothesis rather than deleting it.
- Tendi admits that fear Rutherford might stop liking her motivated her intervention. Rutherford responds that altered preferences do not end their friendship. The episode therefore distinguishes observed post-implant changes from the unsupported inference of progressive brain failure.
- Rutherford's changed pear preference is partly reversed through Tendi's interventions, but the episode does not establish that all post-injury differences are pathological or that pre-injury preferences define the uniquely correct person-state.
- The closing Titan material is outside Mariner's holodeck frame and directly depicts the physical Boimler in an active Pakled battle. It therefore supplies counterevidence to treating the holographic Boimler as the same evidentiary kind of occurrence.

### Provisional assertions

- A generated simulation can model an absent person while remaining evidentially distinct from that person's physical occurrence.
- Post-injury preference change is not sufficient evidence of identity loss or medical degeneration.
- Freeman/Mariner cooperation and mutual frustration coexist; neither supports a simple permanently-harmonious or permanently-adversarial relationship model.
- Ransom's transformed state involves both externally induced capability change and source-supported evidence of ego/agency, so neither dimension should erase the other.

### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Apparent Cardassian captivity -> explicit holodeck exercise reveal.
- Tendi's SMD diagnosis -> later explicit disconfirmation.
- Changed Rutherford preferences -> continued friendship and competence; change does not equal replacement.
- Freeman/Mariner public teamwork -> their later mutual admission that shared command is intolerable.

## S02E02 — Kayshon, His Eyes Open

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Kayshon, Mariner, Jet Manhaver, Tendi, Rutherford, Freeman, Ransom, Kerner Hauze's automated collection ship, Chairman Siggi, Titan personnel, Pakled personnel, and two physically present Boimler occurrences after transporter duplication. Global mapping of either Boimler occurrence is outside this worker's authority.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Kayshon is directly depicted serving as the Cerritos security chief and speaking both Federation Standard and Tamarian metaphor. Tendi calls him the first Tamarian in Starfleet; that historical priority claim remains dialogue unless separately bound to broader evidence.
- Kerner Hauze's automated defenses treat the boarding party as thieves and convert Kayshon into a puppet-like form. The later investigation reveals Siggi secretly took an artifact despite instructing Starfleet to catalog the collection. His action triggers the defenses, so the holographic accusation of generalized Starfleet greed should not be treated as a reliable causal account.
- T'Ana later expects Kayshon to return to normal after the transformation. His temporary puppet state is therefore a depicted bodily/state transformation, not evidence that a permanently distinct person replaced him.
- Mariner and Jet compete over leadership, each criticizing the other's style. Their conflict suppresses Tendi and Rutherford's input until they explicitly defer to them; the latter then identify a safer technical route. This is direct counterevidence against reducing competence to formal/claimed leadership posture.
- Freeman's command evaluation criticizes micromanagement. She deliberately becomes hands-off with Kayshon's away mission, then reacts to the crisis by swinging back toward close control. The episode depicts overcorrection rather than a simple stable trait change.
- On the Titan, Boimler participates in an undercover mission against Pakled activity. Dialogue about prior Enterprise/TNG events remains character reference and should not be imported wholesale into this lane.
- A transporter rescue through a distortion field yields two physically present Boimler occurrences. Both identify as Boimler, share the same pre-duplication history, and are socially recognized by Titan personnel. The source therefore supports a genuine duplication event rather than a mere disguise, hallucination, or simulation.
- Neither resulting Boimler is entitled by this worker to the global label 'original' or 'copy.' Riker explicitly treats both as equivalent enough that Starfleet decides one must leave the Titan for operational reasons.
- One Boimler volunteers to return to the Cerritos; the other remains aboard the Titan and adopts the name William. This is a post-duplication divergence in assignment/name, not evidence that their shared prior history belonged to only one.
- The returning Boimler is directly welcomed by the Cerritos lower-deckers at the end. His return is not a reset that erases the continuing Titan Boimler occurrence.

### Provisional assertions

- S02E02 is a high-value transporter-duplication regression case: shared causal history branches into two simultaneously present local persons with later divergent assignments.
- Identity dimensions should remain lazy: biological/material duplication, memory continuity, social recognition, name, and assignment need not collapse into one SAME/DIFFERENT verdict.
- Leadership failure can arise from exclusion of relevant subordinate knowledge, independently of whether the leader is cautious or risk-seeking.

### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- 'Only one Boimler' assumption -> two simultaneously depicted post-transport occurrences.
- Any original/copy claim -> source does not designate one globally privileged continuation.
- Freeman's attempt to stop micromanaging -> crisis-driven reversal toward tighter control.
- Jet-versus-Mariner competence contest -> Tendi/Rutherford supply the decisive technical solution.

## S02E03 — We'll Always Have Tom Paris

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include the returned Cerritos Boimler, Shaxs, Tom Paris, Mariner, Tendi, Rutherford, T'Ana, Qualor II participants, D'Onni and Orion outpost participants, and Boimler's Tom Paris commemorative plate as an object onto which dialogue is projected during his ordeal. Prior-series people/places named in dialogue remain references unless directly depicted in this episode.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The Cerritos computer repeatedly refuses to recognize the returned Boimler after security changes made during his absence. This is an institutional/access-control failure affecting a socially present person, not evidence that Boimler ceased to exist or is ontologically invalid.
- Shaxs is directly depicted alive after his prior death. Rutherford wants a causal explanation and is told that bridge officers' returns are usually not explained to lower-deck crew.
- Shaxs eventually describes a 'black mountain' and other extraordinary post-death experiences. The episode establishes that Shaxs gives this account; it does not automatically establish the described spiritual cosmology as objective world-state truth.
- Rutherford's preexisting guilt about Shaxs's death motivates the question. Shaxs explicitly tells Rutherford that saving him was Shaxs's duty and not Rutherford's fault. This provides a local relational/ethical claim without resolving the resurrection mechanism.
- Boimler's repeated access failures trap him in maintenance spaces and intensify his identity/belonging crisis. He talks with his Tom Paris plate while distressed and exposed to fumes. The plate's apparent dialogue is therefore not independent testimony from Tom Paris.
- Physical Tom Paris is separately depicted aboard the Cerritos. When he later encounters the disheveled Boimler, he mistakes him for a Kazon and strikes him. The physical Paris occurrence must remain distinct from the talking-plate material.
- Tendi and Mariner discover that each knows far less about the other's history and interests than assumed. Mariner describes earlier service on Deep Space 9 and the Quito; those off-screen details are testimony within this episode, not automatically imported records.
- Tendi explicitly rejects a Nausicaan stereotype that all Orions use pheromones to cheat and later rejects Mariner's request to perform that stereotype. This is direct counterevidence to species-level flattening.
- Tendi's Orion contacts call her 'Mistress of the Winter Constellations,' and Mariner learns her first name is D'Vana. These are locally supported identity/name facts while the exact history behind the old title remains unresolved.
- Mariner and Tendi initially try to conceal damage to T'Ana's heirloom. The ending reveals T'Ana cared about the containing box rather than the damaged object they focused on, reframing the entire repair mission's assumed stakes.
- Tendi ultimately accepts responsibility for the mistake; Mariner also attempts to take blame. The episode ends with a stronger friendship claim, but it does not imply complete mutual knowledge or permanent conflict resolution.

### Provisional assertions

- Institutional authentication and personal identity are distinct: a computer can fail to authorize a person whom the narrative/social environment plainly recognizes.
- Direct depiction that Shaxs is alive is stronger than any particular character theory about how resurrection occurred; mechanism remains source-relative and partly unresolved.
- Species stereotypes, self-description, social title, legal name, and personal history are separate evidence dimensions for Tendi.
- Apparent testimony from an anthropomorphized object under stress should not be treated as testimony from the represented real person.

### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Cerritos 'user not recognized' -> direct social/physical evidence that Boimler is present and known.
- Rutherford's imagined resurrection mechanisms -> Shaxs gives a different extraordinary account, with no objective mechanism independently established.
- Orion pheromone stereotype -> Tendi explicitly rejects it as not applicable to her.
- Assumption that the damaged post itself mattered -> T'Ana reveals the box was the important object.

## S02E04 — Mugato, Gumato

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Mariner, Boimler, Rutherford, Tendi, Shaxs, Freeman, T'Ana, mugato populations on Frylon IV, Ferengi poachers, Honus, Patingi, and medical-physical holdouts. References to prior covert-operations precedents remain in-universe comparisons rather than imported evidence.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Honus tells Boimler and Rutherford that Mariner is a secret Starfleet black-ops killing machine. They initially reject the rumor, then use gaps in Mariner's service record and ambiguous observations to build a conspiracy model.
- Boimler and Rutherford misinterpret Mariner's emergency treatment of an injured Shaxs as evidence that she attacked/consumed him. Mariner later explains that she was extracting mugato venom. Preserve the original observation and mistaken interpretation separately.
- Mariner ultimately states that she herself started the black-ops rumor because she enjoys having a mystique. This directly disconfirms Honus's presentation of the rumor as insider knowledge, though her confession remains dialogue supported by her demonstrated familiarity with the rumor.
- Mariner explains a large apparent gap in the Atlantis record as Kerplickian lice and says Starfleet omitted it from the official record. The episode therefore supplies a source-relative explanation for the record gap but not an independently produced Atlantis log.
- Patingi is initially accepted as an expert because he says he has studied mugatos for years. He later reveals that he merely read five books and inferred he could safely imitate them. His self-presentation is disconfirmed by his own clarification and immediate consequences.
- The Ferengi operation is directly depicted capturing/exploiting mugatos. Boimler and Rutherford use economic modeling to negotiate conversion from slaughter to a live preserve/tourism model.
- Freeman later receives a simplified report about apparently eco-friendly Ferengi caring for mugatos. That institutional summary omits the coercive/criminal origin and negotiation that produced the preserve. Preserve both levels of description rather than allowing the later summary to rewrite causality.
- Tendi's assignment to scan crew members avoiding physicals is designed to test assertiveness. Her increasing willingness to confront avoidance is directly depicted; T'Ana's final correction shows that assertiveness does not erase role boundaries in medical care.

### Provisional assertions

- Missing records plus ambiguous behavior are insufficient to establish a conspiracy when the episode later provides a mundane competing explanation and an origin for the rumor.
- Institutional summaries can be factually compatible with an outcome while omitting ethically/causally important history.
- Boimler/Rutherford's negotiation provides counterevidence to the idea that their value is primarily combat competence.

### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Mariner black-ops theory -> Mariner says she started the rumor herself.
- Apparent evidence Mariner harmed Shaxs -> later explanation of venom extraction.
- Patingi expert presentation -> self-revealed amateur overconfidence.
- 'Eco-friendly Ferengi preserve' summary -> incomplete relative to directly depicted poaching and coerced economic transition.

## S02E05 — An Embarrassment of Dooplers

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Freeman, Mariner, Boimler, Rutherford, Tendi, the Doopler emissary and proliferating Doopler bodies, Starbase personnel, Malvus, party attendees, and references to Kirk, Spock, Data/Lore and other prior Trek figures. Doopler duplication/recombination should be represented locally without prematurely selecting a global personhood model.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The Doopler emissary's emotional response is directly depicted producing multiple simultaneously present bodies. Later anger/insult causes recombination. The episode therefore contains literal duplication and recombination rather than metaphorical 'splitting' alone.
- Multiple Doopler bodies speak and react individually while duplicated. This is enough to create local occurrences/evidence; it is not enough for this worker to decide global legal, biological, or psychological identity rules for the species.
- Freeman's attempts to avoid embarrassing the emissary worsen the duplication. Once she discovers that anger reverses the process, she orders the crew to insult the Dooplers. The same behavior that is ordinarily socially hostile functions here as a context-specific operational intervention.
- Mariner and Boimler impersonate William Boimler to obtain party access. The bouncer accepts Boimler's claim. Successful authentication by a social gatekeeper does not make the impersonation true.
- Mariner describes prior life on the station and encounters people who accuse her of abandoning them. Specific off-screen incidents are mixed testimony/dispute; one alleged abandonment is admitted by Mariner while others are contested.
- Mariner says station security is corrupt and likely to coerce them after they unknowingly transport illegal weapons. This remains her claim/prediction; the episode does not independently establish the full institutional accusation before they flee.
- Rutherford interprets old technical notes and his inability to finish a model as evidence that pre-memory-loss Rutherford was a better engineer. Tendi later reveals that the old Rutherford also never finished the model because the project functioned as a social excuse for them to spend time together.
- Tendi's explanation directly disconfirms Rutherford's competence comparison and reframes the notes as intentionally unhelpful delay tactics rather than superior engineering knowledge.
- Rutherford explicitly experiences his pre-memory-loss self as a competitor/'ghost.' The episode supplies identity/self-concept evidence without establishing two simultaneously embodied Rutherfords.
- A bartender says Kirk and Spock previously failed to enter the same party and drank at the bar. That is in-universe historical testimony, not independent primary evidence for the prior event.
- Freeman and the senior Cerritos officers are themselves refused entry to the prestigious party despite Freeman's Pakled-battle argument. The episode directly supports social/status exclusion, not an objective ranking of ship worth.
- Freeman ultimately transports a Doopler into the exclusive party, deliberately causing a duplication crisis there. This is directly depicted retaliation/agency, distinct from any later sanitized account.

### Provisional assertions

- E05 is a high-value literal duplication/recombination case that should remain local until reconciliation policy addresses what continuity dimensions the source actually challenges.
- Post-memory-loss Rutherford's comparison to his antecedent self is psychologically salient but factually mistaken in at least one concrete competence claim.
- Authentication, prestige, and institutional recognition can all be wrong or manipulated; they should not be treated as identity primitives.

### Counterevidence / regression candidates

- Rutherford's belief that old Rutherford was the better engineer -> Tendi says old Rutherford also never finished the model.
- William-Boimler party identity claim -> deliberate impersonation despite successful admission.
- Doopler embarrassment as uncontrollable proliferation -> anger supplies a directly depicted recombination condition.
- Prestige-party exclusion of Cerritos -> later social satisfaction at the supposedly less prestigious bar; status judgment and participant value are distinct.

## Cross-episode provisional regression set (S02E01-S02E05)

1. **Simulation contamination:** E01 opens inside a holodeck prison scenario containing a generated Boimler occurrence.
2. **Mistaken medical identity inference:** E01 changed preferences are misread as progressive synthetic-memory disease.
3. **Genuine transporter branching:** E02 creates two simultaneously present Boimler continuations with shared pre-event history and later divergent names/assignments.
4. **Authentication versus identity:** E03's computer rejects Boimler while humans and narrative events establish his presence.
5. **Resurrection testimony:** E03 directly depicts Shaxs alive but leaves the mechanism dependent on extraordinary character testimony.
6. **Stereotype versus individual evidence:** E03 explicitly rejects an Orion stereotype while revealing additional local names/titles/history.
7. **Rumor provenance failure:** E04 shows how a self-started rumor plus record gaps becomes a false black-ops theory.
8. **Institutional-summary information loss:** E04's later 'eco-friendly preserve' description omits the poaching/coercion chain that created it.
9. **Literal duplication/recombination:** E05 supplies a second, biologically different duplication case that must not simply inherit Boimler-transporter semantics.
10. **Memory-self comparison:** E05 shows Rutherford treating his pre-memory-loss self as a superior competitor until concrete evidence disconfirms that comparison.
11. **Reference-density discipline:** references to earlier Trek events/figures remain utterances, analogies, testimony, or cultural knowledge unless this episode independently depicts the referenced event.
