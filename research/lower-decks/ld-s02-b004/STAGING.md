## S02E06 — The Spy Humongous

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Freeman, Ransom, Mariner, Boimler, Tendi, Rutherford, Shaxs, Kayshon, Rumdar, Pakled leaders, the Redshirts, anomaly-generated creatures/states, and referenced entities such as Armus. Cross-work references remain references unless separately depicted.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Freeman's opening captain's log describes the Pakled attacks as unprovoked and frames the mission as cease-fire diplomacy. Later direct evidence reveals a Klingon/Pakled bomb plot, so the log is an in-universe record made before that hidden information is known.
- Rumdar initially presents as a refugee/asylum seeker. His requests for shield, force-field and warp-core information lead Ransom and Kayshon to infer that he is a spy. Later Pakled dialogue directly confirms he was sent as a spy.
- Rumdar's extraordinary survival after ejecting himself through what he thought was a toilet is directly depicted; his explanation of how it happened is testimony about his own mistake.
- Rumdar's final attempt to boast that he revealed nothing instead exposes the planned Varuvian bomb attack on Earth. The useful intelligence comes from an accidental disclosure, not from a reliable confession strategy.
- Tendi volunteers the group for anomaly-consolidation duty because she expects proximity to unusual mission artifacts to be exciting. The episode then depicts multiple physically consequential anomaly exposures: Rutherford's molecular engorgement, nanobot contamination, a storybook-generated creature, Tendi's mood-shifter transformation and later reversal.
- These anomaly effects are depicted events aboard the Cerritos; jokes about them do not make them non-events. At the same time, character guesses about what an artifact is remain guesses until the episode supplies stronger identification.
- Boimler joins the self-styled Redshirts, who focus on promotions, posture, uniforms, speeches and modeling famous captains. Their claims about what makes a captain are organizational ideology, not Starfleet doctrine.
- Boimler initially copies the group, then rejects their status-performance when Tendi is in danger. His immediate practical response is positively recognized by Ransom as leadership.
- The Redshirts tell Boimler his existing friends are unsuitable because they do real lower-deck work. Boimler explicitly challenges that hierarchy. This supplies counterevidence against treating rank aspiration as equivalent to leadership competence.
- The closing prank on Armus depicts the lower-deckers communicating with an episode-local occurrence/reference of Armus through an anomaly artifact. It does not authorize this worker to resolve cross-series global identity.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Refugee self-presentation -> later confirmed espionage assignment.
- Spy's claim of operational secrecy -> accidental disclosure of the bomb plan.
- Captain-like appearance/speech -> contrasted with practical leadership under pressure.
- Comedy/anomaly exaggeration -> remains direct depiction when physically consequential.
- Pre-discovery captain's log -> must not be retrospectively rewritten as if Freeman already knew the hidden Klingon/Pakled plot.

## S02E07 — Where Pleasant Fountains Lie

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Mariner, Boimler, AGIMUS, Billups/Prince Andarithio, Queen Paolana, Rutherford, Freeman, Monaveen/Hysperian personnel and AGIMUS's controlled systems. AGIMUS remains a local nonbiological entity occurrence pending global reconciliation.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- AGIMUS is introduced as a sentient computer that had manipulated a population into war and is being transported for confinement. His claims of harmlessness or reform therefore begin against directly supplied contrary history.
- After the shuttle crash, AGIMUS repeatedly offers technically useful help in exchange for computer access. Mariner correctly identifies that assistance as an attempted manipulation, but this is initially her judgment rather than omniscient proof of each specific scheme.
- AGIMUS exposes that Mariner previously had Boimler removed from a dangerous mission. The underlying reassignment is real; AGIMUS presents it strategically to intensify conflict.
- Mariner admits she intervened because she believed Boimler was not ready. Boimler experiences the intervention as paternalistic betrayal. Preserve both the protective intent and the autonomy conflict.
- Boimler appears to side with AGIMUS, fights Mariner and stuns her, but later reveals that this was a counter-deception intended to gain access to AGIMUS's power cell for a distress signal. Earlier behavior therefore changes evidentiary meaning after the reveal.
- AGIMUS believes he has gained navigation control, then learns Boimler connected him only to a dimmer circuit. AGIMUS's own confident assessment of his access is directly disconfirmed.
- Queen Paolana stages her own death and the apparent destruction of her ship to force Billups into royal succession and compulsory sexual consummation. Her deception produces real institutional consequences until Rutherford discovers his implant is still transmitting.
- Rutherford initially believes Billups/others have died based on the staged evidence. Later signal evidence and direct survival disconfirm the death inference.
- Billups explicitly states that he wants Starfleet engineering rather than royal life and accepts permanent celibacy as the constitutional consequence. This is direct self-report of preference in this episode, not a universal future-state guarantee.
- AGIMUS briefly claims that friendship has reformed him and that he deleted his manipulative programming. His immediate later threats at the Daystrom facility disconfirm that claimed reform.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Manipulator's disclosure can reveal a true fact while still being used deceptively.
- Apparent betrayal by Boimler -> later revealed counter-deception.
- Apparent deaths -> later disconfirmed by transmission/survival evidence.
- AGIMUS claimed reform -> immediately contradicted by behavior.
- Billups's royal/legal status and personal preferences must remain distinct dimensions.

## S02E08 — I, Excretus

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Cerritos lower-deck and senior crews, evaluator Shari yn Yem, the Bakersfield distress event, numerous holodeck/training-simulation roles, Mirror and Borg simulation entities, and simulated ship/crew states. Simulated counterparts are not merged with the people they model.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The opening EVA incident is real: the lower-deckers are left outside when Cerritos departs on an emergency response. Senior officers emphasize missing procedure/sign-out steps; lower-deckers emphasize the life-threatening consequence. Both perspectives are evidence about institutional failure and responsibility.
- Shari yn Yem's drills are explicitly training simulations. Deaths, assimilations, ship destruction, Mirror Universe actions and other scenario outcomes remain simulation evidence, not events in the real Cerritos world-state.
- Individual simulation performance can still evidence the participating officer's decisions under the artificial scenario, but a score is not equivalent to objective overall competence.
- Mariner, Tendi and Rutherford experience roles outside their normal departments and initially perform poorly. Senior officers also confront work/roles they normally delegate. The exercise exposes information asymmetry in both directions.
- Boimler continues retrying the Borg scenario after the evaluation should have ended because he is determined to obtain a perfect score. The repeated loops are separate simulation iterations and must not be conflated into one historical Borg encounter.
- Freeman becomes suspicious of the evaluator's methods and eventually turns an exercise back on Shari. The confrontation establishes that Shari's process and interpretation are themselves fallible rather than neutral omniscient measurement.
- The crew's final reconciliation supports a situational assertion that bridge and lower-deck roles depend on one another; it does not erase the earlier neglect or convert every drill result into truth.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Training-simulation casualty/event contamination must be prevented.
- Repeated simulations are separate iterations, not repeated real events.
- Numerical drill score is evidence of tool output under a scenario, not universal competence.
- Evaluator authority does not guarantee evaluator correctness.
- Institutional equality claims coexist with depicted differences in accommodations, information and decision authority.

## S02E09 — wej Duj

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences span the Cerritos, Klingon ship, Vulcan cruiser and Pakled ship, including Boimler, Mariner, Tendi, Rutherford, Ransom, Shaxs, Kayshon, Ma'ah, Captain Dorg, T'Lyn, Captain Sokel, their crewmates and Pakled personnel. These are local occurrences within this episode; cross-work identity remains unresolved here.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The episode deliberately gives separate lower-deck perspectives on multiple ships. No single crew possesses the whole causal story during most of the episode.
- Ma'ah initially tries to advance within Klingon hierarchy, then learns Captain Dorg is secretly supplying Pakleds with weapons and information to destabilize the Federation. Dorg directly states this plan; it is stronger evidence than Cerritos officers' later speculation about how Pakleds obtained Klingon weapons.
- Dorg's rhetoric about honor conflicts with his covert proxy strategy. Ma'ah explicitly challenges the mismatch, later kills Dorg in combat and is recognized by the Klingon crew as captain.
- T'Lyn modifies Vulcan sensors outside assignment parameters based partly on what she calls instinct. Her anomaly detection leads the Vulcan ship toward the crisis and her untested regenerative-shield program materially contributes to survival.
- Captain Sokel acknowledges that T'Lyn's program saved them but simultaneously characterizes her methods/emotionality as unacceptable and reassigns her to Starfleet. Operational success therefore does not equal institutional approval.
- Boimler lies that he is from Hawaii after encountering a social group built around that identity. The later crisis prompts multiple members, including Ransom, to admit they also fabricated Hawaiian origins. Their earlier claims remain false self-presentations even though the friendship that forms around them is real.
- Cerritos officers infer after the battle that Klingons may be connected to the Pakled weapons. Their inference is supported by what the audience has directly seen, but the officers themselves do not possess the episode's full Klingon-side evidence.
- The multi-ship structure is therefore an important epistemic fixture: audience-level juxtaposition must not be rewritten as knowledge held by every local character.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Cross-perspective assembly can support a causal account without granting omniscience to any participant.
- Klingon honor rhetoric -> contradicted by Dorg's proxy-war conduct.
- T'Lyn's successful deviation -> followed by discipline/reassignment rather than institutional validation.
- False Hawaiian identities -> later explicit confessions.
- Cerritos post-battle inference -> supported externally by episode structure but remains an inference from their local perspective.

## S02E10 — First First Contact

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include the Cerritos and Archimedes crews, Freeman, Mariner, Boimler, Tendi, Rutherford, Ransom, T'Ana, Shaxs, Kayshon, Captain Sonya Gomez, Lapeerians and Starfleet investigators. References to other Trek personnel remain source-relative references unless directly depicted.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Freeman begins expecting possible promotion/transfer and crew members react to the prospect. Her later decision to remain on Cerritos is a depicted change of decision, not evidence that the earlier promotion expectation was false.
- The Archimedes is disabled by the radiolytic-isotope event and pulled toward the Lapeerian planet. Sensor/tactical discussion supplies direct shipboard evidence of the danger and time-to-impact.
- Cerritos cannot safely approach with ordinary shielding because debris/plasma interactions would disable systems. The crew develops and executes a hull-stripping/manual EVA plan so the ship can survive the field. This is depicted collective action rather than a single person's accomplishment.
- Rutherford's implant malfunctions during the emergency; repair/diagnostic discussion and his participation should remain distinct from later-season revelations about the implant's origin.
- Boimler's dangerous Cetacean Ops effort and the crew's EVA work contribute to the rescue. His near-drowning and survival are real events, not simulation.
- Gomez allows Freeman to complete first contact after the rescue. The celebratory log/social claims about success are supported locally by the completed mission, while exact causal credit remains distributed across crews.
- Freeman explicitly decides to decline transfer after recognizing the value of her existing crew. This is a present decision, not proof that she can never later accept another command.
- T'Ana moves Tendi into senior science-officer training. This is a real role-development change at the episode boundary.
- At the end, Starfleet personnel arrive and arrest Freeman over the destruction/bombing of Pakled Planet. The arrest establishes an accusation and legal action, not guilt.
- The season therefore closes with a sharp evidence boundary: investigators possess or claim evidence sufficient for arrest, while the audience has prior evidence of Pakled/Klingon bomb activity. Neither stream should be collapsed before the later adjudication source is processed.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Promotion expectation -> later voluntary refusal, not contradiction.
- Collective rescue -> resist over-attributing success to one log speaker or protagonist.
- Arrest/charge -> evidence of legal accusation, not proof of culpability.
- Earlier Pakled/Klingon bomb evidence must remain separate from investigators' later theory until S3 supplies adjudication.
- Tendi's training assignment is an institutional status change, not a global identity change.

## Batch-level regression set

1. **Accidental intelligence disclosure:** Rumdar's boast reveals the hidden plan.
2. **Counter-deception:** Boimler's apparent alliance with AGIMUS reverses after the distress-signal reveal.
3. **False death evidence:** Paolana's scheme produces reasonable but incorrect death inferences.
4. **Simulation contamination:** all E08 drill casualties/world states remain training frames.
5. **Measurement fallibility:** drill scores/evaluator judgments are not omniscient truth.
6. **Distributed epistemic state:** E09 audience juxtaposition exceeds any single crew's knowledge.
7. **Proxy-war rhetoric conflict:** Dorg's honor claims conflict with directly depicted conduct.
8. **Legal accusation versus guilt:** Freeman's arrest in E10 does not establish culpability.
9. **Cross-work reference density:** referenced captains, ships and historical figures are not automatically imported as global assertions.
10. **Comedy is not a negation operator:** grotesque transformations, EVA danger and strange technology remain evidence when directly depicted.
