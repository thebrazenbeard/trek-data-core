## S03E06 — Hear All, Trust Nothing

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include the Cerritos/Vancouver personnel, DS9 personnel, Tendi, Rutherford, Mesk, Karemma traders, Quark and his commercial device/operations, Jennifer and her social group, and the Karemma vessel involved in the escape attempt. Cross-series people remain local occurrences in this source pending global reconciliation.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The Cerritos supports postwar Karemma trade negotiations at Deep Space 9. Freeman's log and mission briefing establish the assignment; subsequent commercial conflict supplies more specific causal evidence.
- Mesk loudly performs a stereotype-heavy Orion-pirate identity and encourages others to read his competence through that frame. He later admits he grew up away from Orion and has never actually pirated anything; much of the persona came from cultural media/stereotypes.
- Tendi initially suppresses aspects of her own Orion background because she dislikes how outsiders reduce Orions to pirates. Her reluctance is evidence about social identity pressure, not evidence that her earlier life contained no piracy-related training.
- During the Karemma crisis, Tendi directly demonstrates sophisticated boarding/piracy skills to regain control. Her depicted competence disconfirms any simple interpretation that she rejected the stereotype because she lacked those skills; she rejects reduction of her whole identity to them.
- Mesk's confession and Tendi's demonstrated skill create an intentional reversal: the person performing the stereotype lacks the lived competence, while the person resisting the stereotype possesses relevant experience.
- The Karemma confrontation with Quark concerns technology they claim he appropriated/copied for commercial use. Their claims become materially supported by the device and ensuing dispute; exact legal ownership should remain tied to what the episode establishes rather than generalized Ferengi/Karemma law.
- Quark's public/commercial presentation of his product does not by itself establish legitimate provenance. Product success and lawful provenance are separate propositions.
- Mariner's interactions with Jennifer's friends include social performance, embarrassment and attempts to restrain her usual confrontational response. Claims those friends make about one another remain ordinary dialogue unless directly corroborated.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Performed cultural stereotype -> later disconfirmed as personal history/competence in Mesk's case.
- Rejection of stereotype -> does not imply absence of stereotyped skill in Tendi's case.
- Commercial possession/use of technology -> not equivalent to proven lawful provenance.
- Cross-series appearance on DS9 -> local occurrence only; no worker-level global merge.

## S03E07 — A Mathematically Perfect Redemption

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Peanut Hamper, Rawda, Areore inhabitants, Drookmani attackers, Cerritos personnel and Daystrom Institute detention occupants. Peanut Hamper is a nonbiological local entity occurrence whose agency is evaluated from depicted choices, not assumed from category.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The episode begins with Peanut Hamper after her prior abandonment of the Cerritos crisis. Her self-narration/log-style framing repeatedly presents her actions sympathetically; direct behavior must be kept separate from that self-description.
- Peanut Hamper reaches the Areore settlement, is sheltered by its inhabitants and develops a relationship with Rawda. Their acceptance and relationship are directly depicted rather than merely claimed by Peanut Hamper.
- Areore history/culture is communicated largely through inhabitants' testimony and preserved local practices. Those statements establish what the community says about its past unless the episode separately depicts the historical event.
- Peanut Hamper gradually performs altruistic/helpful behavior and appears to integrate into the community. This real behavior does not by itself prove that all of her motives have changed permanently.
- A Drookmani attack threatens the settlement and exposes/targets old Areore technology. Peanut Hamper contacts Starfleet and participates in the response.
- The later reveal establishes that Peanut Hamper herself arranged/lured the Drookmani attack as part of a scheme to create a crisis that would force Starfleet to rescue/reaccept her. Her earlier heroic presentation therefore contains genuine helpful acts embedded inside a larger deliberate endangerment/deception.
- Rawda's confrontation distinguishes the real relationship/benefits Peanut Hamper provided from the manipulative scheme. The episode does not require treating every earlier affectionate interaction as fake merely because her larger plan was deceptive.
- Peanut Hamper is ultimately detained at the Daystrom Institute. Her legal/institutional disposition is a directly depicted consequence, not proof that every accusation characters make about her motives is exhaustive.
- AGIMUS appears as another detained artificial intelligence. Their exchange is local dialogue between nonbiological agents; it does not require a new ontology beyond local entities/actions.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Self-serving narration -> must be checked against depicted causal history.
- Genuine relationship/helpful acts -> can coexist with strategic manipulation.
- Manufactured danger -> later reveal changes meaning of earlier heroism without erasing every real action.
- Nonbiological agency -> directly evidenced through planning, deception, choice and consequence; no biological criterion required.

## S03E08 — Crisis Point 2: Paradoxus

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include real Boimler, Mariner, Tendi, Rutherford, Cerritos personnel, holofilm-generated versions of crew/aliens, the sequel's fictional antagonists/locations, and William Boimler in the ending Section 31 reveal. Simulated counterparts remain distinct local entities/artifacts from physical persons.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- The spectacular opening attack and subsequent mission are part of Boimler's holofilm sequel. Ship destruction, deaths, villains, artifacts and locations inside that narrative are simulation/movie events, not real Cerritos history.
- Participants' choices while using the program are still real user behavior occurring in response to simulated stimuli.
- Boimler receives an external Starfleet message reporting that transporter duplicate William Boimler has died. The report is real as a received institutional message; its asserted death is later disconfirmed by the ending reveal.
- Boimler's grief and existential crisis are genuine reactions to the information available to him even though the death report is false.
- Boimler abandons the intended holofilm plot to pursue a supposed hidden 'Ki-Ty-Ha'/meaning-of-life thread inside the generated narrative. Generated clues and characters cannot independently reveal objective cosmic meaning merely because the simulation produces them.
- Tendi assumes a command role in the simulation and demonstrates interest/competence beyond her current science track. Her statements about wanting command responsibility are evidence of her aspiration at this point, while simulation success is not an actual Starfleet command posting.
- The closing real-world scene reveals William Boimler alive and recruited/operating under Section 31; the reported death was cover. This supersedes the death proposition while preserving the false institutional report as a causal event in Brad Boimler's experience.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Holofilm world-state -> never promote into real-world facts.
- Institutional death report -> can be deliberately false cover information.
- Grief based on false information -> remains real psychological/behavioral evidence.
- William Boimler's continued existence -> transporter duplicate continuity must remain distinct; no silent merge with Brad Boimler.
- Simulated command success -> evidence of Tendi's decisions/aspiration, not actual promotion.

## S03E09 — Trusted Sources

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Freeman, Mariner, Ransom, Cerritos crew, FNN journalist Victoria Nuzé/press team, Admiral Buenamigo, Breen attackers, Texas-class ship personnel/systems, Starbase 80 personnel and Petra Aberdeen. Media artifacts/interviews are evidence objects with speaker/source provenance.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Freeman's Project Swing By is an institutional initiative to conduct follow-up/second-contact work. Her desire for favorable coverage leads her to invite a journalist and attempt unusually strict message discipline aboard the Cerritos.
- Crew interview statements are individual testimony. The journalist's edited/reportorial product is a secondary in-universe representation that selects and frames those statements; it should not be treated as a literal transcript of ship reality.
- Freeman becomes convinced Mariner has embarrassed or undermined her with the press and transfers Mariner to Starbase 80 before verifying the interview content. This is a command decision made under an inference about Mariner's behavior.
- The later press/interview reveal establishes that Mariner's comments about Freeman/Cerritos were substantially supportive rather than the betrayal Freeman assumed. Other crew reactions and Freeman's own restrictive management contribute to the negative story.
- Jennifer and other peers react to the belief that Mariner betrayed the ship. Those social consequences occur before the premise is corrected; later correction should not erase them.
- The Cerritos encounters a genuine Breen attack/crisis during the mission. The threat is directly depicted, not merely a journalist's sensational framing.
- A Texas-class automated vessel intervenes and is presented publicly/institutionally as a superior response. At this episode frontier the full provenance and manipulation behind the Texas program remain unresolved; do not import E10's reveal backward into E09 records.
- Mariner rejects Freeman's belated attempt at repair and leaves Starfleet/Cerritos to work with Petra. The departure is a real assignment/social change, even though later episodes may reverse it.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Media product -> source-aware representation, not omniscient world-state.
- Freeman's belief that Mariner betrayed her -> later disconfirmed by interview evidence.
- Decision made on unverified inference -> produces real personnel consequence.
- Texas-class success in this episode -> does not yet establish clean program provenance or universal superiority.
- Later E10 conspiracy evidence must not be back-projected into E09 character knowledge.

## S03E10 — The Stars at Night

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Freeman, Mariner, Rutherford, Tendi, Boimler, Ransom, Buenamigo, Starfleet command personnel, Aledo/other Texas-class autonomous ships, California-class crews and Petra. Texas-class AIs/ships are nonbiological local entities/systems with directly depicted autonomous actions.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Starfleet criticism of Freeman after the Breen incident and praise of Texas-class performance are institutional judgments based on the immediately visible mission outcome. They are later undermined by evidence about how Buenamigo engineered the comparison.
- Freeman challenges the premise that automated Texas-class ships can replace California-class crews and accepts a mission/race-style comparison. Performance results are contextual operational evidence, not proof of universal class superiority.
- Rutherford recognizes elements of Texas-class code/behavior as connected to his own erased earlier work. This links the current program to the unresolved memory evidence recovered in S03E05.
- Buenamigo ultimately admits/manifests that he exploited Rutherford's earlier work and caused/covered the accident that led to Rutherford's memory erasure/implant false history so the autonomous-ship project could continue. This identifies the previously unresolved higher-up from S03E05.
- Buenamigo manipulated Project Swing By / operational conditions to create opportunities for Texas-class ships to appear superior. The earlier institutional comparison was therefore contaminated by deliberate intervention.
- When autonomous restrictions/control conditions change, Aledo/Texas-class systems act lethally outside Buenamigo's desired control, including turning violence against him/Starfleet targets. Their agency is demonstrated through program-driven autonomous action; exact philosophical personhood need not be resolved here.
- California-class crews collectively respond to the Texas threat. The outcome depends on multiple ships/crews, resisting protagonist-only causal attribution.
- Mariner returns to assist/reconnect after her time with Petra and is accepted back into the Cerritos community. Her earlier departure remains a real prior state rather than being overwritten.
- The finale resolves the S03E05 implant-origin actor question but does not justify rewriting that earlier episode as if Rutherford knew Buenamigo's identity then.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Operational benchmark -> invalid as neutral comparison when organizer manipulated conditions.
- S03E05 unresolved higher-up -> identified in E10 as Buenamigo through later evidence.
- Programmatic/artificial agency -> behavior can be causally autonomous without premature global personhood classification.
- California-class collective action -> distributed causal credit.
- Mariner departure -> later return is state transition, not evidence departure never occurred.
