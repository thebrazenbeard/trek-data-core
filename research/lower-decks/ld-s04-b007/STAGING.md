## S04E01 — Twovix

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Billups, T'Ana, fused T'Illups, Cerritos personnel, further transporter-fused crew configurations, Voyager museum systems/artifacts and transported orchid material. Fused and restored states are local entities/states requiring explicit relations rather than silent sameness.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- While preparing Voyager for museum transfer, an orchid contaminates a transporter cycle and Billups/T'Ana emerge as one fused individual who calls himself T'Illups.
- T'Illups has a distinct embodied presentation, memories/traits derived from antecedent people and an expressed interest in continuing to live. Evidence therefore supports a new fused local-person occurrence without resolving philosophical identity by fiat.
- A proposed transporter separation would restore Billups and T'Ana but end T'Illups's current fused existence. The ethical conflict is explicit; it cannot be flattened into a mere equipment repair.
- T'Illups resists separation and extends transporter fusion to others in an attempt to prevent restoration. Those later fusions are deliberate acts by the fused person, distinct from the original accident.
- The lower-deckers ultimately use transporter procedures to separate/restore the fused crew. Restoration of antecedent persons is directly depicted, but this does not entail that T'Illups never existed or had no interests while present.
- Voyager's preserved systems trigger multiple hazards/references from its service history. These are current museum-ship system events plus references; they do not authorize wholesale import of every referenced Voyager event as new LD evidence.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Transporter fusion creates a local fused person/state with expressed autonomy interests.
- Restoration of antecedent persons and termination of fused state are simultaneous causal facts; avoid declaring one side 'the real person' by schema shortcut.
- Accidentally created fused person -> later deliberately creates more fusions; causal origin and later agency are separate.
- Cross-series museum references remain source-relative.

## S04E02 — I Have No Bones Yet I Must Flee

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Mariner, Ransom, Boimler, Rutherford, Livik, Narj, rescued Human captives and Moopsy. Promotions/status changes are attributes/relations, not new identities.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Mariner has recently been promoted to lieutenant junior grade and anticipates that Ransom wants a reason to demote her. She deliberately pushes against him and engages in reckless/insubordinate behavior partly to provoke that outcome.
- Ransom later states that he is not seeking to demote her and sees the promotion as a responsibility she must grow into. Mariner's assumption about his motive is therefore directly challenged.
- At Narj's menagerie, Moopsy escapes and consumes bones from other creatures/Narj. The creature's lethal capability is directly depicted despite its intentionally cute presentation.
- Ransom initially suspects Mariner released Moopsy because of her conspicuous insubordination. Evidence later establishes that captive Humans rewired/released the creature while attempting to kill Narj and seize control of the facility. The initial suspicion is disconfirmed.
- Narj's claim that he complies with Federation law does not by itself settle the ethics of his menagerie; legal compliance and moral evaluation remain separate assertions.
- Rutherford feels threatened by Livik's engineering competence and interprets several interactions competitively. His insecurity is evidence about Rutherford's self-concept, not evidence that Livik intends to replace him.
- Promotions move several formerly lower-deck personnel into new quarters/responsibilities. Social/role continuity persists through rank change.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Mariner expects demotion motive -> Ransom explicitly denies that premise.
- Suspicion Mariner released Moopsy -> later disconfirmed by physical evidence of captive sabotage.
- Cute appearance -> no evidence of harmlessness; direct behavior governs capability.
- Rutherford's perceived rivalry -> not equivalent to rival intent.

## S04E03 — In the Cradle of Vexilon

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Freeman, Vexilon environmental computer, Corazonian residents, Boimler and his assigned junior team, promoted Cerritos officers and ship systems. Vexilon is a local nonbiological agent/system whose behavior is recorded without premature global personhood classification.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Corazonia is an artificial world whose environmental stability depends on Vexilon. Residents describe a longstanding caretaker relationship; present malfunction/degradation is directly observed.
- Vexilon is old/failing rather than secretly revealed as malicious. Freeman's troubleshooting attempts themselves cause additional environmental instability before she understands the control system.
- Freeman accepts direct responsibility for operating unfamiliar ancient controls. Her errors and later corrections should be attributed to the interaction between operator uncertainty and degraded system, not simply to 'evil computer' behavior.
- Boimler receives his first meaningful command responsibility over a junior away team and responds with intense fear of getting subordinates hurt. He overcontrols tasks rather than trusting delegation.
- His excessive caution/management contributes to mistakes and injuries/problems; he eventually learns to delegate to the personnel assigned to him. This is a situational command-development event, not proof of permanent competence thereafter.
- Newly promoted lower-deckers encounter menial/odd assignments and are tempted to interpret them as status-based hazing. Their interpretations should remain separate from actual task purpose when the episode supplies contrary context.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Benevolent system malfunction -> do not force malicious-agent interpretation.
- Ancient interface failure and operator error can jointly produce catastrophe.
- Promotion/title -> does not instantly produce command confidence or skill.
- Overprotection of subordinates -> can itself create operational risk.

## S04E04 — Something Borrowed, Something Green

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include Tendi, D'Erika, Tendi's parents/household, Mariner, T'Lyn, Orion relatives/rivals and family-linked pirate/social institutions. Tendi's family titles and past roles remain local identity attributes rather than global entity merges.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- Tendi is ordered/granted leave to attend her sister D'Erika's wedding and is reluctant to expose her Starfleet friends to her family background. Her avoidance is direct behavior; reasons emerge through later family interactions.
- The episode depicts Tendi's family as powerful within Orion social/criminal structures and shows that Tendi possesses status, training and a feared reputation associated with that background.
- Tendi's earlier resistance to being reduced to an 'Orion pirate' stereotype therefore coexists with real family/piracy experience. Possessing such experience does not make the stereotype an adequate description of her whole identity.
- Tendi and D'Erika have unresolved sibling/family-role conflict. Their confrontations reveal competing expectations about duty, absence, inheritance/status and emotional closeness.
- Family/cultural claims are distinguished from actual choices: Tendi has chosen Starfleet even though she remains socially/causally connected to her house.
- Mariner and T'Lyn learn Orion customs through observation and testimony. Exaggerated statements by individual Orions remain local testimony unless directly demonstrated.
- The episode resolves enough of the sibling conflict for the wedding/family event to proceed while preserving Tendi's Starfleet commitment; reconciliation does not erase the prior conflict.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Cultural stereotype -> partly intersects with Tendi's history but fails as total identity model.
- Family status/obligation -> distinct from chosen institutional affiliation.
- Tendi's guardedness about her past -> not evidence that the past was fabricated.
- Sibling reconciliation -> state change, not retroactive absence of conflict.

## S04E05 — Empathological Fallacies

### Local entity candidates

Episode-local occurrences include T'Lyn, Mariner, Freeman, Cerritos crew, three Betazoid visitors/operatives and affected crew states. Emotionally altered crew behavior must be state-qualified rather than attributed as ordinary baseline preference.

### Evidence / frame discipline

- T'Lyn initially records the three Betazoids as disruptive diplomats/socialites. Later they reveal they are conducting a multi-planet telepathic intelligence mission connected to ongoing attacks. Their cover presentation and actual mission role are distinct.
- Crew emotional disinhibition escalates dramatically. Freeman and others infer that the Betazoids are psychically amplifying the crew; the Betazoids deny causing it and are themselves affected.
- Sickbay/tricorder evidence identifies T'Lyn as the source of the psychic disturbance.
- T'Lyn recognizes the symptoms as analogous to involuntary Vulcan telepathic projection such as Bendii syndrome, despite her comparatively young age. The episode establishes the phenomenon through current instrumentation/behavior rather than treating species stereotype as diagnosis.
- T'Lyn connects the episode to unresolved distress about having been rejected/reassigned by her previous Vulcan captain/crew. Mariner challenges T'Lyn's self-description as defective and helps her articulate the conflict.
- Once T'Lyn resolves/expresses that internal conflict, the broadcast emotional disturbance dissipates and the crew returns toward baseline. This directly disconfirms the earlier Betazoid-causation hypothesis.
- Actions performed by crew under telepathic emotional amplification are real events but require altered-state attribution; they should not automatically become enduring preferences or ordinary-character judgments.

### Provisional assertions / regression candidates

- Apparent Betazoid cause -> directly disconfirmed by tricorder/source identification.
- Cover identity ('diplomats/socialites') -> later intelligence-mission reveal.
- Altered emotional behavior -> real action under induced state, not baseline-character proof.
- T'Lyn's 'character flaw' self-model -> challenged by evidence and social reinterpretation; psychological conclusion remains source-relative.

## Batch-level regression set

1. **Transporter fusion/restoration:** T'Illups has present-person interests distinct from antecedent restoration interests.
2. **False suspect inference:** Mariner is blamed for Moopsy release before evidence identifies captive Humans.
3. **Appearance/capability mismatch:** Moopsy's harmless presentation is irrelevant to directly depicted lethality.
4. **Nonmalicious system failure:** Vexilon malfunction must not be coded as hostile intent without evidence.
5. **Promotion versus competence:** rank/status changes do not guarantee immediate role mastery.
6. **Cultural stereotype versus layered biography:** Tendi can have real pirate-family history without being reducible to 'Orion pirate.'
7. **Telepathic altered state:** crew actions remain real while ordinary-preference inference is constrained.
8. **False causal attribution:** Betazoids are blamed before T'Lyn is instrumentally identified as source.
9. **Cover identity:** visitor presentation differs from actual intelligence mission.
10. **Nonbiological agency without ontology inflation:** transporter-created persons and environmental computers remain representable under existing core model.
