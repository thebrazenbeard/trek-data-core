## S05E01 — Dos Cerritos

- A quantum fissure takes the prime Cerritos into a parallel reality containing another Cerritos and counterpart crew. Similar names, appearances and histories are counterpart evidence, not SAME_AS evidence.
- The alternate ship is commanded by a Mariner counterpart using the name Captain Becky Freeman. She governs through fear and discipline, while prime Mariner explicitly rejects that command style.
- Counterpart differences are concrete: alternate Boimler has different confidence/command experience; alternate Rutherford has modified himself and removed memories connected to his Tendi after that counterpart chose full-time piracy; alternate Carol Freeman has a different career outcome.
- Prime Rutherford modifies the alternate Rutherford's friendship matrix, changing the counterpart's presented social state. This is a direct cybernetic/state intervention, not proof that either Rutherford was the 'original' of the other.
- Captain Becky incapacitates prime Mariner and swaps places with her in an attempt to remain in the prime reality. Presence aboard the other ship therefore becomes a replacement/deception event rather than simple travel.
- Prime Mariner convinces the alternate crew that she is not their captain and helps reverse the swap. Both Mariners return to their originating realities.
- The alternate crew's later action against their own abusive captain is evidence of their agency; it is not evidence that prime Mariner commands them.
- Tendi separately continues work for House Tendi to repay the Orion warship debt incurred during the S4 rescue. Her pirate role is an actual temporary family assignment, not an alternate-reality counterpart.
- Regression: counterpart identity; cross-reality replacement; memory editing; same-looking ship/crew does not authorize global merge.

## S05E02 — Shades of Green

- Tendi remains with House Tendi and competes against a rival Orion house while trying to settle her family obligation and return to Starfleet.
- D'Erika's private logs reveal concerns/motives that are not initially communicated directly to Tendi. Preserve the log as source evidence rather than treating Tendi as having known it at the time.
- Tendi resolves the rival-house contest by destroying the disputed treasure so neither side can claim victory. Orion authorities punish the competing houses economically; the outcome is real even though Tendi's intent is compromise rather than conquest.
- D'Erika releases Tendi from the obligation and explicitly encourages her to return to the life she wants in Starfleet. Tendi's return therefore follows a family/social decision, not an escape from a fabricated debt.
- D'Erika's pregnancy/future-child concerns help contextualize her behavior. That later disclosure should not be back-projected into earlier scenes as information Tendi already possessed.
- On Targalus IX, Cerritos personnel assist a new Federation member transitioning away from a monetary/capitalist structure after acquiring post-scarcity technology. Wealthy residents' resistance is stakeholder behavior, not proof that the entire society rejects Federation transition.
- The Targalus economic transition and House Tendi's loss/redeployment of wealth intersect at the end, but one does not make every prior Orion family conflict economically motivated.
- Regression: private log versus character knowledge; family obligation versus chosen career; compromise producing punitive institutional response; societal transition versus individual resistance.

## S05E03 — The Best Exotic Nanite Hotel

- A nanite cluster on the Cosmic Duchess consumes material to self-replicate. The cluster's physical effects are directly depicted; crew nicknames such as 'Glumpus' are labels, not formal taxonomy.
- Attempts to lure/capture the nanites alter their behavior and scale. Instrumental observations are stronger evidence for capability than characters' early guesses about motive.
- The nanites are influenced by an external signal. T'Lyn uses a resonant musical/instrumental intervention to disrupt the signal and collapse the larger formation.
- The post-event scan reveals a miniature Intrepid-class starship within the nanite remains. At this episode frontier the ship's precise identity, origin and relation to the nanite activity remain unresolved; do not import later answers backward.
- Jennifer behaves as though she and Mariner are still a couple, forcing explicit conversation about their prior breakup. They finally clarify that the relationship ended while retaining the possibility of functional friendship/teamwork.
- Jennifer's earlier affectionate behavior therefore reflects her chosen interpersonal strategy, not objective evidence that the prior breakup never happened.
- Boimler participates in an undercover effort with Ransom and Billups to retrieve an absent officer. Their civilian/cover presentation remains mission identity rather than ordinary biographical state.
- Regression: external signal versus nanite intrinsic motive; unresolved miniature ship; ambiguous relationship behavior resolved by explicit conversation; undercover role versus ordinary identity.

## S05E04 — A Farewell to Farms

- Ma'ah has lost his ship command after prior mutiny-related institutional consequences and is living/working on his family farm. His rank/status loss is a role transition, not identity discontinuity.
- Mariner and Boimler help Ma'ah invoke old Klingon procedures through which a discharged captain can seek restoration. Ritual/legal claims are institution-specific rules in this source.
- Ma'ah reveals that he does not actually want restoration into Bargh's fleet. His earlier participation therefore combines obligation/pride with ambivalence rather than a simple desire for the old post.
- The political/ritual maneuver ultimately changes Ma'ah's standing, but he chooses a different command path: captaining the family transport with Malor as first officer rather than simply returning to the former ship.
- Mariner later reveals a second mission purpose: access to a quantum fissure Bargh had blocked. The fissure scan suggests the anomalies are artificial/man-made rather than naturally occurring. This is a new scientific/causal clue, not yet identification of the creator.
- A parallel Cerritos mission involves Migleemo and Klowahkan food critics. Their repeated positive reviews conflict with sensory evidence; they eventually admit they have lost their sense of taste under professional stress.
- Medical interpretation identifies a stress-related conversion disorder. Earlier reviews therefore remain genuine published records with compromised sensory provenance rather than reliable food-quality measurement.
- Regression: role restoration versus chosen new command; disclosed secondary mission motive; fissure artificiality without creator identity; expert review versus impaired measurement.

## S05E05 — Starbase 80?!

- Cerritos reaches Starbase 80 after a navigation malfunction. Mariner initially interprets the station through her earlier negative experience and repeatedly calls it cursed.
- Starbase personnel appear unconventional and under-resourced but are locally adapted to old infrastructure. Mariner's stereotype is progressively disconfirmed by their competence and willingness to help.
- Chief engineer Gene initially uses deception/subterfuge to obtain overdue Starfleet repair assistance. His misleading behavior is real while his underlying infrastructure need is also real.
- Cerritos crew members begin showing abnormal behavior. Because Starbase locals are unaffected, Mariner initially interprets the contrast as support for the station curse.
- Nox notices the pattern is tied to Cerritos comm-badge usage, shifting the causal hypothesis away from Starbase 80.
- Medical investigation identifies an anaphasic consciousness controlling/communicating through a Cerritos host and spreading through the crew via the communication network. Affected crew actions require altered-agency attribution.
- The entity later identifies itself as Clem and explains that it attempted communication/proof of competence but overwhelmed itself by merging with too many hosts. This is entity testimony paired with directly observed possession/network effects.
- Clem is given a stable vessel at Starbase 80 and agrees not to take hosts. Resolution preserves the entity as an agent rather than reducing the event to an impersonal 'curse.'
- Freeman's conduct is partly driven by comparison with the alternate Freeman who had been sent to Starbase 80. Cross-reality information affects prime decision-making without making the alternate history prime history.
- Regression: place stigma versus local evidence; apparent curse versus transported anaphasic cause; host actions under possession; entity communication motive versus unintended overload; alternate-counterpart history influencing but not replacing prime history.
