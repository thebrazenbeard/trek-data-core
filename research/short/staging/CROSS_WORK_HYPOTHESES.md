## H01 — constrained self-presentation and concealment are recurring mechanisms for negotiating agency

### Candidate hypothesis

Short Treks repeatedly places characters in situations where what they reveal, hide, perform, or allow others to infer materially affects their agency and relationships.

### Supporting evidence

- **Runaway**: Po initially conceals both her presence and later her royal status; her explanation for withholding the title is that people listen differently after learning it. Tilly likewise begins from institutional pressure to report the unauthorized visitor.
- **The Brightest Star**: Saru secretly retains forbidden Ba'ul technology and uses it to construct a beacon after authority explicitly orders disposal (`THE_BRIGHTEST_STAR.md` E03-E05).
- **The Escape Artist**: the fraud scheme depends on manufactured Mudd-presenting duplicates, shifting testimony, and physical identity disguise (`THE_ESCAPE_ARTIST.md` E02-E08).
- **Q&A**: Spock describes long-term suppression of parts of himself, while Number One simultaneously values difference and counsels managed command presentation; she then reveals an unconventional interest and immediately orders professional concealment (`Q_AND_A.md` E08-E12).
- **The Trouble with Edward**: Larkin conducts the prohibited genetic intervention despite a direct command to stop the line of work (`THE_TROUBLE_WITH_EDWARD.md` E03-E06); the work distinguishes acknowledged expertise from concealed/unauthorized conduct.
- **Ask Not**: the institution deliberately conceals the true test frame, Pike's status inside the exercise, the reality of the external crisis, and the falsity of Sidhu's earlier rejection in order to observe her decisions under believed conditions (`ASK_NOT.md` E01-E09).

### Neutral / weakly relevant evidence

- **Ephraim and Dot** centers conflict and caregiving across a largely nonverbal animated chase; concealment is not established as a primary mechanism by the staged record.
- **Children of Mars** is dominated by school conflict and an external catastrophe; the staged source limitations do not justify making concealment a central claim.

### Disconfirming / limiting evidence

- **Calypso** moves toward resolution through increasingly direct mutual disclosure: Craft discusses family and home, Zora exposes attachment and creates a visual representation, and the true-name exchange marks intimacy (`CALYPSO.md` E06-E11). Concealment is therefore not presented as the only or preferred route to agency.
- **The Girl Who Made the Stars** explicitly uses open narration and reinterpretation of fear to change Michael's immediate behavior; the father's disclosed story, rather than hidden identity, drives the change (`THE_GIRL_WHO_MADE_THE_STARS.md` E02-E10).

### Staging result

**SUPPORTED AS A RECURRENT MECHANISM, NOT A UNIVERSAL THEME.** Concealment/self-presentation appears across multiple unrelated plots, but other works make disclosure, storytelling, or direct recognition the productive mechanism instead. The evidence does not support a claim that Short Treks treats concealment as inherently good, bad, or franchise-defining.

## H02 — institutional and machine-mediated information is actionable but repeatedly shown as non-omniscient

### Candidate hypothesis

Across Short Treks, databases, scanners, official records, technical diagnoses, inquiries, orders, and institutional simulations often guide action while remaining source-relative, incomplete, stale, deceptive, or revisable.

### Supporting evidence

- **Runaway**: tricorder and computer/database outputs classify Po and flag her strategic importance; the staged record preserves these as reports rather than omniscient truth.
- **Calypso**: Zora's records incorrectly show no human settlement on Alcor IV despite Craft's presence and testimony, while Zora reports almost a millennium of autonomous development (`CALYPSO.md` E03-E04, A04). This is direct evidence of institutional-data staleness.
- **The Escape Artist**: superficial identity expectations initially treat a delivered captive as Mudd; deeper inspection reclassifies it as a manufactured duplicate (`THE_ESCAPE_ARTIST.md` E05-E07, A03).
- **The Trouble with Edward**: scientific proposals, crew analysis, Larkin's admission, ship-system reports, and the later inquiry each provide different layers of information; Lucero's single-actor causal judgment is explicitly preserved as testimony rather than a complete causal decomposition (`THE_TROUBLE_WITH_EDWARD.md` E02-E10, A03).
- **Ask Not**: institutional information is intentionally false inside the exercise. The simulated attack, mutiny, threatened spouse, and rescue crisis are constructed inputs, while the post-reveal explanation establishes that the deception itself was real institutional conduct (`ASK_NOT.md` E01-E09, A01-A03).
- **Ephraim and Dot**: archival/montage references are explicitly prevented from becoming independent evidence for the other Trek works represented in the montage; representation inside the Short is not automatic external corroboration.

### Neutral / weakly relevant evidence

- **Q&A** contains system failure and repair communication, but the strongest source-relative issues are interpersonal testimony and inference rather than demonstrably false institutional data (`Q_AND_A.md` E03-E06).
- **The Brightest Star** includes Starfleet rule testimony and Georgiou's explanation of intervention limits, but this record does not establish those rules as erroneous; their importance is constraint, not demonstrated informational failure (`THE_BRIGHTEST_STAR.md` E08-E09).
- **The Girl Who Made the Stars** is governed chiefly by a story-within-story frame rather than machine or institutional information.

### Disconfirming / limiting evidence

The corpus does **not** support the stronger proposition that institutional or technical information is generally unreliable. In several works, system outputs and technical analyses function adequately enough for local action. The repeated pattern is narrower: **their evidentiary status remains mediated and revisable**, and the source does not authorize treating institutional origin as omniscience.

### Staging result

**SUPPORTED IN THE NARROW EPISTEMIC FORM; REJECTED IN THE STRONG 'SYSTEMS ARE UNRELIABLE' FORM.** The useful hypothesis concerns evidentiary discipline, not an anti-institutional thesis.

## H03 — frame transitions are a recurring cause of retrospective evidence reclassification

### Candidate hypothesis

Several Short Treks works deliberately reveal that material initially presented in one apparent narrative status belongs to another frame, requiring earlier evidence to be reclassified without being erased.

### Supporting evidence

- **The Escape Artist**: repeated apparent Mudd captures are retrospectively understood through the duplicate scheme. Earlier conversations still occurred, but identity and corroboration status change (`THE_ESCAPE_ARTIST.md` E04-E09, A01-A04).
- **The Trouble with Edward**: the post-incident inquiry is a different evidentiary frame from the shipboard events, and the final cereal advertisement has an explicitly unresolved literal-status relationship to ordinary continuity (`THE_TROUBLE_WITH_EDWARD.md` E09-E11, A04).
- **Ask Not**: the attack/mutiny/rescue sequence is explicitly terminated as a simulation. Pre-reveal claims remain evidence about Sidhu's perceived decision environment rather than evidence that the external crisis occurred (`ASK_NOT.md` E01-E07, A01-A03).
- **The Girl Who Made the Stars**: the ancient/cosmological events are explicitly embedded in a father's bedtime narration. They are evidence of the story told and its effect on Michael, not automatic ancient history (`THE_GIRL_WHO_MADE_THE_STARS.md` E02-E10, A01).
- **Ephraim and Dot**: archival/montage material depicts or references other Trek-era events from within an animated Short; the staging record therefore refuses to convert montage appearance into independent evidence for those external works.

### Neutral / weakly relevant evidence

- **Runaway**, **Calypso**, **The Brightest Star**, and **Q&A** contain source-relative testimony, reports, inference, or self-presentation but do not depend on a comparable large-scale frame reveal in the staged records.
- **Children of Mars** presents a different problem: the available transcript/subtitle representation underspecifies the visual narrative. That is a **source-representation limitation**, not itself a diegetic frame transition.

### Disconfirming / limiting evidence

At least four staged works proceed largely without a major frame reclassification. The hypothesis therefore cannot be upgraded to "Short Treks stories are generally unreliable narratives." The recurring pattern is that **when** frame transitions occur, they are structurally important and materially change what propositions the evidence can support.

### Staging result

**STRONGLY SUPPORTED AS A RECURRING FORMAL DEVICE IN A SUBSET; NOT CORPUS-UNIVERSAL.** This is also a useful regression family for future audit because flattening any one of these frames can create false world-state facts.

## H04 — agency is often depicted as action under fear, social pressure, or uncertainty rather than the elimination of those conditions

### Candidate hypothesis

A recurring Short Treks pattern treats agency/courage as choosing and acting while fear, uncertainty, social judgment, or institutional pressure remains present.

### Supporting evidence

- **The Brightest Star**: Saru explicitly experiences an urge to flee back to safety yet remains for contact and later chooses departure; his closing interpretation frames hope as stronger than fear without claiming fear vanished (`THE_BRIGHTEST_STAR.md` E06, E09-E10, A03).
- **The Girl Who Made the Stars**: both Michael and the inner protagonist remain afraid while changing behavior through curiosity/action; Michael's ending shows immediate meaning uptake rather than permanent fear eradication (`THE_GIRL_WHO_MADE_THE_STARS.md` E01, E05-E10, A02, A04).
- **Ask Not**: Sidhu acts while believing her spouse, career, rank hierarchy, and surrounding crisis are all at stake; the test evaluates her decisions precisely because she does not know the scenario is false (`ASK_NOT.md` E03-E08, A02).
- **Runaway**: Po's decision to return is made in the context of fear of political exploitation and unwillingness/readiness conflict; Tilly's own command self-doubt remains present while she exercises judgment.
- **Q&A**: Spock and Number One negotiate the cost of self-suppression and social perception without the episode supplying a simple removal of institutional pressure (`Q_AND_A.md` E08-E12, A02).

### Neutral / weakly relevant evidence

- **Calypso** includes attachment, war trauma, and duty conflict, but the staged record's dominant arc is relational recognition and return rather than an explicit courage thesis.
- **Children of Mars** contains fear and catastrophe, but direct audiovisual verification is absent and the source-limited record should not be overextended into a cross-work thematic claim.

### Disconfirming / limiting evidence

- **The Escape Artist** demonstrates substantial agency through deception, fraud, and engineered duplicates rather than courage-under-fear. Agency itself is therefore morally and structurally broader than the candidate hypothesis.
- **The Trouble with Edward** contains decisive action under professional conflict, but Larkin's persistence under criticism produces reckless unauthorized experimentation. Persistence under pressure is not automatically courage or good judgment (`THE_TROUBLE_WITH_EDWARD.md` E03-E07, A01-A03).
- **Ephraim and Dot** is not adequately characterized by this hypothesis in the staged semantic record.

### Staging result

**SUPPORTED AS ONE RECURRING AGENCY PATTERN, WITH IMPORTANT COUNTEREXAMPLES.** The disconfirming works prevent a flattering simplification in which persistence under pressure is automatically valorized.

## Cross-hypothesis conclusions

1. No tested hypothesis applies cleanly to all ten staged titles.
2. The strongest corpus-level result is methodological rather than thematic: **frame, source type, institutional origin, repeated appearance, and character testimony repeatedly alter evidentiary weight without deleting the underlying occurrence.**
3. Identity claims are especially vulnerable to flattening in `Calypso`, `The Escape Artist`, `Ask Not`, and montage/archive material; these should remain regression candidates for later governed validation/audit.
4. The staged corpus does not support a simple anti-institutional, pro-rebellion, pro-concealment, or "courage conquers fear" synthesis. Each stronger formulation encounters direct neutral or disconfirming cases.
5. `Children of Mars` remains deliberately underweighted in cross-work interpretation because the available transcript/subtitle representation does not adequately encode its visual semantics.
