## Source-relative evidence

### E01 — Saru distinguishes his own response to the stars from prevailing Kelpien teaching
Kind: narration + cultural testimony. Saru describes seeing hope where his people are taught to see death and accept harvesting. Limit: his narration establishes his perspective and reported cultural norm, not exhaustive Kelpien unanimity.

### E02 — Vahar'ai and harvesting are embedded in a religious account of balance
Kind: dialogue/testimony. Saru and Aradar discuss the claim that harvest ends Vahar'ai pain and preserves the Great Balance; Aradar presents sacrifice as necessary for Ba'ul sustenance and communal peace. Limit: these are Kelpien teachings/claims, not independently verified cosmology.

### E03 — possession of Ba'ul technology is explicitly forbidden
Kind: dialogue + action. Aradar orders Saru to dispose of a fallen Ba'ul fragment and says keeping it is forbidden. Saru secretly retains and studies it.

### E04 — Saru openly questions inherited explanations and asymmetry of power
Kind: dialogue. Saru asks what lies beyond the sky, why Kelpiens cannot reach it, and why they sacrifice themselves instead of questioning whether they could equal the Ba'ul. Aradar orders him to stop questioning.

### E05 — Saru converts forbidden technology into a communication beacon
Kind: depiction + later corroborating dialogue. Saru manipulates the fragment and sends a signal seeking someone beyond Kaminar; Georgiou later identifies the technology as Ba'ul-derived and credits Saru with turning it into a beacon.

### E06 — Saru's threat response conflicts with his chosen direction
Kind: self-report/action. While away from the village he reports a powerful urge to run back to safety, yet remains to await contact. Limit: supports immediate conflict between fear response and choice, not a generalized trait claim.

### E07 — Siranna offers a counterweight to Saru's upward focus
Kind: dialogue/interpersonal. Siranna urges Saru to notice beauty on the ground as well as in the stars before leaving him to wait. Limit: supports her expressed perspective, not opposition to all exploration.

### E08 — Georgiou identifies Starfleet rules as limiting intervention
Kind: dialogue/institutional testimony. Georgiou says Saru's contact with a pre-warp society created difficulty, that Starfleet made an exception for him, and that complicated rules prevent her from doing more.

### E09 — departure is presented as effectively irreversible at that moment
Kind: dialogue/decision. Georgiou tells Saru that leaving means he will not be able to return home; Saru accepts the consequence and says his place is no longer there. Limit: establishes the decision framing at this point, not an eternal impossibility across later works.

### E10 — closing narration explicitly frames hope as stronger than fear
Kind: narration. Saru interprets his departure as moving toward hope despite fear. This is his self-understanding, not an omniscient judgment of all motives.

## Interpretive assertions

### A01 — inherited cosmology functions as both explanation and behavioral constraint
Supports: E01-E04. The Great Balance account explains harvesting while also discouraging technological curiosity and questioning. Counterlimit: the source does not independently establish whether every Kelpien accepts the account or whether the belief originated solely as coercive manipulation.

### A02 — technical agency emerges through epistemic dissent
Supports: E03-E06. Saru's refusal to accept the permitted explanation leads directly to experimental reuse of forbidden technology and contact beyond Kaminar.

### A03 — fear and agency are simultaneous rather than mutually exclusive
Supports: E06, E09, E10. The work does not depict courage as absence of fear; Saru explicitly experiences fear-oriented impulses while choosing departure.

### A04 — Starfleet contact is constrained rather than presented as unrestricted rescue
Supports: E08-E09. Georgiou describes rules and an exceptional allowance for Saru; the source therefore supports a bounded institutional response, not a general right of intervention.
