## Cross-work neutral coding and hypotheses

### Recurring dimensions

- **Chosen versus inherited roles:** Darem/Kaira in E07, Sam/Doctor/Makers in E08, Caleb/Anisha/Academy in E09–E10.
- **Trauma and adaptation:** Miyazaki aftermath, Tarima's changed self, Sam's reconstructed development, Caleb/Anisha/Nahla/Braka historical injury.
- **Identity continuity:** E08–E10 make Sam a direct identity-continuity edge case involving memory, self-identification, causal history, and developmental experience.
- **Epistemics:** encrypted messages, direct reunion, testimony, institutional models, simulations, scientific reconstruction, and computer reports are kept distinct.
- **Institutions:** Academy/Starfleet are depicted as both sources of belonging/opportunity and actors capable of procedural or historical harm.

### Provisional hypotheses

`B004-H1` The season-local narrative increasingly treats healthy belonging as integration of past and present rather than replacement of one by the other. Support: Darem, Tarima, Sam, Caleb/Anisha. Disconfirm if later sources require repudiation/erasure of prior identity to belong.

`B004-H2` Trauma recovery is not represented as simple return to a prior self. Support: Tarima's transfer/identity rupture, Sam's developmental reconstruction, Caleb/Anisha/Nahla's changed relationships. Disconfirm if later works explicitly restore pre-trauma identity without residue and treat that restoration as complete.

`B004-H3` Action repeatedly functions as counterevidence to characters' defensive self-narratives. Support: Darem returns, Caleb sends the message, Caleb turns back for friends, Caleb publicly claims Academy belonging. Disconfirm if later works demonstrate those actions were coerced or narratively reversed.

`B004-H4` Institutional self-correction is presented as possible but not exculpatory: acknowledging harm, changing plans, and making room for outsiders do not erase prior institutional damage. Support: Nahla/Anisha, Caleb's due-process history, Betazed compromise, finale trial. Disconfirm if later sources present institutional reform as retroactively canceling responsibility.
