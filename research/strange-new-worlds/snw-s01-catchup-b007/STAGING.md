## Neutral close-read staging notes

### S01E08 — The Elysian Kingdom

- M'Benga's daughter Rukiya has cygnokemia and is being preserved between treatments in the Enterprise transporter buffer. The episode directly establishes her medical condition and M'Benga's continued attempts to find a cure.
- While Enterprise is trapped in the Jonisian Nebula, most of the crew begin behaving as characters from the story M'Benga has been reading to Rukiya. Clothing, ship environments, perceived social roles, and behavior are altered to match the story frame.
- M'Benga remains aware of his ordinary identity while being treated by others as King Ridley. Hemmer also retains substantial self-awareness, attributing his resistance to Aenar telepathic training. Their retained awareness is counterevidence to treating the transformed crew behavior as voluntary ordinary-role preference.
- The transformed crew should therefore be coded under an altered-reality / induced-cognition frame. Pike's chamberlain role, La'An's princess role, Ortegas's guard role, and the other story performances are depicted actions but not stable personal identities.
- The altered story contains details that come from Rukiya's preferred revisions rather than M'Benga's version of the text. This helps M'Benga infer that the phenomenon is centered on Rukiya rather than simply on his own reading or memory.
- Hemmer offers a speculative model in which the nebula entity could resemble a spontaneously formed disembodied consciousness. The explanation is explicitly hypothesis-level and should not be promoted into settled ontology.
- The entity removes Rukiya from the transporter buffer and interacts with her directly. It reports sensing her loneliness and creating the fantasy environment to make her happy. Those motive claims are the entity's testimony; the reality alteration itself is directly depicted.
- Proximity to the entity removes the immediate effects of Rukiya's disease. The entity offers Rukiya a continued existence with it in which she would leave her diseased body behind rather than die from the illness.
- M'Benga tells Rukiya that the decision is hers. Rukiya chooses to remain with the entity. The choice is directly depicted, but its context includes childhood, terminal illness, parental attachment, and the entity's enormous power; the evidence should not be reduced to a generic adult-autonomy template.
- After the transition, an older-appearing Rukiya manifests to M'Benga and reports that what was only moments for him has been years for her. She displays memory and relational continuity with the child Rukiya while the episode simultaneously depicts a break from her prior diseased biological embodiment.
- The event therefore activates psychological/memory and biological continuity dimensions without resolving a global same-person/different-person identity question.
- After the phenomenon ends, the affected crew have no memory of roughly five hours and ordinary surveillance/logging evidence is absent. M'Benga retains memory of the event. Memory continuity, documentary absence, and depicted altered-reality events must remain separate evidentiary facts.

### S01E09 — All Those Who Wander

- Uhura begins the episode at the formal end of her Enterprise training rotation and remains uncertain whether Starfleet is her destination. Her personal log and conversations with Hemmer establish her current self-assessment, not an externally determined future career.
- Enterprise receives a delayed distress signal from USS Peregrine while also carrying time-sensitive vidium power cells. Pike splits the mission: Enterprise continues to K-7 under Una while a landing party investigates Peregrine. This is a command tradeoff under competing obligations rather than evidence that either obligation is unimportant.
- Peregrine Captain Gavin's recovered log states that the ship rescued three castaways, one of whom was an Orion infected with Gorn eggs. The Orion attempted to destroy himself/the infestation with a plasma grenade and Gavin's log warns Starfleet to stay away. This is documentary first-person evidence about the Peregrine crisis.
- Two survivors are found: the child Oriana and an alien she calls Buckley. Initial medical scans show no anomaly. Buckley later produces multiple Gorn hatchlings, directly disconfirming the adequacy of the earlier scans.
- La'An initially presses Oriana aggressively about the Gorn and why the survivors did not warn Peregrine. M'Benga intervenes. Later La'An recognizes similarities between Oriana's trauma and her own history and becomes protective of her. The sequence depicts changing behavior and interpretation, not a clinical diagnosis of either person.
- La'An supplies tactical claims based on prior Gorn experience: young Gorn mature quickly, compete for dominance, avoid cold, respond to aggression, and are dangerous even without adults. Several of these claims become operationally useful during the episode, but they remain experience-derived models rather than universal species laws.
- Peregrine sensor systems cannot detect the hatchlings. M'Benga and Spock later interpret the biology as sensor-evasive / genetically adaptive, and Hemmer reports being unable to perceive them telepathically. These observations strengthen the specific detection problem without proving complete knowledge of Gorn biology.
- Cadet Chia and Lieutenant Duke are killed by hatchlings. The deaths occur after ordinary scans or situational assumptions had failed to reveal the danger, providing counterevidence to confidence in those earlier safety assessments.
- Hemmer is struck by Gorn spray while protecting Uhura. The group initially treats the secretion as venom, but Hemmer recognizes changes in his own body and La'An identifies the spray as part of the reproductive process. The later evidence supersedes the narrower toxin-only interpretation.
- The crew deliberately exploits expected Gorn aggression and dominance competition. Spock intentionally evokes rage so a hatchling will respond; after the Gorn turn on each other, La'An and Hemmer use the cold environment to kill the surviving alpha.
- Spock's induced rage persists after the immediate tactical use. He later punches the corridor wall and tells Chapel he cannot control the rage and pain he has let out. Chapel's statement that this makes him human is her interpretation/supportive framing, not an objective species or identity rule.
- Hemmer concludes that Gorn eggs are developing inside him and that there is insufficient time for extraction. Chapel and M'Benga propose treatment attempts, but Hemmer rejects delay because he believes emergence would endanger the crew. He goes outside into lethal cold and dies by falling from the ship's position.
- Hemmer frames his act as a sacrifice that will save those he cares about and connects it to Aenar beliefs about purpose. Those beliefs and his reasoning are testimony; the physical infection, time pressure, and his fatal act are directly depicted.
- The memorial preserves distinct perspectives on Hemmer. Uhura adopts his earlier language about bonds, pain, and fixing what is broken, showing the causal impact of his mentorship without converting his personal philosophy into narrator-certified metaphysics.
- La'An finds a weak lead on Oriana's family and requests leave from Enterprise to pursue it outside Federation space. Pike grants the leave. The episode ends with an open-ended search commitment, not evidence that the family has already been found.

### S01E10 — A Quality of Mercy

- Pike meets Maat Al-Salah, one of the cadets he expects to be injured in the future training accident, and begins planning to warn the cadets in advance. This is an attempt to change a future Pike remembers/foresees rather than a neutral observation of an already changed history.
- An older Pike appears and warns that the intervention creates much worse downstream consequences. The episode presents the older figure as a future Pike and supports that presentation through private knowledge and the Boreth time-crystal mechanism. For worker records, the future-Pike occurrence should remain locally distinct; global identity reconciliation is not this lane's job.
- The time crystal places present Pike into a conditional future roughly seven years later. The episode therefore supplies evidence about a shown counterfactual/alternate future caused by Pike avoiding the original accident outcome, not direct evidence that the accepted baseline necessarily reaches every depicted event.
- In this conditional future Pike remains Enterprise captain during the Romulan incursion corresponding to the Neutral Zone crisis, while James Kirk commands Farragut. Pike emphasizes communication and restraint; Kirk argues that Romulan interpretations of hesitation can make restraint strategically dangerous.
- The Romulan commander himself repeatedly favors pragmatism, withdrawal, and avoiding unnecessary war, while a subordinate pushes a more aggressive course. This is strong counterevidence against flattening Romulans into a uniformly aggressive species characterization.
- Pike's proposed cease-fire creates a temporary opening for negotiation and repair. The Romulan Praetor later arrives with reinforcements, interprets the Federation posture as weakness, removes the original commander, and declares war. The causal chain includes multiple independent choices and should not be reduced to `Pike tried diplomacy -> war` as a single-variable fact.
- Kirk's response includes a bluff using unmanned mining vessels to simulate a larger Federation force. The episode therefore contrasts command strategies within the same conditional crisis rather than simply declaring one captain intrinsically correct in every context.
- Spock is catastrophically injured during this alternate outcome. Older Pike reports that the Boreth monks showed him repeated paths in which changing his own fate results in Spock dying, with consequences for the galaxy. This is highly salient future-Pike/time-crystal testimony but remains epistemically framed rather than an unqualified metaphysical theorem.
- The conditional future also depicts Una imprisoned for genetic modification. That state belongs to the shown conditional timeline and must not be imported as an already-established outcome of the restored present.
- Present Pike returns to his original time and deletes the warning he had prepared for Maat. He accepts the known accident future rather than intentionally displacing the harm onto Spock within the model he has been shown.
- The episode supports the narrower proposition that Pike's attempted fate-avoidance has catastrophic consequences in the future model he experiences. It does not establish that Pike lacks agency generally, that every possible unknown timeline is exhausted, or that all future events are immutable by universal law.
- After Pike restores his course, Una is arrested under the Federation prohibition concerning genetic modification. This is a present-timeline institutional event and should remain separate from the conditional future's version of Una's later imprisonment.

## Cross-episode frame and counterevidence traps

- **S01E08:** induced story roles are altered-reality performances; older-appearing Rukiya supplies psychological/memory continuity evidence while biological embodiment changes, so identity must remain dimensioned and unresolved globally.
- **S01E09:** initial medical/sensor clearance is explicitly disconfirmed; La'An's Gorn expertise is useful but model-based; Spock's rage is intentionally induced for tactical use and then persists beyond it; Hemmer's sacrifice includes both directly depicted biology/time pressure and his own cultural/purpose interpretation.
- **S01E10:** the seven-years-later sequence is a conditional future generated by attempted intervention, not ordinary accepted-timeline history; older Pike's repeated-path claim remains testimony within the time-crystal frame; Romulan internal disagreement prevents species-essentialist causal coding.
