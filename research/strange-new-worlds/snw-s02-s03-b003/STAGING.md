## Neutral close-read staging notes

### S02E01 — The Broken Circle

- Pike leaves Enterprise to pursue legal help for Una, and Spock is placed in temporary command. Spock explicitly worries that his recently less-suppressed emotions may impair judgment; M'Benga frames those emotions as something to manage rather than evidence that Spock has become a different person.
- Uhura receives a coded distress message attributed to La'An from Cajitar IV. Admiral April refuses intervention while the planet is under Klingon treaty access because an Enterprise appearance could reignite war. Spock nevertheless chooses to take Enterprise without authorization after allowing the crew an opportunity to decline or report him.
- Pelia identifies the simulated emergency used to clear inspectors from the ship and chooses to help rather than report it. Her statements about her Lanthanite history and prior relationship with Amanda Grayson are testimony about off-screen history.
- La'An reports that former Klingon and Federation soldiers have organized around profit from renewed conflict and are acquiring Federation technology. Her account is subsequently strengthened by direct evidence of a stolen/constructed Starfleet-configuration vessel and the false-flag plan.
- M'Benga and Chapel encounter Klingon veterans and survivors while providing medical aid. Their statements and reactions establish war memory and trauma-related context; the episode does not turn every recalled J'Gal detail into neutral omniscient history.
- M'Benga and Chapel use Protocol 12, an enhancement drug associated with prior wartime use, while escaping confinement. The episode directly depicts increased combat performance but also character testimony about adverse effects and discontinuation.
- Uhura detects that the false Federation vessel's transponder is carrying a coded warning. Spock infers that M'Benga and Chapel altered it and that the vessel is intended to create a false-flag Federation attack. The chain should preserve sensor evidence, code interpretation, and Spock's inference as separate steps.
- Spock delays firing until the last possible moment partly because Chapel and M'Benga may still be aboard. Their escape beacon is detected after the false-flag ship is destroyed, and they are recovered alive. The later recovery disconfirms the immediate possibility that the ship's destruction killed them.
- Spock negotiates directly with the Klingon captain after the confrontation and accepts responsibility for his unauthorized action. April later treats the successful prevention of wider conflict as material to discipline but explicitly notes that the same choice could have produced renewed war.
- The episode therefore supports neither `orders are irrelevant if the outcome is good` nor `unauthorized action necessarily causes escalation`; the command decision remains an institutional/ethical conflict with contingent consequences.

### S02E02 — Ad Astra Per Aspera

- Una is offered a plea bargain requiring admission of nondisclosure and dishonorable dismissal without prison. She rejects the deal, causing the prosecution to add serious charges and expose her to a much harsher sentence.
- Neera Ketoul is an Illyrian civil-rights attorney with prior personal history with Una. Her hostility toward Federation genetic law and her strategic objectives are explicit; her legal arguments should not be flattened into narrator-certified moral facts simply because the episode gives them substantial force.
- The prosecution establishes that Una knowingly concealed her genetically modified status and that Starfleet regulations prohibit permanent genetic modification and bar genetically modified people from service. Admiral April states that, had he known her status, he would not have sponsored her Academy application.
- Neera uses April's history of discretionary violations of General Order One to challenge the idea that Starfleet regulation is applied mechanically. The court strikes part of this line from the record. The existence of prior exceptions supports institutional inconsistency/discretion, not automatic invalidation of every regulation.
- La'An initially fears that her personal log may have exposed Una. Neera determines that the timing and access rules make that unlikely. This explicitly disconfirms La'An's self-blaming hypothesis.
- Una testifies to childhood persecution in a Federation member colony after anti-augmentation law and social stigma separated Illyrian and non-Illyrian communities. Her childhood account is testimony about lived history; the episode presents specific harms, concealment, arrest, segregation, and medical danger through that testimony rather than an objective historical montage.
- Una states that she herself reported her Illyrian status because she was tired of concealment and hoped Starfleet might accept her openly. This reverses the working assumption that an external betrayer necessarily exposed her.
- Neera constructs the successful defense through Federation asylum law: Una describes persecution, Starfleet as the institution in which she sought safety, her disclosure as a request for protection, and Pike's knowledge as the captain's granting of asylum.
- The tribunal explicitly preserves the general genetic-engineering prohibition while finding that modified persons cannot all be treated identically and granting Una asylum under her specific circumstances. Una is found not guilty of all charges.
- The outcome is therefore a case-specific legal resolution, not repeal of the Federation genetic-engineering ban. Neera herself characterizes the result as a limited beginning rather than complete justice for Illyrians.

### S02E03 — Tomorrow and Tomorrow and Tomorrow

- A wounded future operative gives La'An a temporal device and tells her to prevent an attack in the past. The device moves her first into an alternate 23rd-century context and then into 21st-century Toronto with that timeline's James Kirk.
- In the alternate 23rd century, Earth is described as devastated and the United Earth Fleet is at war with Romulus; Starfleet and the Federation known to La'An do not exist. Kirk's knowledge and testimony belong to that alternate timeline and must not be projected into the restored timeline as shared history.
- La'An remembers her original timeline because of the temporal device. Kirk remembers his own alternate history. Their memories therefore have different causal provenance even while they cooperate in the same 21st-century setting.
- A bridge bombing initially appears to be the attack they were sent to stop, but both recognize it as an event already present in their remembered histories. Photonic damage and suspicious removal of wreckage move the investigation toward deliberate temporal interference.
- Sera initially presents herself as a contemporary human investigator of alien interference. Her useful information about unusual technology is mixed with speculative and conspiratorial material. She is later revealed as a Romulan temporal operative, retroactively changing the credibility and motive attached to her earlier presentation.
- Pelia is encountered in the 21st century and is identified by La'An as a long-lived Lanthanite. Pelia helps with a practical detection problem but explicitly lacks the engineering expertise La'An expected from her later career. This is direct counterevidence to treating later professional identity as timeless.
- Kirk tells La'An that, if only one timeline can survive, he would prefer hers. Their developing relationship is therefore embedded in a situation where his own remembered world may be erased by mission success.
- Sera reveals that her actual target is Khan Noonien Singh as a child. She reports that Romulan simulations predict killing Khan will prevent the Federation from forming, while also describing repeated temporal efforts that have shifted the timing of events. Those statements establish Romulan modeling and Sera's theory, not an omniscient rule that time literally possesses agency.
- Kirk is killed during the confrontation with Sera. La'An then refuses the opportunity to kill young Khan despite acknowledging his later history and her own inherited stigma. The episode depicts this as a causal choice required to preserve the target timeline, not moral exoneration of his later acts.
- After the timeline is restored, an agent from a future Department of Temporal Investigations confirms the mission framework and orders La'An not to discuss the experience even with Pike or others of her time.
- The restored-timeline James Kirk does not remember the relationship or the alternate timeline. La'An retains those memories and later contacts him for an ordinary security pretext. Psychological/memory continuity is therefore asymmetric even though the person she contacts resembles the man she knew in the erased branch.

### S02E08 — Under the Cloak of War

- Ambassador Dak'Rah is presented publicly as a former Klingon general who defected, killed three Klingon officers, and became a Federation peace advocate. Multiple Enterprise veterans react negatively because of J'Gal and Athos, while others emphasize his diplomatic accomplishments and possible reform.
- Ortegas and other veterans repeat stories about Rah before the episode establishes what each actually knows first-hand. Those statements should remain reputation/testimony evidence rather than a single objective biography.
- M'Benga and Chapel experience involuntary wartime memories while Rah's visit reactivates J'Gal trauma. The flashback material is framed through remembered experience and should preserve that epistemic frame.
- Wartime scenes depict severe triage constraints, repeated mass casualties, civilians and children among the wounded, transporter-buffer suspension as an emergency survival technique, and disagreement over military use of Protocol 12.
- M'Benga initially refuses a special-forces request to join an assassination mission and to manufacture Protocol 12. Later, after the Athos massacre intensifies and evacuation approaches, he takes the remaining drug and leaves to complete the mission.
- Rah tells others that J'Gal changed him, that he killed his own officers in opposition to atrocities, and that this act led to his later reputation as the Butcher of J'Gal. These are Rah's self-presentations and official story, not independently established facts.
- M'Benga tests Rah's account by asking which officer fought hardest. Rah gives an answer inconsistent with M'Benga's memory. M'Benga then reveals that he, not Rah, killed the three Klingon officers and allowed Rah to take credit because he was ashamed of the killing.
- Rah admits that he used the story because Starfleet trust and his peace work depend on it, while also arguing that his later work has constructive value. This creates a conflict between truthful historical attribution, accountability, and consequences of exposing a politically useful false narrative.
- A physical struggle follows after M'Benga repeatedly tells Rah to leave him alone. Rah dies by the knife associated with the J'Gal killings. Chapel reports witnessing Rah initiate the confrontation after M'Benga rejected him, and available logs/DNA corroborate a compatible timeline, but the episode does not provide an omniscient external view resolving every movement or intention in the fatal moment.
- M'Benga tells Pike he did not start the fight, then states that even if he had, he would be glad Rah was dead. Pike emphasizes due process and the Federation's commitment to second chances; M'Benga emphasizes victims, justice, and limits of forgiveness. The episode leaves the ethical dispute unresolved rather than endorsing one frame as neutral fact.

### S03E01 — Hegemony, Part II

- This episode directly continues the unresolved S02E10 situation. Enterprise remains constrained by the Gorn demarcation line while Pike refuses to abandon the captured landing party and colonists.
- Scott's Gorn-signature device is an improvised technical success whose exact construction he cannot fully reproduce from memory. Pelia's insistence on engineering logs makes the difference between `it worked once` and reproducible technical knowledge explicit.
- Enterprise crosses the Gorn line using an EM-signature deception. Characters infer that Gorn sensing depends heavily on biometric, thermal, and electromagnetic information; this is a working technical model derived from observed behavior, not total knowledge of Gorn perception.
- The captured landing party observes Gorn infrastructure, transporter/buffer systems, and the processing of captives. Their escape plan generates transport codes that later allow Enterprise to recover hundreds of colonists.
- Batel's Gorn implantation begins accelerating. Initial simulations repeatedly predict fatality. Chapel and Spock then propose an Illyrian plasma transfusion from Una to support Batel's physiology during surgery. The method violates regulation but produces a viable survival path.
- This treatment activates a biological continuity dimension for Batel but does not imply any broader identity transfer from Una. The evidence supports transfused biological function and treatment outcome, not personhood mixing.
- Uhura and Sam Kirk continue testing the prior episode's hypothesis that stellar activity correlates with Gorn behavioral cycles. When a Gorn vessel intentionally uses the radiation zone and enters free fall, the crew develops a stronger model that specific radiation signatures may trigger rest/hibernation behavior.
- Uhura explicitly calls the proposed Gorn response a theory before Pike acts on it. Enterprise then deliberately generates an artificial radiation/EM signature analogous to a stellar flare. The Gorn ships disengage and enter what the crew interprets as a long hibernation period.
- The successful response strengthens the stellar-cycle hypothesis substantially but does not establish a timeless universal law of all Gorn behavior. Pike immediately notes the possibility that their solution merely postpones the problem for someone else.
- Ortegas is critically injured during the escape but survives and is reported stable. Batel survives the surgery and remains in recovery. These later depictions disconfirm immediate death possibilities present during the crisis.
- The episode resolves the immediate captivity and attack but not the larger Federation-Gorn relationship, the long-term consequences of induced hibernation, or the durability of the biological/behavioral model used to achieve it.

## Cross-episode frame and counterevidence traps

- **S02E01:** coded sensor/transponder evidence, Spock's false-flag inference, unauthorized command action, and successful outcome are separate evidentiary layers; success does not retroactively make the order violation risk-free.
- **S02E02:** Una's acquittal arises from a specific asylum-law construction while the tribunal explicitly leaves the genetic-engineering prohibition intact.
- **S02E03:** La'An retains memories of an erased/alternate branch that the restored-timeline Kirk does not share; later contact must not collapse those relationship histories into one symmetric continuity record.
- **S02E08:** Rah's public biography, his own testimony, official records, M'Benga's memory, Chapel's witnessed report, and physical/DNA evidence overlap but are not interchangeable. The fatal confrontation remains partly epistemically bounded.
- **S03E01:** a behavioral hypothesis introduced around solar activity gains experimental/operational support, but successful intervention should not be promoted into an unrestricted species law.
