## Neutral close-read staging notes

### S03E02 — Wedding Bell Blues

- Federation Day festivities provide the ordinary frame. Batel is reported recovering after the prior Gorn crisis and the implanted material has been described as reabsorbed.
- Chapel returns with Roger Korby. Spock's expectations about Chapel are disrupted before the episode's altered-reality event begins.
- An advanced energy being presents through multiple roles and demonstrates the ability to rewrite memories, relationships, social circumstances, and immediate reality. Claims about its age, status, or omnipotence remain its own presentation unless directly supported by depicted capability.
- The being creates a reality in which Spock and Chapel are preparing to marry and much of the crew accepts that history. Korby initially retains memory of the prior state; Spock later recognizes contradictions and joins the investigation.
- The altered wedding is therefore **altered-reality evidence**, not stable relationship history. Characters' conduct inside that frame should not be silently projected into ordinary continuity.
- The being coerces continuation of the wedding and threatens destructive consequences. Chapel ultimately recognizes that the situation is wrong during the ceremony.
- A more authoritative parental figure intervenes and terminates the manipulation. Pike subsequently describes the prior condition as a shared delusion; Chapel treats it as a trickster's interference with emotions. Those are post-event interpretations, while the demonstrated reality alteration remains direct depiction.
- Spock uses the wedding context to articulate his feelings and then releases Chapel rather than accepting the forced marriage as legitimate.
- Post-restoration interactions, including Spock and La'An's developing closeness, occur in the ordinary frame and must remain distinct from the compelled wedding state.

### S03E03 — Shuttle to Kenfori

- Batel's Gorn-derived tissue becomes medically aggressive again. A Chimera Blossom on treaty-restricted Kenfori is sought as part of an experimental treatment.
- Pike and M'Benga knowingly undertake an off-books treaty violation to retrieve it. The Enterprise initially remains outside the restricted zone before later intervention during the rescue crisis.
- Kenfori's former agricultural research used a genetic agent to introduce hardy plant traits into crops. War disruption exposed humans and Klingons to the agent; the resulting beings retain predatory physical behavior while failing ordinary life-sign scans. The evidence supports the depicted biological condition and technical history presented in the episode, not a fantasy-category label such as `zombie` as ontology.
- Batel explains that the proposed treatment would permanently integrate Gorn biology rather than simply remove it. This activates a biological-continuity dimension, but the episode supplies no basis for treating the therapy as identity replacement.
- Spock uses a mind meld to reduce Batel's pain and reports contact with something experienced as both part of her and not part of her. The frame remains subjective/mind-meld evidence; it does not settle whether an independent Gorn consciousness exists inside her.
- Klingon Bytha identifies herself as Dak'Rah's daughter. She states that she welcomed her father's death but seeks to restore family honor after his disgrace by killing his assassin.
- M'Benga explicitly says that he killed Rah willingly and would do it again. He characterizes his earlier account as a protective lie intended to contain a violent part of himself until it might be needed. This later admission materially changes the evidentiary balance around Rah's death without erasing the earlier source record.
- M'Benga rejects ritual execution and spares Bytha. She later chooses a sacrificial fight against the infected rather than accepting the social consequences of survival.
- Pike tells M'Benga that officially there was no Kenfori mission and supplies a cover-story frame for any later account. The official narrative is therefore knowingly distinct from the depicted events.
- Ortegas intentionally increases speed against Una's order so Klingons will detect Enterprise and force adoption of a risky rescue plan. She later acknowledges doing so. Una removes her from duty temporarily and orders chain-of-command retraining; the episode links the conduct to post-Gorn strain without authorizing a broader diagnosis.

### S03E04 — A Space Adventure Hour

- Enterprise tests a prototype Re-Creation Room/holodeck while operating near a neutron star. Holographic characters are generated using detailed crew biosignatures and transporter-pattern information.
- Simulated versions of Enterprise personnel are **local simulated entities**. Their actions and apparent deaths remain simulation evidence and must not be merged with the biological crew members they resemble.
- La'An instructs the computer to create a difficult Amelia Moon mystery. The environment responds adaptively to her behavior and expectations.
- A holographic Spock becomes La'An's investigative partner. The later reveal establishes that the program generated this version using information about La'An's familiarity with the real Spock precisely because she would be less likely to suspect him. This strongly supports a `SIMULATION_OF` relation rather than identity equivalence.
- The program blocks communications and consumes enough real power to endanger Enterprise during the neutron-star event. This demonstrates that simulated events can produce material consequences without making the simulated murders or identities physically real.
- Scotty initially resists seeking help because of guilt associated with the Stardiver disaster; Una reframes seeking assistance as compatible with competent command and engineering practice. Her framing remains dialogue/interpretation linked to his conduct.
- La'An recommends against installing the system on active starships because the prototype's adaptive behavior and resource demands created unacceptable risk. Scotty proposes dedicated computing and power isolation as possible mitigation rather than proof the hazard is solved.
- After leaving the simulation, La'An distinguishes the simulated Spock from the actual Spock through their different behavior and her embodied familiarity with him. Her subsequent interaction with the real Spock is ordinary-frame relationship evidence, separate from the program-generated encounter.
- Production-show material embedded inside the mystery remains story-within-simulation content rather than historical evidence about the fictional universe.

### S03E05 — Through the Lens of Time

- Chapel and Korby investigate an archaeological site on Vadia Nine associated with M'Kroon traditions about an ancient civilization and immortality. Those cultural accounts and initial translations remain archaeological hypotheses/testimony until stronger evidence appears.
- Beto Ortegas uses an AI-assisted camera that interprets movement, inflection, and social cues. Its outputs are a sensor/algorithmic interpretation layer, not direct access to mental states.
- The site exhibits dimensional and quantum behavior in which apparently separated rooms occupy layered aspects of the same space. Characters also describe inverted causal effects. Ordinary spatial assumptions therefore cannot simply be applied to every observation.
- Ensign Dana Gamble is injured by an ancient containment object. His eyes are destroyed and his short-term memory is affected; later medical scans indicate a physiologically nonfunctional or `dead` brain despite continued conscious interaction. This is counterevidence to using one ordinary biological criterion as sufficient identity or consciousness resolution.
- An entity later speaks and acts through Gamble, accesses private information he should not possess, manipulates memory/voice phenomena, and attempts to obtain command access. Gamble repeatedly expresses a desire to remain himself. Host and possessing entity should remain distinct local occurrences with continuity unresolved where the evidence is ambiguous.
- Archaeological translation characterizes the beings as parasites, hitchhikers, unwelcome guests, or Vezda. The expedition hypothesizes that ancient dimensional technology opened access and that the site functioned as a prison. These are source-relative interpretations rather than omniscient cosmology.
- Batel experiences a violent impulse toward Gamble/the entity that characters connect hypothetically to her Gorn-derived biology. The causal link remains proposed rather than established.
- M'Benga, Pelia, and Scotty ultimately use a transporter-buffer solution as a proxy containment dimension. Gamble is reported lost/dead afterward. The exact mechanics of host continuity should remain unresolved rather than compressed into a simplistic possession model.
- Characters explicitly disagree about whether the Vezda are `evil`: Pike treats good/evil as context-dependent while Korby argues for genuine malevolence. The ontology/ethical classification is therefore contested in the source itself.

### S03E06 — The Sehlat Who Ate Its Tail

- Farragut operates under James Kirk as acting captain after Captain V'Rel is injured. A vast scavenger vessel attacks a planet, Farragut, and Enterprise.
- Multiple cultures supply folklore names such as Destroyer of Worlds, Astrovore, or Annihilator. These are reputation/cultural-story evidence, not reliable species or origin classifications.
- The vessel consumes or incorporates ships and resources and is attracted to aldentium. Enterprise becomes physically trapped while power and systems are compromised.
- Kirk challenges and then inherits command decisions under pressure. Some of his early choices, including pushing damaged systems despite engineering warnings, produce further failure. The episode depicts command learning through consequences rather than a uniformly competent or incompetent state.
- Kirk eventually solicits more crew input and develops a plan that exploits the scavenger's drive to consume, using deceptive resource cues and coordinated engineering/communications workarounds.
- After the engagement, scans reveal thousands of **human** life signs within the wreckage. Historical records and Pelia's recollection connect the vessel to a mid-21st-century pre-warp Earth generation ship launched amid environmental and wartime crisis and later lost.
- The discovery directly disconfirms the crew's earlier working assumption that the adversary was simply an alien monster. The present inhabitants are descendants of the human passengers after roughly two centuries of isolated development.
- Many survivors cannot be transported from debris and their life signs decline. Kirk explicitly confronts that he did not think of the adversaries as people until the late reveal.
- Pike's comments about empathy and command responsibility are interpretive/ethical guidance arising from this event. They should not be promoted into universal institutional doctrine without independent support.
- V'Rel is later expected to recover, providing counterevidence to the possibility that the earlier injury was fatal.

## Cross-episode frame and counterevidence traps

- **S03E02:** a coerced reality rewrites memories and relationship history; altered-reality behavior is not ordinary continuity evidence.
- **S03E03:** M'Benga's later explicit admission changes the evidentiary status of Rah's death, while Pike knowingly constructs an incompatible official account.
- **S03E04:** a high-resolution holographic Spock built from transporter-pattern data is still a simulation-local entity, not the biological Spock.
- **S03E05:** possession, dimensional instability, anomalous biological readings, and contested `evil` language resist a single simplified ontology.
- **S03E06:** folklore and tactical assumptions identify an apparent alien monster until direct evidence reveals human descendants, making it a strong misclassification regression case.
