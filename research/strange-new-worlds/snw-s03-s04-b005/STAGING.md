## Neutral close-read staging notes

### S03E07 — What Is Starfleet?

- The episode is framed substantially through Beto Ortegas' documentary and declassified/security material. His accusation that the Federation can resemble an empire is the filmmaker's thesis and interview framing, not narrator-certified world-state fact.
- Interviewed crew members' claims about Starfleet, personal history, institutional ideals, fear, duty, and trauma remain testimony. Spock's account of childhood self-harm in response to anti-human stigma is self-report about prior experience.
- Enterprise is ordered to transport cargo and a Jikaru into an active war zone on Lutani VII. The weaponization purpose is initially classified, so crew compliance occurs under acknowledged informational limits.
- A scientist attacks/sabotages the project and warns against weaponization. Subsequent direct evidence shows that the Jikaru's brain has been artificially altered to intensify violence and that its restraint device functions through painful neural suppression.
- The Jikaru is therefore simultaneously handled by institutions as cargo/military asset and depicted as an intelligent coerced being. Those classifications should not be collapsed into one ontological label.
- Spock's telepathic contact overwhelms and injures him. Uhura later volunteers for a second neural link and establishes communication. The source route is telepathic/mediated, not ordinary verbal testimony.
- Uhura interprets the Jikaru as asking for death to escape continued weaponization. The creature's subsequent behavior and the mission outcome operationally support that interpretation without making every detail of Uhura's subjective experience omniscient fact.
- Pike obtains Command agreement, leads the Jikaru into the star, ends the Lutani metamorphosis program, and establishes Tychus-B as a protected location for unaltered offspring. Lutani objections emphasize that ending the program may cost lives in their ongoing war; the ethical/institutional conflict is not erased by the rescue.
- Pike also threatens Federation force if the Lutani resist. That action complicates Beto's imperial/warship critique instead of simply disproving it.
- Beto's documentary motive is partly bound up with fear and anger over Erica's Starfleet service and injury; Uhura and Erica identify that bias. His framing is therefore interested rather than neutral, while some questions he raises remain supported by depicted institutional choices.
- The closing formulation that Starfleet is its people is Uhura's interpretive testimony, not a literal ontology of the institution.

### S03E08 — Four-and-a-Half Vulcans

- Tezaar is pre-warp but has historical Vulcan contact predating the Federation, including earlier Vulcan technology transfer. Enterprise must repair a planet-threatening system without revealing present-day Starfleet involvement.
- Pike, Uhura, La'An, and Chapel use a modified Kerkhovian serum to become biologically Vulcan for the mission; Pelia is unaffected because of Lanthanite physiology and Spock participates in his existing mixed state.
- The transformation alters physiology, sensory processing, cognition, affect, and social judgment. The first reversion attempt fails.
- The transformed crew concludes that remaining Vulcan permanently is logical. Spock explicitly notes that this conclusion conflicts with their pre-transformation expressed preferences. M'Benga and Spock discuss continuing human katra/core identity despite altered bodies and cognition.
- Biological state, current preference, prior preference, memory, katra/self-concept, and legal/social identity therefore remain separate continuity dimensions rather than becoming a binary same-person/different-person judgment.
- The crew rejects simply forcing a reversion treatment on people still capable of expressing preferences. They state that if the transformed individuals still choose Vulcan life after access to their less-filtered prior selves, that choice should be respected.
- Altered-state conduct materially affects relationships and command: Uhura mind-melds Beto to suppress impulses; Chapel treats relationships as inefficient and breaks ties; Pike tries ship-wide operational changes and interferes with Batel's career decision; La'An becomes unusually aggressive/manipulative and contemplates wider conflict.
- Spock and Doug use katra contact to reconnect the transformed crew with earlier emotional and autobiographical perspectives. Pike, Uhura, and Chapel then choose reversion.
- La'An initially resists. Within the katra experience Spock attributes aspects of her aggression/paranoia to Augment/Khan ancestry, but that is an interpretation inside a mind-space interaction rather than a settled genomic law.
- All four ultimately revert and later address consequences of their transformed conduct. Batel's separate confrontation with Pasalk results in an offer to lead Starfleet's Judicial Department, preserving her agency apart from Pike's altered-state interference.

### S03E09 — Terrarium

- Ortegas volunteers for a solo manual shuttle survey in an anomalous gravity region after prior Gorn trauma. She has passed a psychological evaluation and M'Benga cleared the mission, while La'An and Una had separately questioned whether her return to duty was too soon. The source supports concern and recovery context, not an invented diagnosis.
- A wormhole appears and pulls the shuttle through. Enterprise loses contact. Uhura believes a faint mayday originates beyond the anomaly while Spock and Una note signal-refraction ambiguity. Later operational evidence strengthens Uhura's interpretation.
- Rescue planning creates an explicit command tradeoff between thousands of colonists awaiting vaccines and one missing officer. Multiple simulations fail; Uhura manipulates probability figures and Pike says he knew, yet chooses the search anyway.
- Ortegas is stranded on a toxic moon and meets an injured Gorn. Initial enemy/prey assumptions are progressively disconfirmed: the Gorn kills food for her, shares shelter, accepts treatment, cooperates, and communicates through a simple translator.
- The Gorn is identified locally as a female pilot. She understands human speech better than Ortegas initially realizes, plays chess, and believes rescue by her own people may result in death because she is injured/weak.
- Ortegas and the Gorn develop a genuine survival relationship. Their behavior supplies episode-local counterevidence to species-level assumptions that all Gorn-human encounters are necessarily reducible to predation.
- Enterprise enters the wormhole using a static warp field. Ortegas and the Gorn generate an atmospheric signal and are located.
- The Starfleet landing party immediately shoots and kills the Gorn despite Ortegas' protest. This is a direct tragic consequence of the rescuers' prior hostile classification.
- A Metron then states that the situation was engineered as an experiment forcing human and Gorn together, characterizes both species as barbaric, and says the Gorn kept Ortegas alive from loneliness/friendship. Those are Metron claims, although some align with the directly depicted cooperative behavior.
- The Metrons say Ortegas will forget them while retaining the genuine experience and that her perception may later reset. Memory manipulation is therefore explicit. Ortegas retains the relationship memory strongly enough to tell Uhura that the Gorn was her friend while La'An is also her friend.
- The episode supports the local friendship and the later memory state; it does not authorize a global conclusion about all Gorn or all humans.

### S03E10 — New Life and New Civilizations

- Transporter residual information indicates that the Vezda reconstructed a Gamble-pattern body from emergency backup data and escaped confinement. The rebuilt body/local occurrence is controlled or constituted through Vezda action; it should not be silently merged with the original Gamble as a simple resurrection.
- The Vezda/Gamble occurrence travels by inter-dimensional routes to Skygowan, where Korby is researching Vezda traditions and immortality. Skygowan is non-warp but participates in external trade, creating a complicated contact context.
- The Vezda gains worshippers and threatens to free imprisoned Vezda. Starfleet orders observation/noninterference while Enterprise and Farragut prepare for possible intervention because of the projected wider threat.
- Batel experiences a pull toward the Vezda. Chapel and Spock hypothesize that her Gorn-derived biology, Una/Illyrian transfusion, and Chimera treatment may combine adaptive traits into a protector state. This remains a medical/identity hypothesis rather than established universal biology.
- Batel identifies her biosignature with the ancient Beholder/warden and says she has always been the sentry because of reversed causality/inter-dimensional time. Biosignature evidence supports a relation, while exact ontological identity across eons remains unresolved/structurally paradoxical rather than a silent SAME_AS merge.
- Spock and Kirk use a mind meld to synchronize Enterprise and Farragut actions, explicitly sharing consciousness and later retaining at least some shared-memory consequence. Mind-meld content should remain source-framed unless independently acted upon.
- During Batel's confrontation with the Vezda, Pike experiences an apparent decades-long life with Batel, including marriage, family, and a future in which his predicted accident does not occur. The sequence returns to the confrontation and is associated with Batel/Beholder action; it is altered/vision/time-experience evidence, not automatically a literal future timeline.
- Batel declares both Beholder and Marie Batel, defeats/reimprisons the Vezda, and survives as a continuing local identity. The exact causal relation between Batel and the ancient warden remains richer than simple sameness.
- Skygowan recovers and Korby remains to study Vezda history. New maps provide further unexplored destinations. Pike's closing claims about memory/time are interpretive narration rather than omniscient metaphysics.

### S04E01 — Valles Marineris

- Pike has spent months keeping Enterprise in near-continuous mission activity and acknowledges using work to avoid dwelling on recent events. Una warns about crew strain. April offers Pike a commodore promotion and Pike delays deciding.
- An unusual stellar storm pulls Enterprise through a temporal displacement into the ancient Sol system. The ship is disabled and loses iridium; an away team lands on an M-class world later established as ancient Earth.
- The away team encounters dinosaurs. Once the temporal location is understood, the crew explicitly avoids killing native animals where possible because future consequences are unknown. This is a direct temporal-noninterference constraint, not merely ordinary wildlife policy.
- A salvage/military vessel under Commander Cassell seizes Enterprise. Before location/time are established, the crew considers whether the unknown people might be pre-warp. Later scans reveal Cassell's people are Martians in ancient Sol.
- Cassell states that Mars is at war with the Dol'drm, whom she describes as insect-like, unreasoning world-devourers. Her characterization is wartime testimony and should not be promoted into an objective species ontology.
- Cassell sees Enterprise's multispecies cooperation, medicine, and weapons. Pike argues that communication and Federation-scale resources might provide alternatives to force. Cassell counters with her society's history of loss and wartime necessity.
- Cassell identifies Valles Marineris as the scar of a Dol'drm attack that killed roughly two million people and argues that force was the only effective response. The visible geography and her testimony are separate evidentiary components; the transcript does not provide an omniscient replay of the event.
- The ancient-Earth away team finds a crashed alien colony ship containing iridium and cryogenic remains. Damage suggests the ship was shot down. The occupants are treated as would-be settlers whose exact prior intentions/history are not fully known.
- Cassell learns that Enterprise is from millions of years in her future and steals its primary antimatter fuel cell to use against the Dol'drm. Pike's regulatory concealment and Cassell's extraction of the truth are distinct from her later decision to appropriate future technology.
- The Dol'drm homeworld is identified as an additional populated planet in ancient Sol. Cassell's side uses the stolen antimatter technology to destroy it despite Pike's objections.
- Enterprise has an opportunity to fire to stop the attack, but Pike orders no fire because in his known future no such planet exists. He later acknowledges acting on a hunch rather than proof available at the moment.
- The resulting destruction creates the energy event used to return Enterprise to its own time. Spock's later simulations infer that fragments become the Chicxulub impactor/asteroid belt and that the planetary destruction shifts the later evolution of Mars and Earth, contributing causally to the dinosaur extinction and eventual human history. These are in-universe scientific model results, not external astronomy asserted by this worker.
- Characters confront a causal-loop problem: their intervention appears already embedded in the history that produced them, while they still question whether alternatives were ever possible. Preserve the ambiguity rather than resolving free will/determinism by fiat.
- Later genetic analysis indicates Martian ancestry persisted among descendants despite ancient Mars becoming lifeless. This supports some survival/descent but does not establish Cassell's individual survival.
- Pike declines the promotion and recommends Una; Una is advanced toward captaincy. The time-travel details are withheld from the ordinary official report.
- La'An reports enhanced strength/reflexes and proposes that experiences may have activated recessive Augment DNA. Chapel responds that enhanced warrior genetics are not equivalent to identity. The activation mechanism remains La'An's hypothesis, not a settled genetic fact.

## Cross-episode frame and counterevidence traps

- **S03E07:** documentary framing, classified institutional purpose, telepathic communication, and the Jikaru's status as coerced intelligent weapon must remain distinct evidence layers.
- **S03E08:** temporary full-Vulcan biology changes cognition and preference without erasing memory/katra/social continuity; transformed preferences require separate treatment from prior and post-restoration preferences.
- **S03E09:** direct cooperative behavior disconfirms the rescuers' Gorn classification before a Metron adds a second interpretive layer and manipulates memory.
- **S03E10:** a rebuilt Gamble-pattern body, Vezda possession/reconstruction, Batel-Beholder causal identity, mind-meld sharing, and Pike's experienced lifetime all resist simple global identity merges.
- **S04E01:** Cassell's wartime species claims and Spock's later causal simulations are different epistemic layers; the episode creates a causal-loop structure without proving that alternative action was impossible.
