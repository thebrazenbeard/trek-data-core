### Evidence

- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev01` — **log/record, ordinary frame, lines 13–63.** The Enterprise evaluates Rubicun III for shore leave after an away-team report presents an apparently peaceful society; that initial report does not identify a severe punishment regime. [plot/problem; institutions; epistemic pattern]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev02` — **sensor/report, ordinary frame, lines 129–178.** A partially detectable orbital presence blocks communications and instructs the Enterprise not to interfere with life forms it calls its children. [technology; testimony; continuity]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev03` — **dialogue/testimony, ordinary frame, lines 229–251.** Edo representatives explain that a randomly selected punishment zone is policed by mediators and that any violation inside it receives one punishment: death. [institutions; culture; ethics]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev04` — **depiction/action, ordinary frame, lines 253–320.** While playing ball, Wesley crosses a marked plant boundary without understanding the rule, admits what he did, and is condemned to death; the away team stops the immediate execution. [agency; institutions; ethics]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev05` — **dialogue/testimony, ordinary frame, lines 299–318.** The away team states that its pre-visit legal review listed nothing about punishment; the Edo maintain that witnesses and Wesley's admission establish guilt. [epistemic pattern; counterevidence; institutions]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev06` — **dialogue/testimony, ordinary frame, lines 349–378.** The Edo defend their law as the basis of social tranquility and delay enforcement until sundown because the visitors are strangers. Picard contrasts this with the Federation's abandonment of capital punishment. [ethics; institutions; culture]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev07` — **dialogue/testimony via Data, ordinary frame, lines 454–483.** After communication through Data, the orbital presence is described as multiple rather than a single entity, aware of Edo worship, and existing under dimensional conditions Data finds difficult to describe. [epistemic pattern; identity; continuity]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev08` — **dialogue/testimony, ordinary frame, lines 473–504.** Data reports that the presence knows the Prime Directive through him and might judge the crew by its own stated rules; Picard explicitly frames non-interference against his obligation to protect his people. [institutions; ethics; command]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev09` — **depiction/action, ordinary frame, lines 519–580.** Picard decides he will not permit Wesley's execution, attempts to beam the party up, is temporarily blocked, and argues that absolute rules cannot cover every just outcome. [command; ethics; institutions]
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:ev10` — **depiction/action, ordinary frame, lines 581–597.** The orbital presence departs and the Enterprise leaves with Wesley. The source does not contain an explicit declaration from the presence endorsing Picard's legal theory. [counterevidence; unresolved consequence]

### Staging assertions

- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:as01` **PROVISIONAL** — Wesley's jeopardy arises from a real clash between local absolute-law enforcement and Federation crew-protection/non-interference commitments, aggravated by inadequate notice of the punishment regime. Supports: ev03–ev06, ev08.
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:as02` **PROVISIONAL** — The Edo's identification of the orbital presence as a god is a cultural interpretation. Data's communication supports a powerful multi-presence or vessel-like phenomenon, not an objective theological conclusion. Supports: ev02, ev07.
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:as03` **PROVISIONAL** — Picard knowingly privileges protection of a crew member over strict non-interference once the conflict becomes irreducible. Supports: ev08, ev09.
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:as04` **UNRESOLVED** — The presence's departure permits the rescue to stand but does not unambiguously establish agreement with Picard's theory of justice. Supports: ev09, ev10.
- `tng-s01-b002-staging:e08:as05` **PROVISIONAL / counterevidence** — The source directly undermines any claim that Wesley knowingly accepted a capital-risk rule: the away team had not been briefed on punishment and Wesley crossed the boundary during play. Supports: ev04, ev05.
