## 11 — The Big Goodbye

**Source:** `https://www.transcripts.foreverdreaming.org/viewtopic.php?t=97604`  
**Cross-check:** `https://www.chakoteya.net/NextGen/113.htm`  
**Local entities:** Picard, Data, Crusher, Whalen, Wesley, Riker, La Forge, Yar, McNary, Cyrus Redblock, Felix Leech, Jessica Bradley, Jarada, Enterprise, holodeck three, Dixon Hill program.

### Evidence

- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev01` — **log/record, ordinary frame, lines 39–123.** The Enterprise is preparing a protocol-sensitive diplomatic contact with the Jarada while Picard begins a Dixon Hill holodeck program. Picard's log describes holodeck environments as computer-created images used for training and recreation. [institutions; command; simulation]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev02` — **log/record, simulation frame, lines 177–187.** Picard explicitly calls the Dixon Hill characters computer-generated while emphasizing that they seem and feel real to him. The record establishes generation and phenomenology, not a metaphysical personhood ruling. [simulation; epistemic pattern; identity]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev03` — **depiction/action, simulation frame, lines 389–510.** Picard re-enters the program with Data and Whalen; Crusher later joins. Program characters treat them as participants in the 1941 setting and do not recognize the holodeck frame. [simulation; interpersonal; epistemic pattern]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev04` — **log/technical report, ordinary + simulation frames, lines 514–625.** A Jaradan communication increases diplomatic urgency while a holodeck malfunction prevents external communication, program access, and door opening. [institutions; technology; command]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev05` — **depiction/action, simulation frame, lines 646–873.** The trapped crew cannot summon an exit; a program weapon injures Whalen and Crusher diagnoses massive internal bleeding. A simulated-frame event therefore has physical consequences for a non-simulated crew member. [simulation; technology; consequence]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev06` — **dialogue/testimony, simulation frame, lines 898–1061.** Data tells Redblock, Leech, and McNary that they are imaginary characters derived from fiction. They reject or cannot assimilate that account. The evidence establishes the utterance and their responses, not independent proof of subjective status. [identity; epistemic pattern; testimony]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev07` — **technical warning, ordinary frame, lines 1138–1158.** Wesley warns that an attempted repair could abort the program and cause everyone inside to vanish; Riker orders the repair because delay does not reduce the stated risk. [technology; command; uncertainty]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev08` — **depiction/action, simulation boundary, lines 1176–1239.** Once the exit reappears, Redblock and Leech attempt to enter the Enterprise corridor but progressively disappear outside the holodeck, while Data and Whalen can pass through. [simulation; boundary; counterevidence]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev09` — **dialogue/testimony, simulation frame, lines 1244–1265.** McNary asks whether his world, wife, and children will continue after Picard leaves; Picard answers that he does not know. Persistence of the simulated world is left explicitly unresolved by the exchange. [simulation; continuity; unresolved]
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:ev10` — **depiction/action, ordinary frame, lines 1267–1304.** Picard returns to the bridge and completes the Jaradan greeting successfully, resolving the immediate diplomatic task after the holodeck emergency. [command; institutions; consequence]

### Staging assertions

- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:as01` **PROVISIONAL** — The episode distinguishes computer generation from experienced realism: the source identifies Dixon Hill characters as generated while depicting them as behaviorally responsive within the simulation. Supports: ev02, ev03, ev06.
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:as02` **PROVISIONAL** — Holodeck-frame events can produce physical effects on real crew during the malfunction; this does not establish that all represented matter or characters persist beyond the simulation boundary. Supports: ev05, ev08.
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:as03` **UNRESOLVED** — The source does not adjudicate whether McNary's experienced world continues after program exit. The uncertainty is explicit and should not be replaced with a global ontology claim. Supports: ev09.
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:as04` **PROVISIONAL / counterevidence** — Redblock and Leech failing to persist outside the holodeck is evidence against treating their instantiated simulation state as ordinary physical continuity across that boundary; it is not proof of non-personhood. Supports: ev08.
- `tng-s01-b003-staging:e12:as05` **PROVISIONAL** — The holodeck crisis competes with but does not erase Picard's institutional obligation to execute the Jaradan protocol accurately. Supports: ev01, ev04, ev10.
