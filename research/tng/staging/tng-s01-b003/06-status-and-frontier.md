## Cross-work neutral observations

These are staging hypotheses for later testing, not accepted corpus conclusions:

1. **Simulation and synthetic embodiment remain distinct mechanisms.** `The Big Goodbye` concerns computer-generated holodeck characters constrained by a simulation boundary; `Datalore` depicts two concurrently embodied Soong androids. Similar questions about personhood or identity do not make the mechanisms interchangeable.
2. **Identity recognition uses different evidence by case.** Lore's impersonation is challenged through behavioral clues; Jameson's transformed appearance is reconciled through memory, relationship, scar, and medical continuity; Minuet is explicitly linked to an adaptive program. No single identity predicate safely replaces these different relations.
3. **Institutional authority repeatedly operates under incomplete or manipulated information.** Data evacuates Enterprise because a false emergency appears genuine; Riker and Data interpret time-sensitive orders in `Angel One`; Jameson's formal mission authority survives disagreement about judgment.
4. **Testimony requires containment even when later corroborated.** Lore's origin account is undermined; the Bynars' data-transfer account has system corroboration but their motive for deception remains their own explanation; Jameson's medical-process account is only partly corroborated by Crusher.
5. **Counterevidence is source-salient rather than decorative.** `The Big Goodbye` leaves simulation persistence explicitly unknown; `Datalore` shows two separate bodies rather than one duplicated continuity; `Too Short A Season` distributes responsibility for Mordan's war rather than supporting Jameson's claim of sole causation.
6. **Provider coverage itself is provenance evidence.** Springfield's season-one transcript index omits `Too Short A Season`, while Chakoteya and Star Trek Sickbay supply the work/transcript. The omission is a source inventory anomaly, not a reason to alter episode order silently.
